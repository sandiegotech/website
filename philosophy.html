The Philosophy · A Founding Document · MMXXVI

The standard against which everything is measured.

Every course, project, and decision of the Institute is checked against this document, the way law is checked against a constitution. It exists so that what we build cannot drift from why we build.

It is written to be used and argued with. Where the work and the philosophy disagree, the work changes.

Part One · Tenets I–IV

The Foundations — what we hold. True before the Institute existed; true if it ever fails. Amended last, if ever.

Tenet IThe Good

Judged by outcomes, not intentions.

Everything the Institute makes exists for the betterment of humanity and the world. That is the first commitment, and every other tenet serves it. We hold it with open eyes: good intentions, sincerely held, have built catastrophes, and the worst harms of every century arrived in the language of virtue. So the standard is what a thing does, not what its maker meant. And it points inward first. The most dangerous assumption available to us is that we are the good people, and we keep that assumption under permanent review.

The Test What does this actually do to the people who use it and the world that receives it? Would we still call it good if someone we distrust had built it?
Tenet IITruth

Grounded in what is true, not what is comfortable.

The Institute holds that truth exists, that it can be approached, and that it is worth pursuing even when it is unwelcome. Holding a belief sincerely, loudly, or in large numbers does not make it true. There are bad ideas; some, carried out, damage the world. The answer to a bad idea is not condemnation but understanding: take it apart, find its strongest form, and learn exactly where it fails. A bad idea dismissed without being understood is not defeated, only postponed.

The Test Is this true — and how would we know if it were not? Have we understood the strongest version of what we are rejecting?
Tenet IIIFreedom

Freedom of thought — ordered, not absolute.

Freedom of thought, inquiry, and creative expression is the lifeblood of the place; nothing real has been built without it. What we defend is the freedom to think, to question, to make, and to be wrong in good faith. The line is not drawn at discomfort, which is often the first evidence of a real idea, nor at disagreement, which is how the Institute works. It is drawn at harm and at hatred.

The Test Does this defend a person's freedom to think and to make — or someone's desire to harm without answering for it?
Tenet IVThe Person

A life is measured by its quality and its purpose — not its title.

An education that cannot say what a human life is for forms people by accident. We hold that the measure of a life is not a title, a salary, or a rung on someone else's ladder. It is work worth doing, done well, among people worth doing it with, in a life with room left for community, for family, and for the mind. A career is a means to such a life, never the reverse. The faculties most worth forming are the human ones: judgment, taste, curiosity, conviction, character. No machine supplies them and no credential certifies them. We are not training employees. We are forming people.

The Test Does this form judgment, taste, curiosity, conviction, or character — and does it serve the life, or only the résumé?
Part Two · Tenets V–VI

The World — the conditions we answer. The age as we find it, read without flinching.

Tenet VThe Machine

The machine is the instrument. The person is the point.

Artificial intelligence is not a hypothesis to debate but a condition to be met. The question is not whether it will be used, but by whom, for what, and with what understanding. The Institute teaches technology as instrument rather than substitute: a tool that extends judgment rather than replacing it. And it keeps capability in good hands by giving it away, because the more capability rests with people who intend good, the safer the age becomes. The first tenet applies to us without exemption: we remain obliged to verify that we are the good hands we intend to be.

The Test Does this give the person more command over the machine, or the machine more command over the person — and whose hands does it strengthen?
Tenet VICapital

Where capital goes, the future goes.

Markets have produced an enormous share of the modern world's gains, and we teach that history plainly, including its costs. Because capital is powerful, its direction matters: moving it toward things that better the world, and away from things that degrade it, is among the most consequential acts a person or an institution can perform. The Institute is a nonprofit so that greed can never become its operating principle. It will not take a grant, gift, or sponsorship whose terms require it to act against this document.

The Test Does this move capital toward the betterment of the world — and would we still take the money if its terms were published?
Part Three · Tenets VII–XI

The Institution — what we are building. The foundations and the world, answered with a place.

Tenet VIIThe Commons & the Gate

Knowledge open to all. The character, kept.

Two commitments that look like a contradiction and are not. First: every person should have access to the knowledge and the tools to better themselves, as a public service, like roads or water. What the Institute produces is made to be within everyone's reach, and those who draw from it are asked, as they are able, to add to it. Nothing is gated behind that request. Second: the Institute itself is deliberately kept. It is a place with a way of life and a standard of thinking, and its gate selects for character rather than wealth, pedigree, or credentials: openness of mind, seriousness of purpose, the willingness to be wrong.

The Test Is the knowledge open to anyone — and does the gate admit character rather than wealth?
Tenet VIIIThe Place

New traditions, rooted in timeless things.

The Institute is an American institution, rooted in California: the coast, the water, and a way of life its founders live rather than advertise. We learn through the things we love. A wave is fluid dynamics and a lesson in nerve; jiu-jitsu is geometry under pressure and an education in humility. Pursued with rigor, the things a person genuinely loves become the most demanding teachers of judgment, attention, and nerve. The particular pursuits are ours; yours will differ. What is not optional is having one, and taking it seriously enough that it can teach you something you did not go looking for. We are building new traditions, in a physical place in San Diego, under development now.

The Test Does this belong to the way of life — would it teach judgment, nerve, or workmanship to a person we would want here?
Tenet IXThe Negations

What the Institute is not.

A constitution that only affirms is a wish list. These are the refusals, stated so they can never be renegotiated quietly.

Not a pulpit

Not a platform for activism, of any politics. Ideas are weighed on their merits, not their affiliations.

Not a refuge for hatred

No seat for hateful messages, and none for the closed of mind. The price of entry is the willingness to change your mind when shown to be wrong.

Not a free-for-all

Openness of mind is not openness of conduct. The way of life is kept, and freedom is ordered, not absolute.

Not for sale

No grant, gift, or partnership whose terms require misalignment with this document, at any size.

Not for everyone

It is for people who want to make things for the betterment of the world. Narrowness of aim is how the aim is hit.

The Test If this thing succeeded completely, would the Institute still be recognizable as itself?
Tenet XThe Artifact

Knowledge is completed in the making.

An education has two motions: taking the world in, and giving something back. The second completes the first; a thing you can recite but cannot make, you do not yet know. To explore without making is to collect; to make without exploring is to repeat. The Institute refuses both. We read in order to build, and build in order to understand what we have read, and every paper, project, and lesson the Institute produces is held to the same standard.

The Test Of any course, project, or fellow's year: what was made? If the honest answer is "material was covered" and nothing was built, explored, or given back, the work has not yet happened.
Tenet XIThe Horizon

Built to outlast its founders.

The Institute is not built for the moment. Institutions that matter are built by people who accept that the best of what they are building will arrive after they have left. What we assemble now, the framework, the character, the people, the work, is meant to be handed on better than it was received. The ambition is not size but permanence. This document will be revised, openly and under signature, as the world changes. What stays constant is the aim: truth, human flourishing, and the long-term success of civilization.

The Test Does this serve the institution as it must become, not only as it is now? Are we building for the people already in the room — or for those who haven't arrived yet?
ClosingHow it is held

Written to be used.

Every course, project, and decision is checked against these tenets before it ships, by someone empowered to say no. Where the work and the philosophy disagree, the work changes. The document is amended the way it was written: openly, in writing, with reasons, and under a signature. How that works, and the record of adoption and signatures, are kept on the governance page.

If you believe a tenet here is wrong, write and say so. A constitution that exempted itself from argument would fail its own second tenet.
