Lectio · The First Project

# Let any person read a great work. *With genuine ease.*

Lectio is a reader for the great books. Each one gets a new edition in plain current English, faithful to the original, with the historical translations and the original language one tap away.

It is the first project of the San Diego Institute of Technology. 17 editions are in beta. The app is not out yet. This page says where everything stands.

## The greatest books ever written are almost entirely unread.

People are capable of reading them. What stops them is the way in.

The great works are free and in the public domain: *The Iliad*, *Meditations*, *The Divine Comedy*, the *Tao Te Ching*. Almost nobody reads them.

The free copy of each is a translation a century or more old. Its English puts even a native speaker at a distance, and its names and references assume a classical schooling few people have now. Most reading apps hand over the same 1909 file and leave you to it.

Lectio gives each book a clear modern edition and keeps the old ones beside it. It is the whole book, made for the reader it has now. Nothing is cut and nothing is summarized.

## What readable means.

The opening of *Meditations*, Book II. A Roman emperor is telling himself how to face the morning.

**George Long · 1862**

> Begin the morning by saying to thyself, I shall meet with the busybody, the ungrateful, arrogant, deceitful, envious, unsocial. All these things happen to them by reason of their ignorance of what is good and evil.

**Lectio Edition · 2026**

> Begin each morning by telling yourself: today I will meet people who are meddling, ungrateful, arrogant, dishonest, envious, and unsociable. They are like this because they cannot tell good from evil.

The first is the edition most people meet. It is accurate, and it was written for readers who died a century ago. The second is the same two sentences as they stand in the Lectio Edition today. In the app, Long, Casaubon's English of 1634 and the Greek stay beside it.

## How an edition is made.

A Lectio edition is a new rendering of the whole work. It is drafted by an AI model and it says so.

The public-domain translations and, where we have it, the original are brought into one bundle. A model drafts the edition section by section. A second reader, also a model, then reads every section against those sources and reports what is wrong. A further pass tries to prove each finding false, and only the fixes that survive are applied. At that point the edition is a beta. It becomes a release when its readers have reviewed it and the editor has read the whole of it himself. Neither has happened yet for any book.

Each edition is stamped with the year it was drafted and the date it was last revised, and it carries its sources and the places where it departs from the older English.

## The app is built and not yet released.

Lectio is being prepared for the App Store. The Institute's Apple developer enrollment is in review, and nothing can be submitted until it clears. When the app is out it will be free.

It runs on iPhone and iPad. It opens to the books you have been reading. Every book lists its editions, and switching between them keeps your place. You can set a passage beside the same passage in another edition, and keep bookmarks, highlights, notes and saved words.

There is no link here because there is nothing to install yet. The Daily will say when there is.

## Printed editions are planned. One proof exists.

The printed book is set from the same bundle the app reads, so the page and the screen carry the same text.

The only printed artifact today is a proof of *Meditations*. Only a release is printed, and the editor reads the proof on paper before it is sold. The plan after that is one printed edition a month, shortest works first.

The text will be free to read, in the app and as a PDF. The bound book is what is sold, and what it earns goes to the Institute. One PDF exists today, of *Meditations*: [PDF, beta, revised November 2026](https://sandiegotech.org/lectio/meditations/meditations-lectio-edition-2026-11.pdf).

Editions in other languages are planned, Spanish first. None exists yet beyond a trial setting of *Meditations*.

## Every edition is beta until its readers make it a release.

A Lectio edition is labeled beta from the day it is made. It earns its release stamp through the review of its readers, students and scholars alike, together with the editor's own full read.

This is review by care, not by credential. A scholar here is anyone who will read closely. You hold the Lectio edition against the older translations, or against the original if you have the language. You flag where a passage loses the sense or reads badly, and you say so plainly.

Every flagged passage anchors to an exact segment of the text. An accepted correction lands in the next revision with its reason recorded. A released edition names its reviewers in the colophon. Only releases go to print.

There is a precedent. The Oxford English Dictionary began with an appeal to the public, and volunteer readers spent decades mailing quotation slips to its editors. Lectio works the same way, with better instruments.

The machine review of November 2026 was a first pass. It does not replace this one. No edition has had its readers' review yet.
