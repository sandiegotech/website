# The Bhagavad Gita, Chapter VII

Knowing the Supreme

Krishna said: Now hear, Arjuna, how with your mind fixed wholly on me, taking refuge in me and practicing this discipline, you will come to know me through and through, beyond all doubt. I will teach you this knowledge, and the deeper insight that goes with it, in full; once you have it, there is nothing more left to know in this world.

Of many thousands of people, scarcely one strives for perfection; and of those who strive and reach it, scarcely one truly knows me as I am.

Earth, water, fire, air, and space; mind, intellect, and the sense of "I" — this is my nature, divided eightfold. But this is only my lower nature, Arjuna. Know that I have another, a higher nature: the very principle of life, by which this whole world is held up and sustained. All things that live are born of these two natures of mine. I am the origin of the whole universe, and its dissolution as well. There is nothing higher than I am; everything that exists is strung upon me like rows of pearls upon a single thread.

I am the taste in water, Arjuna; I am the light in the moon and the sun; I am the sacred syllable OM that sounds in all the Vedas, the sound in space, the manhood in men. I am the pure scent of the earth and the brightness in fire; I am the life in all that lives, and the austerity of those who practice austerity. Know me, Arjuna, as the eternal seed of every living thing. I am the intelligence of the intelligent, the splendor of the splendid. I am the strength of the strong, free of desire and craving; and in all creatures I am desire itself, when that desire does not run against what is right.

Know, too, that the three qualities of nature — goodness, passion, and darkness — all arise from me alone. They are in me, but I am not in them. Deluded by these three qualities, the whole world fails to recognize me, who stand beyond them, changeless. For this divine illusion of mine, this maya woven of the three qualities, is hard to see past; only those who take refuge in me alone pass beyond it.

Those who do evil do not turn to me — the deluded, the lowest of men, whose discernment has been stripped away by this same illusion, who have given themselves over to a demonic way of being.

Four kinds of good people turn to me in worship, Arjuna: the one in distress, the one who seeks knowledge, the one who seeks some good of his own, and the one who already knows. Of these, the one who knows — the wise one, ever steadfast, devoted to the one alone — stands highest; for I am dear above all things to the wise, and he is dear to me. All four are noble, but the wise one I hold to be my very self; for, steadied in this discipline, he has set his whole course on me, the highest goal there is. After many births the wise one comes at last to me, knowing that Vasudeva is all that is. Such a great soul is very hard to find.

There are others whose discernment is carried off by this desire or that, and who turn instead to lesser gods, each according to his own nature, binding themselves with their various rites. But whatever form a devotee longs to worship in faith, it is I who steady that faith in him and make it firm. Held steady in that faith, he worships that god and gains from it the things he desires — though it is I, in truth, who grant them. Yet the reward such people win is limited and passing. Those who worship the gods go to the gods; but those who are devoted to me come to me.

Men of little understanding take me, the unmanifest, to be merely manifest — some particular form — not knowing my higher nature, which is changeless and beyond all compare. I am not revealed to everyone, veiled as I am by the illusion of my own power; this bewildered world does not recognize me, the unborn and the unchanging. I know all beings, Arjuna — those that are past, those that are present, and those that are yet to come; but no one knows me.

By the pull of the pairs of opposites, by the delusion that springs from craving and aversion, all creatures fall into confusion at their very birth. But those who do what is good, whose wrongdoing has come to an end, are freed from the delusion of these opposites; firm in their resolve, they worship me. Those who take refuge in me and strive for release from old age and death come to know Brahman entire — the whole self, and the whole of action. Those who know me as the ground of all that lives, the ground of all the gods, and the ground of all sacrifice — who know me with their minds held steady — they know me even in the hour of their death.
