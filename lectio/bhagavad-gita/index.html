The Bhagavad Gita

  • 200 BCE – 200 CE
  • Eastern tradition
  • Religion
  • Lectio Edition 2026

On the eve of a battle against his own family, the warrior Arjuna lays down his bow. His charioteer, the god Krishna, answers him. The answer is eighteen chapters on duty, devotion, and how to act without clinging to the result. It sits inside the epic Mahabharata.

Why this editionGoal and purpose

The teaching is direct. Victorian verse is not.

The public-domain Gita is Edwin Arnold's The Song Celestial of 1885, in blank verse with lyric stanzas. It is graceful and it is ornate. "I am Time" becomes "Time who kills, Time who brings all to doom, the Slayer Time, Ancient of Days".

Arnold also changes the names of things from line to line, so a reader cannot tell that the Gita has a fixed set of terms and uses them exactly.

The Lectio Edition is prose. It says what Krishna says in the order he says it, and holds each Sanskrit term to one English form. Arnold stays in the app beside it.

The openingFrom the Lectio Edition

How it begins.

Chapter I · The Distress of Arjuna

Dhritarashtra said: On the sacred plain of Kurukshetra my people and the sons of Pandu have gathered, hungry for battle. Tell me, Sanjaya: what did they do?

Sanjaya said: When he saw the army of the Pandavas arrayed for war, King Duryodhana went to his teacher Drona and said: "Master, look at this great host of Pandu's sons, marshalled by the son of Drupada, your own pupil in the art of war. Here stand heroes, great archers, the equals of Bhima and Arjuna in battle — Yuyudhana, Virata, and Drupada the mighty charioteer; Dhrishtaketu, Chekitana, and the valiant king of Kashi; Purujit, Kuntibhoja, and Saivya, that bull among men; bold Yudhamanyu and brave Uttamaujas; the son of Subhadra, and the sons of Draupadi — every one of them a great chariot-warrior.

"Know, too, best of brahmins, the finest of our own side — the captains of my army. Let me name them, so you may recognize them: yourself first of all, then Bhishma and Karna, and Kripa ever victorious in the fight; Ashvatthaman, Vikarna, and the son of Somadatta as well — and many another hero besides, each armed with his chosen weapon, all skilled in war, every one of them ready this day to lay down his life for me. Yet our forces, guarded by Bhishma, seem to me too few, while theirs, guarded by Bhima, seem strong enough. So let all of you, standing each in his place along the line, give support to Bhishma above all."

The choicesPolicies and divergences

What this edition decided.

The review wrote a term sheet for the whole work and brought every chapter into line with it.

  • Yoga is "discipline" in running text. Where the text names or defines the teaching it is "Yoga", with a capital.
  • The three gunas are "the three qualities". Their names are kept: sattva, rajas, tamas. Their English is goodness, passion, darkness. Tamas is never "ignorance". Ignorance is what it comes from.
  • Where the Sanskrit has brahman, the English says Brahman. It is not turned into "God" or "the eternal". Brahma the creator god is a different word and stays Brahma.
  • Dharma, Sankhya and the Vedas keep their names, with no gloss in the sentence.
  • Akasha is "space", not "air" or "ether".

Where it differs from the older translations

The edition records 60 divergences: places where it departs from the older English, each with its reason. Here are 7. The full record is in the app, one tap from the text.

karmany evadhikaras te

2.47

Older
"Let right deeds be thy motive, not the fruit which comes from them" (Arnold)
Lectio
So your concern is with the action itself, never with its fruits.

The best-known verse in the Gita. The Sanskrit has four clauses and the fourth matters: do not cling to inaction either. Arnold lets it dissolve. The edition keeps all four.

vasamsi jirnani

2.22

Older
"Nay, but as when one layeth / His worn-out robes away" (Arnold)
Lectio
As a person casts off worn-out clothes and puts on others that are new

Death as a change of clothes. Arnold sets it as a sing-song stanza. In plain prose the comparison keeps its weight.

shreyan svadharmo vigunah

3.35

Older
"this is better, that one do / His own task as he may, even though he fail" (Arnold)
Lectio
It is better to do your own duty imperfectly than to do another's duty well

The Sanskrit goes on to say that another's duty is dangerous. Arnold softens that. The edition keeps the edge.

yada yada hi dharmasya

4.7 and 4.8

Older
"When Righteousness / Declines, O Bharata! when Wickedness / Is strong, I rise, from age to age" (Arnold)
Lectio
For whenever dharma declines and the wrong prevails, Arjuna, I bring myself into being.

The teaching of divine descent. Dharma is kept as the term. "Righteousness" and "Virtue" are two English words for one Sanskrit one.

kalo 'smi lokakshayakrit

11.32

Older
"Thou seest Me as Time who kills, Time who brings all to doom" (Arnold)
Lectio
I am Time, grown mighty, the destroyer of worlds

The Sanskrit is one blow: I am Time. Arnold makes it three soft ones.

sattvam rajas tama iti

14.5 to 14.8

Older
"'Soothfastness,' 'Passion,' and 'Ignorance'" (Arnold)
Lectio
goodness, passion, and darkness

"Soothfastness" was never living English. And tamas is darkness and inertia. The draft had followed Arnold's "ignorance", and the review corrected it.

sarvadharman parityajya

18.66

Older
"And let go those— / Rites and writ duties! Fly to Me alone!" (Arnold)
Lectio
Abandon all duties and come to me alone for refuge.

The verse the devotional tradition holds to contain the whole. The Sanskrit says all duties. Arnold narrows it to ritual ones.
