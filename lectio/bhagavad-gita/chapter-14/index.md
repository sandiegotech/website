# The Bhagavad Gita, Chapter XIV

The Three Qualities of Nature

Krishna said: I will open to you once more the highest of all knowledge, the wisdom beyond every other wisdom. All the sages who have come to know it have passed from here to perfection. Resting on this knowledge, they have risen into kinship with my own being; they are not born again when a new world begins, nor troubled when the worlds dissolve.

The whole of nature is the womb in which I plant my seed, and from that planting comes the birth of all that lives. Whatever forms take shape in any womb, Arjuna, that great nature is the mother of them all, and I am the father who gives the seed.

Now sattva, rajas, and tamas — goodness, passion, and darkness — are the three qualities that arise out of nature, and these three bind the changeless soul to the body that it wears. Of these, sattva, being clear and untroubled, gives light, and it binds by attachment to happiness and to knowledge. Rajas is of the nature of desire; it is born of craving and clinging, and it binds the embodied soul by attachment to action and its fruits. But tamas, born of ignorance, deludes all who live in a body; it binds by heedlessness, by sloth, and by sleep. So sattva binds a person to happiness, Arjuna, and rajas binds him to action, while tamas, blotting out all knowledge, binds him to neglect and stupor.

These three are forever rising over one another. Sometimes sattva prevails, overpowering rajas and tamas; sometimes rajas prevails over sattva and tamas; and sometimes tamas prevails over the other two. When the light of knowledge shines out from every gate of the body, then you may know that sattva is in the ascendant. When greed appears, and restless striving, and the launching of one undertaking after another, and longing that knows no peace, these spring up when rajas is in the ascendant. And when dullness comes, and inertia, and heedlessness and bare confusion, these arise, son of Kuru, when tamas is in the ascendant.

If a person dies while sattva prevails, he goes to the pure worlds of those who know the highest. If he dies while rajas prevails, he is born again among people bound to action; and one who dies while tamas prevails is born again in the wombs of the senseless. The fruit of good action, they say, is sattvic and unclouded; the fruit of passion is pain; the fruit of darkness is ignorance. From sattva knowledge is born; from rajas, greed; and from tamas come heedlessness and delusion and, at the last, mere ignorance. Those who hold to sattva rise upward; those given over to rajas keep to the middle; and those sunk in tamas, the lowest of the qualities, sink downward.

When the one who truly sees perceives that there is no doer in any of this but the qualities of nature, and knows that which lies beyond the qualities, then he comes into my own being. And the soul that rises past these three qualities, out of which the body itself arises, is set free from birth and death, from old age and sorrow, and drinks the nectar of the deathless.

Arjuna said: By what marks, my Lord, is a person known who has gone beyond these three qualities? How does he live? And how does he cross beyond the three?

Krishna said: He who does not hate the light of goodness, nor the stir of activity, nor even the cloud of delusion when they come, and does not long for them when they are gone; who sits like one indifferent, unshaken by the qualities, knowing that it is only the qualities at their play, and so stands apart and is not moved; who is the same in pleasure and in pain, who rests within himself, to whom a lump of clay, a stone, and a piece of gold are all one; who meets the pleasant and the unpleasant alike, and is steady; who takes blame and praise as the same, the same in honor and in disgrace, the same toward friend and toward foe, and who has laid down every undertaking — such a person, Arjuna, is said to have crossed beyond the qualities.

And whoever serves me with unswerving devotion, never turning aside, passes beyond these qualities and becomes fit to be one with Brahman. For I am the ground in which Brahman rests — the deathless and the imperishable, the eternal order, and joy that has no end.
