The Bhagavad Gita, Chapter XV

The Supreme Person

Chapter XV15 of 18

Krishna said: They speak of an undying ashvattha tree, the cosmic fig, whose roots reach upward and whose branches grow downward, and whose leaves are the hymns of the Vedas. Whoever knows this tree knows the Vedas. Its branches spread out below and above, nourished by the three qualities of nature; its buds are the objects of sense. And downward from it, into the world of human beings, run other roots, binding them to their actions.

Its true form is not to be seen here as it is — not its end, not its beginning, not where it stands. This ashvattha, with its roots grown so deep, must be cut down by the strong axe of non-attachment. Then a person should seek out that place from which, once reached, there is no returning, saying: I take refuge in that first Person, from whom the whole ancient unfolding of things streamed forth.

Free of pride and delusion, the evil of attachment overcome, ever dwelling in the self, their desires laid to rest, released from the play of opposites — from pleasure and pain — they go undeceived to that imperishable place. Neither the sun lights it, nor the moon, nor fire; once they have gone there, they do not return. That is my supreme abode.

A fragment of my own self, become the eternal living being in the world of life, draws to itself the senses, and the mind that is the sixth, as they rest in nature. When the Lord takes up a body, and when he leaves it again, he gathers these up and carries them off, as the wind carries scents away from where they linger. Presiding over hearing, sight, and touch, over taste and smell, and over the mind as well, he takes in the things of sense.

The deluded do not see him as he stays in the body, or goes out from it, or takes in the world of sense, joined to the three qualities of nature; but those who have the eye of wisdom see. The disciplined, who strive, see him present within themselves; but the unschooled, whose selves are unripe, do not see him, however hard they strive.

Know that the brightness which dwells in the sun and lights the whole world, the brightness in the moon and in fire, is my brightness. Entering the earth, I hold up all living things by my power; and becoming the moon, full of the sap of life, I nourish all the plants that grow. Becoming the fire of life within the bodies of all that breathes, joined to the breath that flows out and the breath that flows in, I digest the four kinds of food. And I am seated in the heart of everyone; from me come memory and knowledge, and the loss of them. I am the one to be known through all the Vedas; I am the author of their final wisdom, and the one who knows the Vedas.

There are two Persons in the world, the perishable and the imperishable. The perishable is all living beings; the imperishable is what stands unchanging above them. But there is another, a highest Person, called the Supreme Self — the imperishable Lord who enters the three worlds and sustains them. Because I surpass the perishable, and am higher even than the imperishable, I am known in the world and in the Vedas as the Supreme Person, the Purushottama.

Whoever knows me thus, undeluded, as the Supreme Person, knows all things, and worships me with his whole being, Arjuna. So now I have taught you this most secret teaching, blameless one. Whoever understands it has awakened to wisdom, and has done all that is to be done.
