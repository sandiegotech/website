The Bhagavad Gita, Chapter X

The Divine Glories

Chapter X10 of 18

Krishna said: Listen once more, strong-armed one, to my highest word, which I speak for your good, because you are dear to me. Neither the host of the gods nor the great sages know my origin; for I am the source from which all the gods and the sages spring. Whoever knows me as unborn and without beginning, the great Lord of the worlds, he among mortals is undeluded, and freed from every sin. Understanding, knowledge, clarity of mind, patience, truthfulness, self-control, and calm; pleasure and pain, coming into being and passing away, fear and fearlessness; harmlessness, an even mind, contentment, austerity, generosity, fame and disgrace — all these many states of living beings arise from me alone. The seven great sages of old, and the four ancient ones before them, and the Manus too, were born of my mind and share my nature; from them came all the races of the world. Whoever truly knows this glory and power of mine is joined to me by an unshakable discipline — of this there is no doubt.

I am the origin of everything, and from me all things flow; knowing this, the wise worship me with their whole hearts. Their thoughts fixed on me, their lives surrendered to me, enlightening one another and forever telling each other of me, they are content and they rejoice. To these, who are devoted to me with love and worship me always, I give the discipline of discernment by which they come to me. Out of compassion for them, dwelling within their hearts, I destroy the darkness born of ignorance with the shining lamp of knowledge.

Arjuna said: You are the supreme Brahman, the highest dwelling place, the great purifier. You are the eternal divine Person, the first of the gods, unborn and everywhere present. So all the sages have called you — Narada, Asita, Devala, Vyasa — and now you yourself declare it to me. I hold all of this to be true, Krishna, all that you have told me; for neither the gods nor the demons, Lord, can fathom how you show yourself. You alone know yourself by yourself, you supreme Person, source of all beings, lord of all that lives, god of gods, master of the world. You alone can tell in full the divine glories by which you fill these worlds and pervade them. How shall I know you, master of this discipline, though I dwell on you without ceasing? In what forms, in what aspects of your being, are you to be held in my thought? Tell me once again, in detail, of your power to spread yourself abroad, and of your glory, Krishna; for I can never have enough of hearing your words, which are like the nectar of life.

Krishna said: So be it, then; I will tell you of my divine glories — but only the chief of them, Arjuna, for there is no end to the full account of them. I am the self, Arjuna, seated in the heart of every living thing; I am the beginning, the middle, and the end of all that lives.

Among the Adityas, the lords of light, I am Vishnu; among the bright lights I am the radiant sun; among the storm-gods I am Marichi; among the stars I am the moon. Of the Vedas I am the Sama Veda; among the gods I am Indra; of the senses I am the mind; and in living beings I am the consciousness that knows. Among the Rudras I am Shankara; among the Yakshas and Rakshasas I am Kubera, lord of wealth; among the bright Vasus I am fire; and of the mountains I am Meru. Among the household priests, Arjuna, know me to be their chief, Brihaspati; among warriors I am Skanda the war-god; of bodies of water I am the ocean. Among the great sages I am Bhrigu; of all words I am the one sacred syllable, OM; of offerings I am the offering of silent prayer; and of things that do not move I am the Himalaya. Of all the trees I am the sacred fig; among the divine sages I am Narada; among the heavenly singers I am Chitraratha; among the perfected ones I am the sage Kapila. Among horses know me to be Uchchaihshravas, born of the nectar of immortality; among lordly elephants I am Airavata; and among men I am the king. Of weapons I am the thunderbolt; among cattle I am the wish-granting cow; I am the god of love that begets; and among serpents I am Vasuki. Among the great snakes I am Ananta, the endless one; of the creatures of the waters I am Varuna; among the ancestors I am Aryaman; and among those who keep order I am Yama, the judge of the dead. Among the demon hosts I am Prahlada; of all that measures, I am Time itself; among the beasts I am the lion, the king of beasts, and among the birds I am Garuda, who bears Vishnu. Of all that purifies I am the wind; among those who carry weapons I am Rama; among the creatures of the deep I am the crocodile; and among rivers I am the Ganges. Of all created things I am the beginning, the middle, and the end, Arjuna; of all knowledge I am the knowledge of the supreme self; and of those who debate, I am the very argument that decides. Of letters I am the first, the letter A; of word-pairs I am the linking of them. I am unending time; I am the maker who faces every way. I am death that carries off all things, and I am the birth of all that is yet to come; among things feminine I am fame, fortune, speech, memory, intelligence, steadfastness, and patience. Of the Sama chants I am the Brihat Saman; of meters I am the Gayatri; of the months I am the first, Margashirsha; and of the seasons I am the flowering spring. I am the gambling of the cheat, and I am the splendor of the splendid; I am victory, I am effort, and I am the goodness of the good. Of the line of Vrishni I am Krishna; of the sons of Pandu I am Arjuna, you yourself. Among the sages I am Vyasa; among the seers I am the poet Ushanas. I am the rod of those who rule, the statecraft of those who would conquer; I am the silence of hidden things, and the wisdom of the wise. And I am the seed of all that lives, Arjuna; there is nothing, moving or unmoving, that could exist apart from me.

There is no end to my divine glories, Arjuna; what I have told you of them is only a sketch of how far my power extends. Whatever is glorious, beautiful, or mighty — know that every such thing springs from a fragment of my splendor. But what need have you, Arjuna, to know all this in such detail? I stand holding up this whole universe with a single fragment of myself.
