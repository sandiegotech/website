# The Bhagavad Gita, Chapter XVI

The Divine and the Demonic

Krishna said: Fearlessness, a pure heart, steadfastness in the knowledge that comes of discipline; giving freely, holding the senses in check, sacrifice, the study of the sacred word, and austerity; uprightness, doing no harm to anything that lives, truthfulness, freedom from anger, the letting go of all one holds; a mind at peace, no taste for slandering others, tenderness toward all that suffers, and no greed; gentleness, modesty, a steadiness that does not waver; vigor, patience, fortitude, purity, freedom from spite and from any swollen pride — these, Arjuna, are the marks of a person born to the divine endowment.

Hypocrisy, arrogance, conceit, quickness to anger, harshness of speech, and ignorance — these are the marks of a person born to the demonic endowment.

The divine endowment leads to liberation; the demonic, to bondage. Do not grieve, Arjuna: you were born to the divine.

There are two kinds of beings brought forth in this world, the divine and the demonic. I have told you at length of the divine; now hear from me about the demonic.

The demonic do not know what should be done, nor what should be left undone. There is no purity in them, no right conduct, no truth. "The world has no truth in it," they say, "no ground to stand on, no God who rules it. Nothing brought it into being by the orderly working of cause on cause; it is born of nothing but desire — and what else holds it together?" Clinging to this creed, these lost souls, small in understanding and savage in their deeds, rise up as the enemies of the world, bent on its ruin.

Giving themselves over to a desire that can never be filled, full of hypocrisy, arrogance, and pride, they take up false aims in their delusion and go to work with hearts that are unclean. They are gripped, right up to the hour of death, by cares beyond all measuring; their highest aim is to glut their appetites; they are certain that this, and nothing more, is what life is for. Bound by a hundred cords of expectation, slaves to lust and anger, they strain to pile up hoards of wealth by any wrong they can, all to feed their cravings.

"This much I have won today," they think, "and that longing of mine I shall yet satisfy. This wealth is mine already, and that other wealth will be mine as well. This enemy I have slain, and the rest of them too I will slay. I am the lord of all I survey; I take my pleasure; I am the one who succeeds, strong, and happy. I am rich, I am nobly born — who else is there like me? I will offer sacrifice, I will give in charity, I will rejoice." So they speak, led astray by their ignorance.

Tossed this way and that by their many schemes, tangled in the net of their own delusion, addicted to slaking their desires, they sink down into a foul hell. Wrapped up in themselves, stubborn, drunk on the arrogance of their wealth, they make their offerings in name only, for show, with no regard for the rule that governs them. Surrendered to self-importance, to force, to insolence, to lust and to rage, these spiteful people come to hate me — me, who dwell within their own bodies and the bodies of all — and to hate everything I have made. These haters, cruel and vile, lowest among men, I cast down again and again, birth after birth, into demonic wombs; falling into demonic wombs, deluded life after life, they never reach me, Arjuna, but go down the road that leads ever lower.

There are three gates to this hell, three gates by which the self is destroyed: desire, anger, and greed. Therefore a person should turn away from all three. The one who escapes these three gates of darkness, Arjuna, does what is good for his own soul, and so reaches the highest goal. But the one who throws away the rule of scripture and acts only as his own desires drive him gains neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the highest goal. So let scripture be your measure for what should and should not be done; know what the scriptures lay down, and let your work in this world be done by that standard.
