# The Bhagavad Gita, Chapter XI

The Vision of the Universal Form

Arjuna said: Out of mercy to me you have spoken this deepest secret, this teaching of the inmost Self; and hearing it, my confusion is gone. From you, whose eyes are like lotus petals, I have learned in full how all beings come to be and how they pass away, and I have learned of your own greatness, which does not pass. You are all you have said you are, Lord of all. Now I long to see, with my own eyes, that form of yours in its full majesty, the supreme Self. If you judge that I am able to look upon it, then show me, lord of Yoga, your own undying being.

Krishna said: Then look, son of Pritha. Behold my forms, a hundredfold, a thousandfold — of every kind, divine, of many colors and many shapes. See in me the Adityas, the Vasus, the Rudras, the two Ashvins, and the Maruts; see, Arjuna, wonders beyond counting, never seen before. Here, gathered into this one body of mine, behold the whole moving and unmoving universe, and whatever else you long to see. But you cannot see me with these eyes of yours. So I give you a divine eye: now look upon the majesty of my power.

Sanjaya said: Having spoken so, O King, the great lord of Yoga, Krishna, showed to the son of Pritha his supreme and sovereign form. It was a form of countless mouths and countless eyes, of countless marvels to behold, hung with heavenly ornaments, lifting up uncounted heavenly weapons, wearing heavenly garlands and heavenly robes, anointed with heavenly perfumes — the God of all wonders, infinite, his faces turned in every direction. If the light of a thousand suns were to blaze out all at once in the sky, that would be something like the splendor of that mighty being. There, in the body of the God of gods, the son of Pandu saw the whole universe gathered together as one, divided into its endless multitudes.

Then Arjuna, struck through with wonder, the hair rising on his body, bowed his head before the God, pressed his palms together, and spoke:

Arjuna said: I see all the gods in your body, O God, and the hosts of every kind of being; I see Brahma the creator seated upon his lotus throne, and all the seers, and the shining serpents of heaven. I see you everywhere — your countless arms and bellies and mouths and eyes — endless, with no beginning, no middle, no end, lord of all, your form the whole world. I see you crowned, armed with the mace and the discus, a mass of blinding light on every side, hard to look upon — burning all around like the blaze of fire and sun, immeasurable. You are the imperishable, the supreme thing to be known; you are the last resting place of all this world. You are the changeless guardian of the eternal order; I know now that you are the everlasting Spirit.

I see you with no beginning, no middle, no end, your power without limit, your arms beyond counting — the sun and the moon are your eyes, your mouth is a blazing fire, and you scorch this whole universe with your own radiance. The space between heaven and earth, and all the quarters of the sky, are filled by you alone. Seeing this terrible and wonderful form of yours, the three worlds shudder, O great Self. The hosts of the gods stream into you; some, in fear, with hands pressed together, cry out your name. The great seers and the perfected ones hail you and sing your praise in a thousand hymns. The Rudras and Adityas, the Vasus and the Sadhyas, the Vishvas, the two Ashvins, the Maruts, the ancestors who taste the offerings; the hosts of the Gandharvas, the Yakshas, the Asuras, and the perfected ones — all gaze upon you in amazement.

Seeing your vast form, with its many mouths and eyes, its many arms and thighs and feet, its many bellies, terrible with many fangs — the worlds tremble, and so do I. For when I see you touching the sky, blazing with many colors, your mouths gaping wide, your great eyes burning, my inmost self shakes within me; I can find no steadiness, no peace, Vishnu. And when I see your mouths, terrible with their fangs, like the fires that consume the world at the end of time, I lose all sense of direction and can take no comfort. Have mercy, Lord of gods, refuge of the worlds!

And the sons of Dhritarashtra, all of them, together with the whole company of kings — Bhishma, Drona, and the son of the charioteer, Karna, along with the chief warriors of our own side too — I see them rushing headlong into your fearful mouths, set thick with fangs. Some I see caught between your teeth, their heads ground to powder. As the many rushing rivers of the world pour toward the sea, so these heroes of the world of men pour into your flaming mouths. As moths fly headlong into a burning flame to their own destruction, so these creatures fly headlong into your mouths to their destruction. You lick up all the worlds on every side with your flaming mouths, devouring them; your terrible blaze fills the whole universe with light and scorches it, Vishnu. Tell me who you are, you of so dreadful a form. I bow before you — have mercy, greatest of gods. I long to understand you, the first being; for I do not grasp what you are doing.

Krishna said: I am Time, grown mighty, the destroyer of worlds, and I have come forth to annihilate these people. Even without you, not one of these warriors drawn up in the opposing ranks shall remain alive. So stand up, win glory; conquer your enemies and enjoy a thriving kingdom. By me they are already slain, long since; be only the instrument, Arjuna. Drona and Bhishma, Jayadratha, Karna, and all the other warriors — they are killed already by me. Kill them, and do not falter. Fight! You will conquer your rivals in the battle.

Sanjaya said: When he had heard these words of Krishna, the warrior with the crown, trembling, his palms pressed together, bowed down; and stammering, terrified, bowing again and again, he spoke to Krishna in a broken voice:

Arjuna said: It is right, Krishna, that the world delights in your praise and is glad to do you honor; the demons flee in terror in every direction, and all the hosts of the perfected ones bow before you. And how should they not bow to you, O great Self, greater even than Brahma, the first maker? Lord of the gods without end, refuge of the worlds, you are the imperishable, what is and what is not, and what lies beyond them both. You are the first of the gods, the most ancient Spirit; you are the last resting place of all this world. You are the knower and what is to be known, the highest dwelling; by you this universe is spread out, you of endless forms. You are Vayu the wind, and Yama lord of death, and Agni the fire, and Varuna of the waters; you are the moon, and Prajapati the father of all, and the great-grandfather of the world. To you be honor a thousand times, and again, and yet again, honor to you! Honor to you from in front and from behind, honor to you from every side, for you are all things. Boundless in power, immeasurable in might, you encompass all; therefore you are all.

If, thinking you a friend, I ever spoke to you carelessly — calling you "Krishna," "comrade," "my friend" — not knowing your greatness, taking it lightly, out of fondness or in heedlessness; if I ever treated you without honor, in jest, at rest, at table, or sitting together, whether alone or before others, O changeless one — forgive me for it, immeasurable Lord. For you are the father of this whole world, of all that moves and all that does not; you are its teacher, the greatest of all, worthy of all worship; there is none equal to you, and in all the three worlds how could there be any greater, you whose power has no rival? Therefore I bow down, I stretch my body low before you, and I beg your grace, O Lord to be adored. As a father bears with a son, a friend with a friend, a lover with his beloved, so, O God, bear with me. I have seen what was never seen before, and my heart is glad; and yet fear shakes me. Show me, God, the form I know; have mercy, Lord of gods, refuge of the worlds. I long to see you again as you were — crowned, bearing the mace and the discus. Take on once more that four-armed shape, O thousand-armed one of countless forms.

Krishna said: By my grace toward you, Arjuna, and through the power that is mine, I have shown you this supreme form — radiant, universal, infinite, the first of forms, which no one but you has ever seen before. Not by the Vedas, not by sacrifices or study, not by gifts or rituals or harsh penances can I be seen in this form in the world of men by anyone but you, great hero of the Kurus. So do not be troubled, do not be bewildered, at the sight of this fearful form of mine. Let your fear be gone, let your heart be glad; look again on the form you know.

Sanjaya said: Having spoken so to Arjuna, Krishna again revealed his own gentle shape; the great Self, taking once more his kindly form, gave comfort to the terrified man.

Arjuna said: Now that I see again your gentle human form, Krishna, my mind is collected and I am myself once more.

Krishna said: This form of mine that you have seen is very hard to come by; even the gods are forever longing to look upon it. Not by the Vedas, not by penance, not by gifts, not by sacrifice can I be seen as you have seen me. But by devotion to me alone, and to no other, I can be known in this way, Arjuna, and truly seen, and entered into. The one who does all his work for me, who holds me as the highest, who is devoted to me, free of attachment, without hatred for any living thing — that person comes to me, Arjuna.
