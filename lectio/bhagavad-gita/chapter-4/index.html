The Bhagavad Gita, Chapter IV

The Way of Knowledge and Sacrifice

Chapter IV4 of 18

Krishna said: This undying Yoga I once taught to Vivasvat, the sun; Vivasvat handed it down to Manu, and Manu to Ikshvaku, and so it passed from king to king along a line of royal sages. But over the long stretch of years the teaching was worn away and finally lost. Now I declare that same ancient Yoga to you, this supreme secret, because you are devoted to me and you are my friend.

Arjuna said: But you were born in our own day, and Vivasvat lived ages ago. How am I to understand that it was you who taught it at the very beginning?

Krishna said: You and I have both passed through many births, Arjuna. I remember them all; you do not. Though I am unborn, deathless, and beyond all change, though I am the lord of every living thing, still I take on birth by my own power, holding sway over the nature that is mine. For whenever dharma declines and the wrong prevails, Arjuna, I bring myself into being. Age after age I am born, to protect the good, to destroy those who do evil, and to set dharma firmly in its place again. The one who truly knows this — the truth of my births and of my work — is not born again when he leaves the body behind; he comes to me. Many have already come to me this way: rid of longing, fear, and anger, made wholly mine, taking refuge in me, made pure by the fire of knowledge. However people approach me, I welcome them on that same path; for whatever road they take, Arjuna, it is my road in the end. Those who long for their works to bear fruit make their offerings to the gods, because in this human world the fruits of action come quickly. It was I who brought forth the four orders of society, dividing them according to their qualities and their work; yet though I am its maker, know me also as the one who does nothing and never changes. Actions do not stain me, for I have no craving for their fruit. Whoever understands me in this way is not bound by his own actions either. Knowing this, the seekers of old did their work and won their freedom. So you too should work, as your forebears worked long ago.

Even the wise are puzzled about what action is and what inaction is. So I will explain to you what action is, and once you know it you will be set free from all that is wrong. You must understand three things — what action is, what wrong action is, and what inaction is — for the way of action is hard to fathom. The one who can see inaction within action, and action within inaction, is the wise one among men; he is disciplined, even while he does every kind of work. The one whose every undertaking is free of the grasping of desire, whose works are burned clean in the fire of knowledge — the wise call that man wise. He gives up all claim on the fruit of his deeds; he is always content, leaning on nothing; and though he is fully at work, in truth he does nothing at all. Wanting nothing, his mind and his deeper self held in check, letting go of everything he might possess, he takes on no fault though his body acts. Content with whatever comes to him, beyond the pull of opposites, free of envy, the same whether he succeeds or fails — he is not bound, even as he acts. For one who is free, whose mind rests in knowledge, who does all his work as an offering, every trace of his action dissolves away.

When such a person makes an offering, the act of offering is Brahman, and what is offered is Brahman; Brahman pours it into the fire that is Brahman; and Brahman is what he reaches, since in all his work he keeps his mind fixed on Brahman. Some who follow this discipline offer sacrifice to the gods; others offer the self itself into the fire of Brahman. Some pour their hearing and their other senses into the fires of restraint; others pour sound and the rest of what the senses crave into the fires of the senses. Still others offer up all the workings of their senses and their breath into the fire of self-mastery, kindled by knowledge. Some make their offering with their wealth, some with hard discipline, some with the practice of Yoga; and others, men of strict vows, offer their study of the sacred word and the knowledge it brings. There are those, intent on the breath, who offer the outgoing breath into the incoming, and the incoming into the outgoing, holding the flow of both in check; and those who fast, cutting back on food, and so pour breath into breath. All of these understand what sacrifice is, and by their offerings their faults are washed away. Those who eat the sacred remnant that is left after the offering pass on to the eternal Brahman; but for the one who offers nothing, even this world is closed — how then could the next be his, best of men?

Offerings of every kind lie open before Brahman. Know that they are all born of action; once you understand this, you will be free. But the offering made of knowledge is worth more than any offering of mere things, Arjuna; for all action, without exception, finds its fulfillment in knowledge. Learn it by bowing low, by asking honestly, by giving service; the wise, who have seen the truth, will hand that knowledge on to you. Once you know it, you will never fall back into such confusion again; for by this knowledge you will see every living thing within yourself, and so within me. Even if you were the worst sinner of all sinners, the boat of knowledge would carry you safely across every wrong you have done. As a kindled fire turns its fuel to ashes, Arjuna, so the fire of knowledge turns all action to ashes. There is nothing in this world so cleansing as knowledge; the one who has grown ripe in this discipline finds it, in time, within his own self. The man of faith, who is intent on it and holds his senses in check, gains this knowledge; and once he has it, he comes swiftly to the highest peace. But the man who is ignorant, who has no faith, whose nature is all doubt, is lost. For the doubting soul there is neither this world nor the next, nor any happiness anywhere. Actions cannot bind the one who has given up his deeds through the discipline of Yoga, whose doubt has been cut away by knowledge, who has come home to himself, Arjuna. So with the sword of knowledge cut down this doubt that springs from ignorance and lodges in your heart. Take up the discipline, Arjuna, and stand up and fight.
