The Bhagavad Gita, Chapter XII

The Way of Devotion

Chapter XII12 of 18

Arjuna said: Some are ever steadfast in their devotion and worship you as I see you now, present and revealed; others worship the imperishable, the unmanifest. Of these two, which have the surer grasp of the discipline?

Krishna said: Those who fix their minds on me and worship me, ever steadfast, with the fullness of their faith — these I hold to be the most deeply joined to the discipline. But those who worship the imperishable, the unmanifest, the indefinable; the unchanging, all-pervading, unthinkable, the one that stands at the summit, fixed and immovable; those who have reined in the whole crowd of their senses, who keep an even mind toward everything and rejoice in the good of all that lives — they too come to me.

Yet theirs is the harder labor, the harder road; for the goal that is unmanifest is painful for the embodied to reach. It is hard for those who wear a body to take hold of what has no form. But those who lay all their actions on me, who make me their one aim, who worship me and meditate on me with a devotion that turns to nothing else — for them, Arjuna, I become before long the one who lifts them up out of the ocean of death and rebirth, since their minds are set on me. So fix your mind on me alone, and let your understanding rest in me; then you will dwell in me from this time on. Of this there is no doubt.

But if you cannot hold your mind steady on me, then seek to reach me by the discipline of steady practice, Arjuna. And if you have not the strength even for steady practice, then make your work an offering to me; for even by doing your work for my sake, you will come to perfection. And if you cannot do even this, if you cannot make me your refuge, then, with your whole self mastered, give up the fruit of all your actions. For knowledge is better than practice, and meditation is better than knowledge, and the giving up of the fruit of action is better than meditation — for from that renouncing, peace comes at once.

The one who hates no living thing, who is friendly and compassionate to all, free of any sense of "I" and "mine," the same in pain and pleasure, patient under everything; the disciplined soul who is always content, master of himself, firm in his resolve, who has given me his mind and his understanding and is devoted to me — that one is dear to me. The one before whom the world does not flinch, and who does not flinch before the world; who is free of the agitation of joy and impatience and fear — that one is dear to me. The one who wants nothing, who is pure and capable, untroubled and unconcerned, who has let go of every undertaking, and who is devoted to me — that one is dear to me. The one who neither rejoices nor hates, neither grieves nor longs, who has given up clinging to good fortune and bad alike, and who is full of devotion — that one is dear to me. The one who holds himself the same toward enemy and friend, the same in honor and disgrace, the same in cold and heat, in pleasure and pain, free of all attachment; who takes blame and praise as one, who keeps silence, content with whatever comes, his heart at home nowhere on earth, his mind made firm, and who is full of devotion — that one, that man, is dear to me. But those who hold fast to this deathless teaching just as I have told it, who keep faith with it and make me their highest aim — those who are devoted to me are dearest of all to me.
