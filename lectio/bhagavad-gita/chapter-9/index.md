# The Bhagavad Gita, Chapter IX

The Royal Knowledge and the Royal Secret

Krishna said: To you, who find no fault in it, I will now reveal this deepest knowledge, this most secret wisdom, knowing which you will be freed from all that is wrong. This is the royal knowledge, the royal secret, the purest of all; you can see its truth for yourself, it accords with what is right, it is easy to live by, and it never wears away.

Those who put no faith in this teaching, son of Bharata, do not reach me; they return again to the round of death and change. By me, in my unseen form, this whole universe is spread out. All beings rest in me, but I am not in them. And yet they do not rest in me either — look on this, the mystery of my power: my self gives birth to all beings and sustains them, and still it does not dwell within them. As the mighty wind moves everywhere, yet always remains within space, so all beings remain within me. Understand this.

At the end of an age, Arjuna, all beings return into the nature that is mine; at the beginning of the next, I send them forth again. Holding sway over the nature that is my own, again and again I send forth this whole host of beings, helpless as they are under the power of that nature. Yet these acts do not bind me, Arjuna; I sit apart from them, like one indifferent, unattached to all this work. Under my oversight, nature brings forth everything that moves and everything that stands still; this is how the world keeps turning.

The deluded despise me when I take a human form, blind to the higher nature that is mine, the lord of all that lives. Their hopes are empty, their deeds are empty, their knowledge is empty; they have given themselves over to the deluded, fiendish nature that misleads. But the great souls, the mahatmas, who have taken on the divine nature, worship me with a mind that does not wander, knowing me to be the imperishable source of all beings. Always glorifying me, striving with firm vows, bowing before me in love, they wait on me with unbroken devotion. Others again worship me with the offering of knowledge, as the one, or the many, or the all — turned toward me in my every face.

I am the rite; I am the sacrifice; I am the offering made to the dead; I am the healing herb; I am the sacred word; I am the clarified butter, the fire, and the offering poured into it. I am the father of this world, its mother, the one who sustains it, its grandfather; I am what is to be known, the purifier, the sacred sound OM; I am the Rig, the Sama, and the Yajur Veda. I am the goal, the upholder, the lord, the witness, the home, the refuge, the friend; I am the origin and the dissolution, the ground, the resting place, the imperishable seed. I give heat; I hold back the rain and I send it; I am deathlessness, and I am death; I am what is and what is not, Arjuna.

Those who follow the three Vedas, who drink the sacred soma and are cleansed of their sins, who worship me with sacrifices seeking to reach heaven — they win the holy world of Indra and taste in the sky the divine pleasures of the gods. But when they have enjoyed that vast world of heaven, their merit spent, they fall back into the world of mortals. So those who follow the letter of the three Vedas, longing for pleasures, gain only what comes and goes. But those who worship me alone, thinking of nothing else, always devoted — to them, who are ever steadfast, I bring all they need and guard what they have.

Even those who, with faith, worship other gods, worship me, Arjuna — though not in the right way; for I am the one who receives every sacrifice, and its lord. But they do not know me as I truly am, and so they fall. Those who are vowed to the gods go to the gods; those vowed to the ancestors go to the ancestors; those who worship the spirits go to the spirits; but those who worship me come to me.

Whoever offers me, with love, a leaf, a flower, a fruit, or water — that offering of love, made by the pure of heart, I gladly accept. So, Arjuna, whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer or give away, whatever discipline you take on, do it as an offering to me. In this way you will be freed from the bonds of action, from its good and evil fruit alike; with your mind made one with me through this discipline of renunciation, set free, you will come to me.

I am the same to all beings; none is hateful to me and none is dear. But those who worship me with love are in me, and I am in them. Even if the worst of sinners worships me, turning to me with his whole heart, he is to be counted among the good, for he has set his mind on the right path. Soon he becomes a soul devoted to what is right, and comes to lasting peace. Know this for certain, Arjuna: no one who is devoted to me is ever lost. For whoever takes refuge in me, son of Pritha — even those born of the womb of sin, women, vaishyas, and shudras too — reaches the highest. How much more, then, the holy brahmins and the devoted royal sages! So, since you have come into this fleeting world of sorrow, worship me. Fix your mind on me, be devoted to me, sacrifice to me, bow down before me; so, having made me your highest aim, your whole self joined to me, you shall come to me.
