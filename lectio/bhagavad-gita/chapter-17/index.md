# The Bhagavad Gita, Chapter XVII

The Threefold Faith

Arjuna said: But what of those who set the scriptures aside, Krishna, and yet worship full of faith — where do they stand? Is their faith of the nature of goodness, of passion, or of darkness?

Krishna said: The faith of every person springs from his own nature, and it is of three kinds — sattvic, rajasic, or tamasic, goodness, passion, or darkness. Hear how. A person's faith answers to what he himself is, Arjuna. A person is made of his faith: as his faith is, so is he. Those in whom goodness rules worship the gods; those ruled by passion worship the Yakshas and Rakshasas; and those wrapped in darkness offer their worship to ghosts and to the hordes of the dead. And there are those who take up fierce penances that no scripture commands, driven by hypocrisy and the pride of self — violent men, racked with desire, torturing the cluster of elements that make up the body, and tormenting me, who dwell within it. Know them for men of demonic resolve.

Even the food that each person prefers is of three kinds, and so too are sacrifice, austerity, and giving. Hear how they differ. The foods that the people of goodness love are those that lengthen life and give strength, health, and good cheer — savory, rich, wholesome, and gladdening to the heart. The foods that the people of passion crave are bitter, sour, salty, scorching, sharp, and harsh, and they bring pain, grief, and sickness. And the food that the people of darkness take is stale and tasteless, putrid and gone off, left over from another's meal, and unfit to offer — the food of impurity.

A sacrifice is sattvic when it is offered as the scriptures direct, by those who look for no reward, who steady the mind and resolve simply, "This is to be done." A sacrifice is rajasic when it is offered with an eye to its reward, or for the sake of show — know this, best of the Bharatas. And a sacrifice is tamasic when it flouts the rule, when no food is shared and no sacred words are sung, when no gifts are given to the priests, and the whole rite is performed without faith.

The austerity of the body is this: to honor the gods, the twice-born, one's teachers and the wise; to keep oneself pure, upright, and chaste; and to do no harm to any living thing. The austerity of speech is this: to speak words that wound no one, words that are true, kind, and helpful — and to keep up the steady recitation of the sacred word. And the austerity of the mind is this: serenity, gentleness, silence, mastery of oneself, and a heart made pure. This threefold austerity, practiced with deep faith by those who look for no reward, the wise call sattvic. Austerity taken up for show, to win respect, honor, and reverence, is rajasic — unsteady, and soon gone. And austerity that a man practices to torture himself with deluded notions, or to bring another to ruin, is called tamasic.

A gift is sattvic when it is given as a duty, with nothing expected in return, to a worthy person, in the right place, and at the right time. A gift is rajasic when it is given grudgingly, or in hope of something back, or with an eye to the reward it will bring. And a gift is tamasic when it is given without respect, even with contempt, to one who does not deserve it, in the wrong place and at the wrong time.

OM TAT SAT: this is the threefold name of Brahman, by which, in the beginning, the brahmins, the Vedas, and the sacrifices were all ordained. So it is that those who speak of Brahman begin every act of sacrifice, of giving, and of austerity always with the sound OM, as the scriptures lay down. With the word TAT, "That," those who seek release perform their acts of sacrifice, austerity, and giving, looking for no reward. The word SAT means what is real and what is good; and so, Arjuna, the word SAT is used also of any action that is worthy. Steadfastness in sacrifice, in austerity, and in giving is called SAT; and any action done for the sake of these is likewise called SAT. But whatever is offered, given, or done without faith is called asat, "unreal" — it is nothing, Arjuna, neither here nor hereafter.
