The Bhagavad Gita, Chapter V

The Renouncing of Works

Chapter V5 of 18

Arjuna said: One moment, Krishna, you praise the giving up of action; the next, you praise action done in discipline. Tell me plainly, settle it for me: which of these two is the better way?

Krishna said: To give up action and to do action in discipline both lead to the highest good; but of the two, the discipline of action is better than the giving up of action. Know that the true renouncer is the one who neither hungers for anything nor recoils from anything; free of the play of opposites, he is set loose from bondage easily, Arjuna. It is only the unschooled who speak of Sankhya and Yoga as two separate things; the wise know better. Whoever is established in one of them gains the fruit of both. The state that the followers of Sankhya reach is reached by the followers of Yoga as well; whoever sees that Sankhya and Yoga are one sees truly.

But renunciation by itself, Arjuna, is hard to come by without the discipline of action; the sage made one with that discipline reaches Brahman before long. The one who is so disciplined, whose self is pure, who has mastered his body and his senses, whose self has become the self of all that lives — such a person is not stained even while he acts. "I do nothing at all," the disciplined one thinks, who knows the truth of things — for in seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking, sleeping, breathing, speaking, letting go, taking hold, opening and closing his eyes, he holds throughout that it is only the senses moving among the things of sense.

Whoever acts laying all his actions on Brahman, having let go of attachment, is untouched by evil — as a lotus leaf is untouched by the water. With the body, with the mind, with the understanding, even with the bare senses alone, the disciplined act, having let go of all clinging, for the cleansing of the self. The disciplined one, giving up the fruit of his action, comes to a peace that is rooted and lasting; the undisciplined, driven by desire, clinging to the fruit, are bound fast.

Renouncing all actions with his mind, the one who has mastered himself sits at ease, the ruler of a city with nine gates, neither acting nor causing any action. It is not the Lord of the world who creates men's sense of agency, or their actions, or the joining of action to its fruit; it is their own nature at work. The all-pervading Lord takes on no one's evil and no one's good. Wisdom is veiled by ignorance, and so the world is led astray. But for those in whom that ignorance of the self has been destroyed by knowledge, knowledge shines out like the sun and makes the highest manifest. Their understanding fixed on it, their very self become it, made steadfast in it, taking it for their one goal, they go where there is no returning, their faults washed away by knowledge.

The learned, those who truly see, look with the same eye on a brahmin rich in learning and humility, on a cow, on an elephant, on a dog, and on an outcaste who lives by eating dogs. Even here, in this life, those whose minds rest in this sameness have conquered the cycle of birth and death; for Brahman is without flaw and the same in all, and so they are established in Brahman. Let a person not be carried away with gladness at getting what he likes, nor sink into bitterness at meeting what he hates; with his understanding steady and unshaken, knowing Brahman, he stands firm in Brahman.

Unattached to the touch of the world outside, he finds the happiness that is within the self; joined by discipline to Brahman, he tastes a joy that does not run dry. For the delights that are born of contact with the senses are themselves the wombs of pain — they have a beginning and an end, Arjuna — and the wise take no joy in them. Whoever can stand fast, even here, even before he is freed of the body, against the surge of desire and anger — that person is disciplined, that person is happy. The one whose happiness is within, whose peace is within, whose light is within — that disciplined soul, having become Brahman, reaches the nirvana of Brahman. The seers win the nirvana of Brahman, their faults worn away, their doubts cut through, their selves under control, rejoicing in the good of all that lives.

For those who have torn themselves free of desire and anger, who have mastered their thought, who know the self, the nirvana of Brahman lies near, on every side. Shutting out the things of sense from his mind, fixing his gaze between the brows, drawing the breath that flows out and the breath that flows in to an even pace within the nostrils, the sage who has made the senses, the mind, and the understanding his own, who has set his whole aim on release, who has thrown off desire and fear and anger — that sage is already, here and now, set free forever. And he comes to my peace, knowing me for the one who enjoys every sacrifice and every austerity, the great Lord of all the worlds, the friend of every living thing.
