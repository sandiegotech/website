# The Tao Te Ching, Chapter 30

Against Force of Arms

Whoever helps a ruler of men by the Way does not use weapons to force his will on the world. Such a course comes back on the one who takes it.

Where armies have camped, thorns and brambles grow. In the wake of great wars come years of famine.

The good commander strikes his blow and stops; he does not dare to press on and take the world by force. He strikes the blow, but does not boast of it; he strikes it, but does not brag; he strikes it, but is not proud. He strikes only because there is no other choice; he strikes, but not to force his mastery.

Things in their full strength grow old. This is what it means to be against the Way, and what is against the Way comes early to its end.
