# The Tao Te Ching, Chapter 20

Alone and Adrift

Give up learning, and you will have no more worries. Between a respectful "yes" and a careless one, how much difference is there really? Between what people call good and what they call bad, how wide is the gulf that separates them?

What everyone fears must indeed be feared. But how vast and endless this all is, with no place to come to rest!

The crowd is merry and content, as if feasting at a great banquet, as if climbing a tower in spring. I alone am still, giving no sign, my desires not yet stirring, like an infant that has not yet learned to smile. I am weary and forlorn, like one with no home to return to. Everyone else has more than enough; I alone seem to have lost everything. Mine is the mind of a fool, muddled and dim.

Ordinary people are bright and clever; I alone am dull. Ordinary people are sharp and sure; I alone am clouded and confused. I drift like the wide sea; I am blown about, as if I had nowhere to settle. Everyone else has their work and their purpose; I alone am awkward and useless, like a rustic. I alone am different from all the rest — I prize being fed by the Mother.
