# The Tao Te Ching, Chapter 55

Full of Power, Like a Newborn Child

One full of power is like a newborn child. Stinging insects do not sting it, fierce beasts do not seize it, birds of prey do not strike it.

Its bones are soft and its sinews weak, yet its grip is firm. It does not yet know the union of female and male, yet its little member stirs — such is the perfection of its vital essence. It can cry all day without growing hoarse — such is the perfection of its harmony.

To know harmony is to know the constant. To know the constant is clarity. To force more life is to court ill omen. For the mind to drive the breath is to overstrain.

Things in their full strength grow old. This is what it means to be against the Way, and what is against the Way comes early to its end.
