# The Tao Te Ching, Chapter 15

The Ancient Masters

The masters of the Way in ancient times were subtle, mysterious, and deep beyond all knowing. Because they could not be known, I can only describe how they appeared.

Wary, like someone crossing a frozen stream in winter. Watchful, like someone who fears the neighbors on every side. Reserved, like a guest. Yielding, like ice on the point of melting. Plain, like an uncarved block of wood. Open, like a valley. And murky, like muddy water.

Who can let the muddy water settle and slowly grow clear? Who can stir what has long been at rest and slowly bring it to life? Whoever holds to this Way does not crave to be full; and because he is never full, he can wear out and be made new again.
