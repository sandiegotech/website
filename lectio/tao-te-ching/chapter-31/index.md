# The Tao Te Ching, Chapter 31

Weapons Are Instruments of Ill Omen

Even the finest weapons are instruments of ill omen, hated, you might say, by every living thing. And so one who has the Way does not dwell among them.

In peace the gentleman honors the left; in war he honors the right. Weapons are instruments of ill omen, not the gentleman's instruments; he takes them up only when there is no other choice. What he prizes is calm and quiet; victory brings him no joy. To find joy in it would be to delight in killing, and the one who delights in killing will never win the world.

On glad occasions the place of honor is the left; on mourning occasions, the right. In the army the second-in-command stands on the left, and the commanding general on the right — set, that is, in the place of the rites of mourning. For when a multitude of men has been killed, we should weep for them with grief and sorrow; and a victory in battle should be observed as a funeral.
