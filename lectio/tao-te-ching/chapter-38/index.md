# The Tao Te Ching, Chapter 38

Power and Its Decline

The highest power makes no claim to power, and that is why it has power. The lowest power never lets go of its power, and that is why it has none.

The highest power acts without forcing, and acts from no motive. The lowest power acts, and acts from a motive.

The highest humaneness acts, but from no motive. The highest righteousness acts, and from a motive. The highest ritual acts, and when no one answers it, it bares the arm and drags them along by force.

So when the Way is lost, power appears. When power is lost, humaneness appears. When humaneness is lost, righteousness appears. When righteousness is lost, ritual appears.

Ritual is the thin husk of loyalty and good faith, and the beginning of disorder. Foreknowledge is only the flower of the Way, and the beginning of folly.

And so the great person dwells in the substance, not in the husk; dwells in the fruit, not in the flower. He lets that go and takes hold of this.
