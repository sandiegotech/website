# The Tao Te Ching, Chapter 2

The Mutual Arising of Opposites

When everyone in the world recognizes beauty as beauty, ugliness has already appeared. When everyone recognizes good as good, what is not good has already appeared.

So it is that being and non-being give rise to each other; the difficult and the easy complete each other; the long and the short measure each other; the high and the low lean on each other; note and voice harmonize with each other; before and after follow each other.

And so the sage goes about the work without forcing it, and teaches without using words.

All things arise, and the sage does not turn any away. They are brought to life, and the sage claims nothing as his own. He acts, but does not rely on what he has done. The work is done, and he does not rest in it as an achievement.

It is just because he does not rest in it that it never leaves him.
