# The Tao Te Ching, Chapter 27

Leaving No Track

A good traveler leaves no track. A good speaker says nothing that can be faulted. A good reckoner needs no counting rods. A good closer uses no bolt, yet what he has shut cannot be opened. A good binder uses no cord, yet what he has bound cannot be loosed.

And so the sage is always good at saving people, and abandons no one; always good at saving things, and throws nothing away. This is called following the light.

So the good are the teachers of the bad, and the bad are the raw material of the good. To think nothing of the teacher, to care nothing for the material — this, however clever you are, is to be badly lost. This is the essential mystery.
