# The Tao Te Ching, Chapter 64

A Journey of a Thousand Miles

What is still is easy to hold. What has not yet stirred is easy to plan for. What is brittle is easy to break. What is small is easy to scatter. Deal with a thing before it comes to be; set it in order before disorder begins.

The tree you can barely reach your arms around grew from a sprout too small to see. A tower nine stories high rose from a single basket of earth. A journey of a thousand miles begins beneath your feet.

Whoever forces it ruins it; whoever grasps it loses it. So the sage acts without forcing, and ruins nothing; grasps at nothing, and loses nothing. People fail at their tasks again and again just as they come within reach of success. Be as careful at the end as at the beginning, and you will spoil nothing.

So the sage desires to have no desires and sets no value on goods that are hard to come by; learns to unlearn and returns to what the crowd has rushed past. In this way the sage helps all things follow their own nature, and never dares to force them.
