# Hamlet, Act III

SCENE I. A room in the Castle.

Enter King, Queen, Polonius, Ophelia, Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.

KING  
And can you find no roundabout way  
to get from him why he puts on this confusion,  
grating so harshly on all his quiet days  
with turbulent and dangerous madness?

ROSENCRANTZ  
He does admit he feels himself unsettled,  
but from what cause he will by no means say.

GUILDENSTERN  
Nor do we find him willing to be sounded;  
with a crafty madness he keeps his distance  
when we would draw him on to some confession  
of his true state.

QUEEN  
Did he receive you well?

ROSENCRANTZ  
Most like a gentleman.

GUILDENSTERN  
But with much forcing of himself to it.

ROSENCRANTZ  
Sparing with questions, but to ours  
most free in his reply.

QUEEN  
Did you try to tempt him to any pastime?

ROSENCRANTZ  
Madam, it so happened that certain players  
we overtook on the way. We told him of these,  
and there did seem in him a kind of joy  
to hear of it. They are about the court,  
and, as I think, already have their orders  
to play before him tonight.

POLONIUS  
It's most true;  
and he begged me to entreat your Majesties  
to hear and see the matter.

KING  
With all my heart; and it does much content me  
to hear him so inclined.  
Good gentlemen, give him a further push,  
and drive his purpose on toward these delights.

ROSENCRANTZ  
We shall, my lord.

[*Exeunt Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.*]

KING  
Sweet Gertrude, leave us too,  
for we have secretly sent for Hamlet here,  
so that he, as if by accident, may meet  
Ophelia face to face.  
Her father and myself, lawful spies,  
will so place ourselves that, seeing unseen,  
we may judge their encounter frankly,  
and gather, by how he behaves,  
whether it's the affliction of his love or not  
that makes him suffer so.

QUEEN  
I shall obey you.  
And for your part, Ophelia, I do wish  
that your good beauty be the happy cause  
of Hamlet's wildness; so I hope your virtues  
will bring him to his usual self again,  
to both your honors.

OPHELIA  
Madam, I wish it may.

[*Exit Queen.*]

POLONIUS  
Ophelia, walk you here.—Gracious, if you please,  
we will conceal ourselves.—[*To Ophelia.*] Read on this book,  
so the show of such devotion may explain  
your being alone.—We are often to blame in this:  
it's too well proved, that with a face of devotion  
and pious action we sugar over  
the devil himself.

KING  
[*Aside.*] Oh, it's too true!  
How sharp a lash that speech gives to my conscience!  
The harlot's cheek, made beautiful with paint,  
is not more ugly next to what helps it  
than is my deed beside my most painted word.  
O heavy burden!

POLONIUS  
I hear him coming. Let's withdraw, my lord.

[*Exeunt King and Polonius.*]

Enter Hamlet.

HAMLET  
To be, or not to be, that is the question:  
Whether it's nobler in the mind to suffer  
the slings and arrows of outrageous fortune,  
or to take arms against a sea of troubles,  
and by opposing end them? To die—to sleep,  
no more; and by a sleep to say we end  
the heartache, and the thousand natural shocks  
that flesh is heir to: it's a consummation  
devoutly to be wished. To die, to sleep.  
To sleep, perchance to dream—ay, there's the rub,  
for in that sleep of death what dreams may come,  
when we have shuffled off this mortal coil,  
must give us pause. There's the consideration  
that makes calamity of so long a life.  
For who would bear the whips and scorns of time,  
the oppressor's wrong, the proud man's contempt,  
the pangs of rejected love, the law's delay,  
the insolence of office, and the spurns  
that patient merit takes from the unworthy,  
when he himself might settle his account  
with a bare dagger? Who would carry these burdens,  
to grunt and sweat under a weary life,  
but that the dread of something after death,  
the undiscovered country, from whose border  
no traveller returns, puzzles the will,  
and makes us rather bear those ills we have  
than fly to others that we know not of?  
Thus conscience does make cowards of us all,  
and thus the native hue of resolution  
is sicklied over with the pale cast of thought,  
and enterprises of great pith and moment,  
with this regard their currents turn awry  
and lose the name of action. Soft you now,  
the fair Ophelia! Nymph, in your prayers  
be all my sins remembered.

OPHELIA  
Good my lord,  
how has your honor been these many days?

HAMLET  
I humbly thank you; well, well, well.

OPHELIA  
My lord, I have remembrances of yours  
that I have long wished to give back to you.  
I pray you, now receive them.

HAMLET  
No, not I.  
I never gave you anything.

OPHELIA  
My honored lord, you know right well you did,  
and with them words composed of such sweet breath  
as made the things more rich; their perfume lost,  
take these again; for to the noble mind  
rich gifts grow poor when givers prove unkind.  
There, my lord.

HAMLET  
Ha, ha! Are you honest?

OPHELIA  
My lord?

HAMLET  
Are you fair?

OPHELIA  
What does your lordship mean?

HAMLET  
That if you are honest and fair, your honesty should allow no dealings with your beauty.

OPHELIA  
Could beauty, my lord, keep better company than with honesty?

HAMLET  
Ay, truly; for the power of beauty will sooner turn honesty into a bawd than the force of honesty can make beauty over in its own image. This was once a paradox, but now the times prove it true. I did love you once.

OPHELIA  
Indeed, my lord, you made me believe so.

HAMLET  
You should not have believed me; for virtue cannot be so grafted onto our old stock but we shall still taste of the wild. I loved you not.

OPHELIA  
I was the more deceived.

HAMLET  
Get thee to a nunnery. Why would you be a breeder of sinners? I am myself reasonably honest; and yet I could accuse myself of such things that it would have been better my mother had not borne me. I am very proud, revengeful, ambitious, with more offenses at my beck than I have thoughts to put them in, imagination to give them shape, or time to act them in. What should such fellows as I do, crawling between earth and heaven? We are downright knaves, all; believe none of us. Go your ways to a nunnery. Where's your father?

OPHELIA  
At home, my lord.

HAMLET  
Let the doors be shut on him, so he may play the fool nowhere but in his own house. Farewell.

OPHELIA  
Oh, help him, you sweet heavens!

HAMLET  
If you do marry, I'll give you this plague for your dowry: be as chaste as ice, as pure as snow, you shall not escape slander. Get thee to a nunnery, go: farewell. Or if you must marry, marry a fool; for wise men know well enough what monsters you make of them. To a nunnery, go; and quickly too. Farewell.

OPHELIA  
O heavenly powers, restore him!

HAMLET  
I have heard of your paintings too, well enough. God has given you one face, and you make yourselves another. You jig, you sway, and you lisp, and give pet names to God's creatures, and pass off your loose ways as innocence. Go to, I'll have no more of it; it has made me mad. I say, we will have no more marriages. Those that are married already, all but one, shall live; the rest shall stay as they are. To a nunnery, go.

[*Exit.*]

OPHELIA  
Oh, what a noble mind is here overthrown!  
The courtier's, soldier's, scholar's eye, tongue, sword,  
the hope and rose of this fair state,  
the mirror of fashion and the mold of form,  
the one observed by all observers, quite, quite down!  
And I, of ladies most dejected and wretched,  
who drank the honey of his music vows,  
now see that noble and most sovereign reason,  
like sweet bells jangled out of tune and harsh,  
that unmatched form and feature of full-blown youth  
blasted with madness. Oh, woe is me,  
to have seen what I have seen, see what I see.

Enter King and Polonius.

KING  
Love? His feelings do not tend that way,  
nor what he spoke, though it lacked form a little,  
was anything like madness. There's something in his soul  
over which his melancholy sits brooding,  
and I do fear the hatch and the disclosing of it  
will be some danger; which to prevent,  
I have, in quick decision,  
set it down: he shall go with speed to England  
to demand the tribute they've neglected.  
Perhaps the seas, and different countries,  
with their changing sights, shall drive out  
this settled something in his heart,  
on which his brain keeps beating and so puts him  
beside himself. What do you think of it?

POLONIUS  
It will do well. But still I do believe  
the origin and beginning of his grief  
sprang from neglected love.—How now, Ophelia?  
You need not tell us what Lord Hamlet said;  
we heard it all.—My lord, do as you please,  
but if you think it fit, after the play,  
let his queen mother, all alone, entreat him  
to show his grief; let her be blunt with him,  
and I'll be placed, if you please, within hearing  
of all their talk. If she find him out no better,  
to England send him; or confine him where  
your wisdom thinks best.

KING  
It shall be so.  
Madness in great ones must not go unwatched.

[*Exeunt.*]

SCENE II. A hall in the Castle.

Enter Hamlet and certain Players.

HAMLET  
Speak the speech, I pray you, as I pronounced it to you, trippingly on the tongue. But if you mouth it, as many of your players do, I would just as soon the town-crier spoke my lines. Nor do not saw the air too much with your hand, like this, but use everything gently; for in the very torrent, tempest, and—as I may say—whirlwind of passion, you must acquire and produce a restraint that gives it smoothness. Oh, it offends me to the soul to hear a robustious, wig-headed fellow tear a passion to tatters, to very rags, to split the ears of the groundlings, who, for the most part, can take in nothing but baffling dumb-shows and noise. I would have such a fellow whipped for overdoing Termagant. It out-Herods Herod. Pray you avoid it.

FIRST PLAYER  
I promise you, your honor.

HAMLET  
Don't be too tame either; but let your own discretion be your tutor. Suit the action to the word, the word to the action, with this special care, that you don't overstep the modesty of nature; for anything so overdone misses the whole point of playing, whose end, both at the first and now, was and is, to hold, as it were, the mirror up to nature: to show virtue her own feature, scorn her own image, and the very age and body of the time its form and impression. Now, this overdone, or done too feebly, though it make the unskilled laugh, cannot but make the discerning grieve; and the judgment of one of those must, in your reckoning, outweigh a whole theater of the others. Oh, there are players I have seen play—and heard others praise, and highly too—who, not to put it crudely, having neither the speech of Christians, nor the gait of Christian, pagan, nor man, have so strutted and bellowed that I have thought some of Nature's apprentices had made men, and not made them well, they imitated humanity so abominably.

FIRST PLAYER  
I hope we have reformed that tolerably well with us, sir.

HAMLET  
Oh, reform it altogether. And let those that play your clowns speak no more than is set down for them. For there are some of them that will laugh themselves, just to set a pack of barren spectators laughing too, when in the meantime some necessary point of the play needs to be considered. That's villainous, and shows a most pitiful ambition in the fool that does it. Go make yourselves ready.

[*Exeunt Players.*]

Enter Polonius, Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.

HAMLET  
How now, my lord?  
Will the King hear this piece of work?

POLONIUS  
And the Queen too, and right away.

HAMLET  
Tell the players to make haste.

[*Exit Polonius.*]

HAMLET  
Will you two help to hurry them?

ROSENCRANTZ and GUILDENSTERN  
We will, my lord.

[*Exeunt Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.*]

HAMLET  
What ho, Horatio!

Enter Horatio.

HORATIO  
Here, sweet lord, at your service.

HAMLET  
Horatio, you are as fair a man  
as I have ever dealt with in my life.

HORATIO  
O my dear lord—

HAMLET  
No, do not think I flatter;  
for what advancement may I hope from you,  
who have no income but your own good spirits  
to feed and clothe you? Why should the poor be flattered?  
No, let the sugared tongue lick foolish pomp,  
and bend the eager hinges of the knee  
where profit follows fawning. Do you hear?  
Since my dear soul was mistress of her choice,  
and could tell one man from another, her election  
has sealed you for herself. For you have been  
as one who, in suffering everything, suffers nothing,  
a man that Fortune's blows and rewards  
has taken with equal thanks. And blessed are those  
whose blood and judgment are so well mingled  
that they are not a pipe for Fortune's finger  
to sound what note she pleases. Give me the man  
that is not passion's slave, and I will wear him  
in my heart's core, ay, in my heart of hearts,  
as I do you. But enough of this.  
There is a play tonight before the King.  
One scene of it comes near the circumstance  
which I have told you, of my father's death.  
I beg you, when you see that act under way,  
with the very closest watching of your soul  
observe my uncle. If his hidden guilt  
does not unkennel itself at one speech,  
it is a damned ghost that we have seen,  
and my imaginings are as foul  
as Vulcan's forge. Give him your closest note;  
for I will rivet my eyes to his face;  
and afterward we will both join our judgments  
in reckoning how he looked.

HORATIO  
Well, my lord.  
If he steal anything while this play is playing,  
and escape detection, I will pay for the theft.

HAMLET  
They are coming to the play. I must act mad.  
Get yourself a place.

Danish march. A flourish. Enter King, Queen, Polonius, Ophelia, Rosencrantz, Guildenstern and others.

KING  
How fares our cousin Hamlet?

HAMLET  
Excellent, in faith; I dine on the chameleon's dish: I eat the air, stuffed with promises. You cannot fatten capons so.

KING  
I can make nothing of this answer, Hamlet; these words are not mine.

HAMLET  
No, nor mine now. [*To Polonius.*] My lord, you acted once at the university, you say?

POLONIUS  
That I did, my lord, and was reckoned a good actor.

HAMLET  
What did you play?

POLONIUS  
I played Julius Caesar. I was killed in the Capitol. Brutus killed me.

HAMLET  
It was a brute part of him to kill so capital a calf there. Are the players ready?

ROSENCRANTZ  
Ay, my lord; they wait upon your pleasure.

QUEEN  
Come here, my dear Hamlet, sit by me.

HAMLET  
No, good mother, here's metal more attractive.

POLONIUS  
[*To the King.*] O ho! Do you mark that?

HAMLET  
Lady, shall I lie in your lap?

[*Lying down at Ophelia's feet.*]

OPHELIA  
No, my lord.

HAMLET  
I mean, my head upon your lap?

OPHELIA  
Ay, my lord.

HAMLET  
Do you think I meant country matters?

OPHELIA  
I think nothing, my lord.

HAMLET  
That's a fair thought to lie between a maid's legs.

OPHELIA  
What is, my lord?

HAMLET  
Nothing.

OPHELIA  
You are merry, my lord.

HAMLET  
Who, I?

OPHELIA  
Ay, my lord.

HAMLET  
O God, your only jig-maker! What should a man do but be merry? For look how cheerfully my mother looks, and my father dead within these two hours.

OPHELIA  
No, it's twice two months, my lord.

HAMLET  
So long? Nay, then let the devil wear black, for I'll have a suit of sables. O heavens! Dead two months ago, and not forgotten yet? Then there's hope a great man's memory may outlive his life by half a year. But, by the Lady, he must build churches then, or else he shall get no remembrance, like the hobby-horse, whose epitaph is 'For, O, for O, the hobby-horse is forgot!'

Trumpets sound. The dumb show enters.

*Enter a King and a Queen very lovingly; the Queen embracing him and he her. She kneels, and makes show of protestation unto him. He takes her up, and declines his head upon her neck. Lays him down upon a bank of flowers. She, seeing him asleep, leaves him. Soon a man comes in, takes off his crown, kisses it, pours poison in the King's ears, and exits. The Queen returns, finds the King dead, and makes passionate action. The Poisoner with some three or four Mutes, comes in again, seeming to lament with her. The dead body is carried away. The Poisoner woos the Queen with gifts. She seems reluctant for a while, but in the end accepts his love.*

[*Exeunt.*]

OPHELIA  
What does this mean, my lord?

HAMLET  
Marry, this is sneaking mischief; it means trouble.

OPHELIA  
Most likely this show sums up the plot of the play.

Enter Prologue.

HAMLET  
We shall learn it from this fellow: the players cannot keep a secret; they'll tell all.

OPHELIA  
Will they tell us what this show meant?

HAMLET  
Ay, or any show that you'll show him. Be not you ashamed to show, and he'll not be ashamed to tell you what it means.

OPHELIA  
You are wicked, you are wicked. I'll watch the play.

PROLOGUE  
For us, and for our tragedy,  
here stooping to your clemency,  
we beg your hearing patiently.

HAMLET  
Is this a prologue, or the motto on a ring?

OPHELIA  
It's brief, my lord.

HAMLET  
As a woman's love.

Enter a King and a Queen.

PLAYER KING  
Full thirty times has Phoebus' chariot gone round  
Neptune's salt tides and Tellus' rounded ground,  
and thirty dozen moons with borrowed sheen  
about the world twelve times thirty have been,  
since love our hearts, and Hymen our hands,  
joined mutually in most sacred bands.

PLAYER QUEEN  
So many journeys may the sun and moon  
make us count over again before love is done.  
But, woe is me, you are so sick of late,  
so far from cheer and from your former state,  
that I distrust you. Yet, though I distrust,  
discomfort you, my lord, it never must:  
for women's fear and love keep parity,  
in nothing at all, or in extremity.  
Now what my love is, you have proof to know,  
and as my love is sized, my fear is so.  
Where love is great, the slightest doubts are fear;  
where little fears grow great, great love grows there.

PLAYER KING  
In faith, I must leave you, love, and shortly too:  
my working powers their functions cease to do;  
and you shall live in this fair world behind,  
honored, beloved; and perhaps one as kind  
for a husband shall you—

PLAYER QUEEN  
Oh, say no more!  
Such love would be sheer treason in my breast.  
In a second husband let me be accursed!  
None wed the second but who killed the first.

HAMLET  
[*Aside.*] Wormwood, wormwood.

PLAYER QUEEN  
The motives that move a second marriage  
are base concerns of gain, but none of love.  
A second time I kill my husband dead,  
when a second husband kisses me in bed.

PLAYER KING  
I do believe you think what now you speak;  
but what we resolve, we often break.  
Purpose is but the slave to memory,  
of violent birth, but poor durability:  
which now, like fruit unripe, sticks on the tree,  
but falls unshaken when it ripe shall be.  
It's most necessary that we forget  
to pay ourselves what to ourselves is debt.  
What to ourselves in passion we propose,  
the passion ending, does the purpose lose.  
The violence of either grief or joy  
does its own intent itself destroy.  
Where joy most revels, grief does most lament;  
grief turns to joy, joy grieves, on slight event.  
This world is not forever; nor is it strange  
that even our loves should with our fortunes change,  
for it's a question still left us to prove,  
whether love leads fortune, or fortune love.  
The great man down, you see his favorite flies;  
the poor man, raised, makes friends of enemies.  
And so far love does upon fortune tend;  
for who needs nothing shall never lack a friend,  
and who in want a hollow friend does try  
at once converts him to an enemy.  
But, to end in order where I begun:  
our wills and fates so contrary run  
that all our plans are constantly overthrown;  
our thoughts are ours, their ends are not our own.  
So think you will no second husband wed;  
but let that thought die when your first lord is dead.

PLAYER QUEEN  
May earth give me no food, nor heaven light,  
may sport and rest be locked from me day and night,  
may my trust and hope turn to despair,  
an anchorite's bare cell be all my fare,  
may each opposite that drains the face of joy  
meet what I would have prosper, and destroy it!  
Both here and beyond pursue me lasting strife,  
if, once a widow, ever I be wife.

HAMLET  
[*To Ophelia.*] If she should break it now.

PLAYER KING  
It's deeply sworn. Sweet, leave me here awhile;  
my spirits grow dull, and I would gladly cheat  
the tedious day with sleep.  
[*Sleeps.*]

PLAYER QUEEN  
Sleep rock your brain,  
and never come mischance between us two.

[*Exit.*]

HAMLET  
Madam, how do you like this play?

QUEEN  
The lady protests too much, methinks.

HAMLET  
Oh, but she'll keep her word.

KING  
Have you heard the plot? Is there no offense in it?

HAMLET  
No, no, they only jest, poison in jest; no offense in the world.

KING  
What do you call the play?

HAMLET  
*The Mousetrap.* Marry, how so? In a manner of speaking. This play is the image of a murder done in Vienna. Gonzago is the duke's name, his wife Baptista. You shall see soon enough; it's a knavish piece of work, but what of that? Your Majesty, and we that have clear consciences, it doesn't touch us. Let the galled horse wince; our backs are unbruised.

Enter Lucianus.

HAMLET  
This is one Lucianus, nephew to the King.

OPHELIA  
You are as good as a chorus, my lord.

HAMLET  
I could interpret between you and your love, if I could watch the puppets dallying.

OPHELIA  
You are sharp, my lord, you are sharp.

HAMLET  
It would cost you a groan to take off my edge.

OPHELIA  
Still better, and worse.

HAMLET  
So you mis-take your husbands.—Begin, murderer. Pox, leave your damnable faces, and begin. Come: the croaking raven bellows for revenge.

LUCIANUS  
Thoughts black, hands ready, drugs fit, and the time agreeing;  
the moment my ally, and no creature seeing;  
you rank mixture, of midnight weeds collected,  
with Hecate's curse thrice blasted, thrice infected,  
your natural magic and dire property  
on wholesome life now seize immediately.

[*Pours the poison into the sleeper's ears.*]

HAMLET  
He poisons him in the garden for his estate. His name's Gonzago. The story is on record, and written in very choice Italian. You shall see soon how the murderer wins the love of Gonzago's wife.

OPHELIA  
The King rises.

HAMLET  
What, frightened by a blank shot?

QUEEN  
How fares my lord?

POLONIUS  
Stop the play.

KING  
Give me some light. Away.

All  
Lights, lights, lights.

[*Exeunt all but Hamlet and Horatio.*]

HAMLET  
Why, let the wounded deer go weep,  
the unhurt deer play;  
for some must watch, while some must sleep,  
so runs the world away.  
Wouldn't this, sir, and a forest of feathers—if the rest of my fortunes turn against me—with two Provincial roses on my slashed shoes, get me a place in a company of players, sir?

HORATIO  
Half a share.

HAMLET  
A whole one, I.  
For you do know, O Damon dear,  
this realm was stripped clean  
of Jove himself, and now reigns here  
a very, very—peacock.

HORATIO  
You might have rhymed.

HAMLET  
O good Horatio, I'll take the ghost's word for a thousand pounds. Did you notice?

HORATIO  
Very well, my lord.

HAMLET  
When the talk turned to the poisoning?

HORATIO  
I watched him very closely.

HAMLET  
Ah, ha! Come, some music. Come, the recorders.  
For if the King doesn't like the comedy,  
why then, it seems he likes it not, by God.  
Come, some music.

Enter Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.

GUILDENSTERN  
Good my lord, grant me a word with you.

HAMLET  
Sir, a whole history.

GUILDENSTERN  
The King, sir—

HAMLET  
Ay, sir, what of him?

GUILDENSTERN  
Is in his chambers, marvelously out of sorts.

HAMLET  
With drink, sir?

GUILDENSTERN  
No, my lord; rather with anger.

HAMLET  
Your wisdom would show itself the richer to report this to the doctor; for me to give him his cure would perhaps plunge him into far more anger.

GUILDENSTERN  
Good my lord, put your talk into some order, and don't start off so wildly from my business.

HAMLET  
I am tame, sir; speak.

GUILDENSTERN  
The Queen your mother, in great affliction of spirit, has sent me to you.

HAMLET  
You are welcome.

GUILDENSTERN  
Nay, good my lord, this courtesy is not of the honest kind. If it shall please you to give me a sensible answer, I will do your mother's bidding; if not, your pardon and my leaving shall be the end of my business.

HAMLET  
Sir, I cannot.

GUILDENSTERN  
Cannot what, my lord?

HAMLET  
Give you a sensible answer. My wit's diseased. But, sir, such answer as I can make, you shall command; or rather, as you say, my mother shall. So no more, but to the matter. My mother, you say—

ROSENCRANTZ  
Then thus she says: your behavior has struck her into amazement and bewilderment.

HAMLET  
O wonderful son, who can so astonish a mother! But is there nothing following at the heels of this mother's amazement?

ROSENCRANTZ  
She desires to speak with you in her room before you go to bed.

HAMLET  
We shall obey, were she ten times our mother. Have you any further business with us?

ROSENCRANTZ  
My lord, you once did love me.

HAMLET  
And so I do still, by these fingers of mine that pick and steal.

ROSENCRANTZ  
Good my lord, what is the cause of your distemper? You surely bar the door upon your own freedom if you deny your griefs to your friend.

HAMLET  
Sir, I lack advancement.

ROSENCRANTZ  
How can that be, when you have the voice of the King himself for your succession in Denmark?

HAMLET  
Ay, sir, but 'while the grass grows'—the proverb is a little stale.

Re-enter the Players with recorders.

HAMLET  
Oh, the recorders. Let me see one.—To have a word with you in private: why do you go about to get to windward of me, as if you would drive me into a trap?

GUILDENSTERN  
O my lord, if my duty is too bold, my love is too unmannerly.

HAMLET  
I don't quite understand that. Will you play upon this pipe?

GUILDENSTERN  
My lord, I cannot.

HAMLET  
I pray you.

GUILDENSTERN  
Believe me, I cannot.

HAMLET  
I do beseech you.

GUILDENSTERN  
I know no touch of it, my lord.

HAMLET  
It's as easy as lying: govern these vents with your finger and thumb, give it breath with your mouth, and it will speak most eloquent music. Look you, these are the stops.

GUILDENSTERN  
But these I cannot command to make any harmony. I haven't the skill.

HAMLET  
Why, look you now, what an unworthy thing you make of me! You would play upon me; you would seem to know my stops; you would pluck out the heart of my mystery; you would sound me from my lowest note to the top of my range; and there is much music, excellent voice, in this little instrument, yet you cannot make it speak. By God's blood, do you think I am easier to be played on than a pipe? Call me what instrument you will: though you can fret me, you cannot play upon me.

Enter Polonius.

HAMLET  
God bless you, sir.

POLONIUS  
My lord, the Queen would speak with you, and right away.

HAMLET  
Do you see that cloud over there, almost in the shape of a camel?

POLONIUS  
By the mass, it's like a camel indeed.

HAMLET  
I think it's like a weasel.

POLONIUS  
It's backed like a weasel.

HAMLET  
Or like a whale.

POLONIUS  
Very like a whale.

HAMLET  
Then I'll come to my mother shortly.—They make me play the fool to the top of my bent.—I will come shortly.

POLONIUS  
I'll say so.

[*Exit.*]

HAMLET  
'Shortly' is easily said. Leave me, friends.

[*Exeunt all but Hamlet.*]

HAMLET  
It's now the very witching time of night,  
when churchyards yawn, and hell itself breathes out  
contagion to this world. Now could I drink hot blood,  
and do such bitter business as the day  
would tremble to look on. Soft, now, to my mother.  
O heart, do not lose your nature; never let  
the soul of Nero enter this firm breast.  
Let me be cruel, not unnatural.  
I will speak daggers to her, but use none;  
my tongue and soul in this be hypocrites.  
However my words may shame her,  
to seal them with deeds, my soul, never consent.

[*Exit.*]

SCENE III. A room in the Castle.

Enter King, Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.

KING  
I like him not, nor is it safe for us  
to let his madness range. So prepare yourselves;  
I'll dispatch your commission at once,  
and he shall go along with you to England.  
The interests of our state cannot endure  
a danger so near us as grows by the hour  
out of his lunacies.

GUILDENSTERN  
We will get ready.  
It's a most holy and religious fear,  
to keep so many, many bodies safe  
that live and feed upon your Majesty.

ROSENCRANTZ  
The single, private life is bound  
with all the strength and armor of the mind  
to keep itself from harm; but how much more  
that spirit on whose welfare rest and depend  
the lives of many. The death of a king  
dies not alone, but, like a whirlpool, draws  
what's near it down with it. It is a massive wheel,  
fixed on the summit of the highest mount,  
to whose huge spokes ten thousand lesser things  
are fastened and joined; and when it falls,  
each small attachment, every petty consequence,  
follows in the violent ruin. The King never sighed alone,  
but always with a general groan.

KING  
Arm yourselves, I pray you, for this speedy voyage;  
for we will put fetters on this fear,  
which now goes far too free.

ROSENCRANTZ and GUILDENSTERN  
We will make haste.

[*Exeunt Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.*]

Enter Polonius.

POLONIUS  
My lord, he's going to his mother's room.  
Behind the tapestry I'll hide myself  
to hear how it goes. I'll warrant she'll take him to task;  
and, as you said—and wisely was it said—  
it's fitting that some ear besides a mother's,  
since nature makes them partial, should overhear  
the talk from a place of vantage. Fare you well, my liege;  
I'll call on you before you go to bed,  
and tell you what I learn.

KING  
Thanks, dear my lord.

[*Exit Polonius.*]

KING  
Oh, my offense is rank, it smells to heaven;  
it bears the primal, eldest curse upon it—  
a brother's murder! Pray I cannot,  
though my desire be as sharp as my will:  
my stronger guilt defeats my strong intent,  
and, like a man bound to two tasks at once,  
I stand and pause where I should first begin,  
and do neither. What if this cursed hand  
were thicker than itself with brother's blood—  
is there not rain enough in the sweet heavens  
to wash it white as snow? What is mercy for  
but to face the offense head-on?  
And what's in prayer but this twofold power,  
to stop us before we fall,  
or pardon us once we're down? Then I'll look up.  
My fault is past. But, oh, what form of prayer  
can serve my turn? 'Forgive me my foul murder'?  
That cannot be; since I am still possessed  
of those very things for which I did the murder—  
my crown, my own ambition, and my queen.  
May one be pardoned and keep the offense?  
In the corrupted currents of this world  
the gilded hand of crime may shove justice aside,  
and often it's seen the wicked prize itself  
buys out the law. But it's not so above:  
there is no dodging there; the deed lies exposed  
in its true nature, and we ourselves are forced,  
even to the teeth and forehead of our faults,  
to give evidence. What then? What's left?  
Try what repentance can. What can it not?  
Yet what can it, when one cannot repent?  
O wretched state! O breast as black as death!  
O limed soul, that, struggling to be free,  
becomes more trapped! Help, angels! Make the attempt:  
bow, stubborn knees; and heart with strings of steel,  
be soft as the sinews of a new-born babe.  
All may be well.

[*Retires and kneels.*]

Enter Hamlet.

HAMLET  
Now might I do it neatly, now he is praying.  
And now I'll do it. And so he goes to heaven;  
and so am I revenged. That would be worth a thought:  
a villain kills my father, and for that  
I, his sole son, send this same villain  
to heaven. Oh, this is wages and reward, not revenge.  
He caught my father gross, full-fed,  
with all his sins in full bloom, fresh as May;  
and how his account stands, who knows but heaven?  
But by our understanding and our reckoning,  
it goes hard with him. And am I then revenged,  
to take him in the cleansing of his soul,  
when he is fit and ready for his passage? No.  
Up, sword, and wait to strike a more horrid blow:  
when he is drunk asleep, or in his rage,  
or in the incestuous pleasure of his bed,  
at gambling, swearing, or about some act  
that has no taste of salvation in it—  
then trip him, so his heels may kick at heaven,  
and his soul may be as damned and black  
as hell, where it is going. My mother waits.  
This medicine only prolongs your sickly days.

[*Exit.*]

The King rises and advances.

KING  
My words fly up, my thoughts remain below.  
Words without thoughts never to heaven go.

[*Exit.*]

SCENE IV. Another room in the Castle.

Enter Queen and Polonius.

POLONIUS  
He will come straight. Be sure you press him hard;  
tell him his pranks have been too gross to bear,  
and that your Grace has screened and stood between  
much heat and him. I'll keep silent right here.  
Pray you be blunt with him.

HAMLET  
[*Within.*] Mother, mother, mother.

QUEEN  
I'll warrant you; have no fear of me.  
Withdraw, I hear him coming.

[*Polonius goes behind the arras.*]

Enter Hamlet.

HAMLET  
Now, mother, what's the matter?

QUEEN  
Hamlet, you have your father much offended.

HAMLET  
Mother, you have my father much offended.

QUEEN  
Come, come, you answer with an idle tongue.

HAMLET  
Go, go, you question with a wicked tongue.

QUEEN  
Why, how now, Hamlet?

HAMLET  
What's the matter now?

QUEEN  
Have you forgotten me?

HAMLET  
No, by the holy cross, not so.  
You are the Queen, your husband's brother's wife;  
and—would it were not so—you are my mother.

QUEEN  
Nay, then I'll set those on you that can speak.

HAMLET  
Come, come, and sit you down; you shall not budge.  
You go not till I set you up a mirror  
where you may see the inmost part of you.

QUEEN  
What will you do? You will not murder me?  
Help, help, ho!

POLONIUS  
[*Behind.*] What, ho! Help, help, help!

HAMLET  
How now? A rat? [*Draws.*]  
Dead, for a ducat, dead!

[*Makes a pass through the arras.*]

POLONIUS  
[*Behind.*] Oh, I am slain!

[*Falls and dies.*]

QUEEN  
O me, what have you done?

HAMLET  
Nay, I don't know. Is it the King?

[*Draws forth Polonius.*]

QUEEN  
Oh, what a rash and bloody deed is this!

HAMLET  
A bloody deed. Almost as bad, good mother,  
as kill a king, and marry with his brother.

QUEEN  
As kill a king?

HAMLET  
Ay, lady, that was my word.—  
[*To Polonius.*] You wretched, rash, intruding fool, farewell!  
I took you for your better. Take your fortune;  
you find that being too busy is some danger.—  
Leave wringing of your hands. Peace, sit you down,  
and let me wring your heart; for so I shall,  
if it be made of penetrable stuff,  
if damned habit has not brazed it over  
so that it's proof and bulwark against all feeling.

QUEEN  
What have I done, that you dare wag your tongue  
in sound so rude against me?

HAMLET  
Such an act  
that blurs the grace and blush of modesty,  
calls virtue a hypocrite, takes the rose  
from the fair forehead of an innocent love  
and sets a blister there; makes marriage vows  
as false as gamblers' oaths. Oh, such a deed  
as plucks the very soul out of the body  
of the marriage bond, and makes sweet religion  
a mere rhapsody of words. Heaven's face glows red;  
yes, this solid world, this mass of matter,  
with a face of grief, as though Judgment Day were near,  
is sick at heart at the act.

QUEEN  
Ah me, what act,  
that roars so loud, and thunders even in the prologue?

HAMLET  
Look here upon this picture, and on this,  
the painted likeness of two brothers.  
See what a grace was seated on this brow:  
Hyperion's curls, the very forehead of Jove,  
an eye like Mars, to threaten and command,  
a bearing like the herald Mercury  
newly alighted on a heaven-kissing hill—  
a combination and a form indeed  
where every god did seem to set his seal,  
to give the world assurance of a man.  
This was your husband. Look you now what follows.  
Here is your husband, like a mildewed ear of grain,  
blighting his wholesome brother. Have you eyes?  
Could you stop feeding on this fair mountain,  
and gorge yourself on this moor? Ha! Have you eyes?  
You cannot call it love; for at your age  
the heyday in the blood is tame, it's humble,  
and waits upon the judgment. And what judgment  
would step from this to this? You have senses, surely,  
or you could not move; but surely that sense  
is paralyzed, for madness would not so err,  
nor were the senses ever so enslaved by frenzy  
but they kept back some measure of choice  
to tell so plain a difference. What devil was it  
that tricked you so at blind man's bluff?  
Eyes without feeling, feeling without sight,  
ears without hands or eyes, smell without all,  
or but a sickly part of one true sense,  
could not so blunder. O shame! Where is your blush?  
Rebellious hell,  
if you can mutiny in a matron's bones,  
then to flaming youth let virtue be as wax  
and melt in its own fire. Cry no shame  
when the driving heat gives the command,  
since frost itself burns just as actively,  
and reason acts as pimp to desire.

QUEEN  
O Hamlet, speak no more.  
You turn my eyes into my very soul,  
and there I see such black and ingrained spots  
as will not lose their stain.

HAMLET  
Nay, but to live  
in the rank sweat of a greasy bed,  
stewed in corruption, honeying and making love  
over the filthy sty—

QUEEN  
Oh, speak to me no more!  
These words like daggers enter in my ears.  
No more, sweet Hamlet.

HAMLET  
A murderer and a villain;  
a slave that is not one-twentieth the tenth part  
of your former lord; a clown of a king;  
a cutpurse of the empire and the rule,  
who from a shelf the precious crown stole  
and put it in his pocket—

QUEEN  
No more!

HAMLET  
A king of shreds and patches!—

Enter Ghost.

HAMLET  
Save me, and hover over me with your wings,  
you heavenly guards! What would your gracious figure?

QUEEN  
Alas, he's mad.

HAMLET  
Do you not come to chide your tardy son,  
that, letting time and passion slip, lets go  
the urgent acting of your dread command?  
Oh, say!

GHOST  
Do not forget. This visitation  
is but to whet your almost blunted purpose.  
But look, amazement sits upon your mother.  
Oh, step between her and her struggling soul.  
Imagination works strongest in the weakest bodies.  
Speak to her, Hamlet.

HAMLET  
How is it with you, lady?

QUEEN  
Alas, how is it with you,  
that you fix your eye on empty air,  
and hold conversation with the bodiless wind?  
Out at your eyes your spirits wildly peer,  
and, like sleeping soldiers roused by the alarm,  
your lying hair, as if it had life of its own,  
starts up and stands on end. O gentle son,  
upon the heat and flame of your distemper  
sprinkle cool patience. What is it you look at?

HAMLET  
On him, on him! Look how pale he glares.  
His form and his cause joined, preaching to stones,  
would make them feel.—Do not look upon me,  
lest with this piteous sight you turn me  
from my stern purpose. Then what I have to do  
will lose its true color: tears, perhaps, for blood.

QUEEN  
To whom do you say this?

HAMLET  
Do you see nothing there?

QUEEN  
Nothing at all; yet all that's there, I see.

HAMLET  
Nor did you hear anything?

QUEEN  
No, nothing but ourselves.

HAMLET  
Why, look you there! Look how it steals away!  
My father, in the very clothes he wore alive!  
Look, where he goes, even now, out at the door!

[*Exit Ghost.*]

QUEEN  
This is the very invention of your brain.  
This bodiless creation madness  
is very skilled at making.

HAMLET  
Madness!  
My pulse, like yours, keeps temperate time,  
and makes as healthful music. It is not madness  
that I have spoken. Put me to the test,  
and I will say the whole matter over again,  
which madness would skip from. Mother, for the love of grace,  
lay not that flattering unction on your soul,  
that it's my madness, not your trespass, speaking.  
It will only skin and film the ulcerous place,  
while rank corruption, mining all within,  
infects unseen. Confess yourself to heaven;  
repent what's past; avoid what is to come;  
and do not spread the compost on the weeds  
to make them ranker. Forgive me this, my virtue;  
for in the fatness of these bloated times  
virtue itself must beg pardon of vice,  
yes, bow and court it for leave to do it good.

QUEEN  
O Hamlet, you have cleft my heart in two.

HAMLET  
Oh, throw away the worser part of it,  
and live the purer with the other half.  
Good night. But do not go to my uncle's bed.  
Assume a virtue, if you have it not.  
That monster, custom, who devours all sense  
of evil habits, is yet an angel in this:  
that to the practice of fair and good actions  
he likewise gives a coat, or uniform,  
that is easily put on. Refrain tonight,  
and that shall lend a kind of easiness  
to the next abstinence; the next still easier;  
for habit can almost change the very stamp of nature,  
and either curb the devil, or throw him out  
with wondrous force. Once more, good night;  
and when you are willing to be blessed,  
I'll beg a blessing of you.—For this same lord,  
[*Pointing to Polonius.*]  
I do repent; but heaven has so pleased,  
to punish me with this, and this with me,  
that I must be their scourge and minister.  
I will dispose of him, and will answer well  
the death I gave him. So, again, good night.  
I must be cruel, only to be kind.  
Thus bad begins, and worse remains behind.  
One word more, good lady.

QUEEN  
What shall I do?

HAMLET  
Not this, by any means, that I tell you to do:  
let the bloated King tempt you again to bed;  
pinch your cheek wantonly; call you his mouse;  
and let him, for a pair of filthy kisses,  
or paddling in your neck with his damned fingers,  
make you unravel the whole matter out—  
that I am not essentially in madness,  
but mad by craft. It would be good to let him know;  
for who that's but a queen, fair, sober, wise,  
would from a toad, from a bat, a tomcat,  
hide such weighty matters? Who would do so?  
No, in spite of sense and secrecy,  
unlatch the basket on the housetop,  
let the birds fly, and like the famous ape,  
to test the outcome, creep into the basket  
and break your own neck in the fall.

QUEEN  
Be assured, if words are made of breath,  
and breath of life, I have no life to breathe  
what you have said to me.

HAMLET  
I must go to England; you know that?

QUEEN  
Alack,  
I had forgotten. It's so decided.

HAMLET  
There are letters sealed; and my two schoolfellows—  
whom I will trust as I would adders fanged—  
they carry the orders; they must clear my way  
and usher me toward some trap. Let it work;  
for it's the sport to have the engineer  
hoist with his own petard; and it shall go hard  
but I will dig one yard below their mines  
and blow them at the moon. Oh, it's most sweet  
when in one line two plots directly meet.  
This man shall set me on my way.  
I'll lug the guts into the neighboring room.  
Mother, good night. Indeed, this counsellor  
is now most still, most secret, and most grave,  
who was in life a foolish, prating knave.  
Come, sir, to make an end with you.  
Good night, mother.

[*Exit Hamlet dragging out Polonius.*]
