Lectio Edition · 2026

Hamlet

William Shakespeare

  • 1600–1601
  • Western tradition
  • Drama
  • Lectio Edition 2026

Shakespeare's tragedy of a prince, a murdered father, and a mind that cannot stop thinking. Five acts, written about 1600. More of its lines have passed into ordinary speech than those of any other play.

Why this editionGoal and purpose

With Shakespeare the English is the art. So this edition does very little.

Hamlet is the one work here that was written in English. There is no original behind it to get closer to. A full rewrite would throw away the thing itself.

But Early Modern English stops many readers. Words have died or changed their meaning. "Fardels" are burdens. "Fancy" meant imagination. Some clauses run backward.

The Lectio Edition keeps the verse lines and nearly all of Shakespeare's words. It steps in only where the language itself blocks a modern reader: a dead word replaced, an inverted clause set straight. It is a way in, to be read beside the original and then left behind.

Where it stands Shakespeare's own text is what opens by default for Hamlet, and it will stay the default. The Lectio Edition is a companion, one tap away.
The openingFrom the Lectio Edition

How it begins.

Act I

SCENE I. Elsinore. A platform before the Castle.

Enter Francisco and Barnardo, two sentinels.

BARNARDO
Who's there?

FRANCISCO
No—you answer me. Stand, and reveal yourself.

BARNARDO
Long live the King!

FRANCISCO
Barnardo?

BARNARDO
The same.

FRANCISCO
You come right on the stroke of your hour.

BARNARDO
It's just struck twelve. Get to bed, Francisco.

FRANCISCO
For this relief, many thanks. It's bitter cold,
and I am sick at heart.

BARNARDO
Have you had a quiet watch?

FRANCISCO
Not a mouse stirring.

BARNARDO
Well, good night.
If you do meet Horatio and Marcellus,
the partners of my watch, bid them make haste.

Enter Horatio and Marcellus.

FRANCISCO
I think I hear them. Stand, there! Who's that?

HORATIO
Friends to this country.

MARCELLUS
And loyal men to the Dane.

FRANCISCO
Good night to you.

MARCELLUS
Oh, farewell, honest soldier. Who has relieved you?

FRANCISCO
Barnardo has my place. Good night to you.

The first 150 words of the Lectio Edition, as they stand in the bundle the app reads.

The choicesPolicies and divergences

What this edition decided.

The first draft changed too much. Most of the review's 147 fixes put Shakespeare's wording back.

  • The verse lines are kept, line for line. Prose stays prose.
  • "Thou" and "thee" become "you", except in a short closed list of lines known by heart: "to thine own self be true", "Get thee to a nunnery", "flights of angels sing thee to thy rest".
  • "Ay" is kept. Every reader takes it as "aye".
  • Claudius keeps the royal "we" wherever Shakespeare gives it to him.
  • A rare word the scene itself discusses is kept. Hamlet and Polonius stop to talk about "mobled", so it stays.
  • Songs, the letter, the Players' speeches and the play within the play keep their own setting.
  • The famous lines are not touched.

Where it differs from the older translations

The edition records 12 divergences: places where it departs from the older English, each with its reason. Here are 7. The full record is in the app, one tap from the text.

"Buzz, buzz"

Act II, scene 2

Older
Buzz, buzz. (Shakespeare)
Lectio
Bah, bah.

Hamlet's answer to stale news. "Buzz" was a noise of contempt. Today it reads as a sound effect.

"the mobled queen"

Act II, scene 2

Older
the mobled queen (Shakespeare)
Lectio
the mobled queen

Kept. The draft had "muffled". But Hamlet stops on the word and Polonius approves of it, and with an ordinary word their exchange is about nothing. The review put it back.

"his quietus make / With a bare bodkin"

Act III, scene 1

Older
his quietus make / With a bare bodkin (Shakespeare)
Lectio
settle his account

"To be, or not to be" is kept almost word for word. Four dead words are cleared, each in the meter. A bodkin is a dagger and fardels are burdens.

"methinks"

Act III, scene 2

Older
The lady protests too much, methinks. (Shakespeare)
Lectio
The lady protests too much, methinks.

Kept word for word. The draft had removed "methinks". Every reader still understands it, and the line is known with it.

"argal" and "se offendendo"

Act V, scene 1

Older
argal, she drowned herself wittingly (Shakespeare)
Lectio
she drowned herself on purpose

The gravedigger's law Latin is wrong on purpose, and that is the joke. His blunders are kept. Only the plain words around them are eased.

"of most excellent fancy"

Act V, scene 1

Older
a fellow of infinite jest, of most excellent fancy (Shakespeare)
Lectio
of most excellent imagination

"Fancy" then meant the power of imagination. Now it means a whim. "Infinite jest" is untouched.

"The rest is silence"

Act V, scene 2

Older
The rest is silence. (Shakespeare)
Lectio
The rest is silence.

Hamlet's last words, left exactly as written. So are "the readiness is all" and "there's a divinity that shapes our ends".

How it was madeProvenance and review

The record for this edition.

Every Lectio edition says how it was made. This one is a beta. It was drafted by an AI model, read against its sources by a second AI reader, and corrected. The method is described here.

Still open

What the first review left for the editor to decide or restore.

  • The editor's own read of all five acts, beside the original.
  • The letter, Hamlet's poem, the Players' speeches and the songs should be set in italics as in the source. Act II still has them in quotation marks and Act IV's songs have lost their indent.
  • A handful of "O" and "Oh" exclamations were left for the editor's ear.
  • Two of the edition's notes quote the text with slightly different punctuation from how it reads.
