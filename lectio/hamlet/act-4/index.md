# Hamlet, Act IV

SCENE I. A room in the Castle.

Enter King, Queen, Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.

KING  
There's meaning in these sighs. These heavy heaves  
you must explain; it's right that we should understand them.  
Where is your son?

QUEEN  
Leave us alone here for a little while.

[*To Rosencrantz and Guildenstern, who go out.*]

Ah, my good lord, what have I seen tonight!

KING  
What, Gertrude? How is Hamlet?

QUEEN  
Mad as the sea and wind, when both of them strive  
over which is the stronger. In his lawless fit,  
hearing something stir behind the tapestry,  
he whips out his rapier, cries 'A rat, a rat!'  
and in that frenzied notion kills  
the unseen good old man.

KING  
Oh, terrible deed!  
It would have been so with us, had we been there.  
His freedom is full of threats to us all—  
to you yourself, to us, to everyone.  
Alas, how shall this bloody deed be answered for?  
It will be laid on us, whose foresight  
should have kept this mad young man on a short rein,  
restrained, and out of the way. But our love was so great  
we would not face what was the wisest course,  
but, like the owner of a foul disease  
who keeps it from being known, let it feed  
even on the very pith of life. Where has he gone?

QUEEN  
To drag away the body he has killed—  
over which his very madness, like a vein of gold  
running through a seam of baser metals,  
shows itself pure. He weeps for what he's done.

KING  
O Gertrude, come away!  
The sun shall no sooner touch the mountains  
than we'll ship him out of here; and this vile deed  
we must, with all our majesty and skill,  
both sanction and excuse.—Ho, Guildenstern!

Re-enter Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.

Friends, both of you, go get some further help:  
Hamlet in his madness has slain Polonius,  
and from his mother's chamber he has dragged him.  
Go seek him out, speak gently, and bring the body  
into the chapel. Please, be quick about this.

[*Exeunt Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.*]

Come, Gertrude, we'll call up our wisest friends,  
and let them know both what we mean to do  
and what's been done before its time—so that slander,  
whose whisper carries across the world's full breadth,  
as straight as the cannon to its mark,  
carrying its poisoned shot, may miss our name  
and strike the wound-proof air instead. Oh, come away!  
My soul is full of discord and dismay.

[*Exeunt.*]

SCENE II. Another room in the Castle.

Enter Hamlet.

HAMLET  
Safely stowed.

ROSENCRANTZ and GUILDENSTERN  
[*Within.*] Hamlet! Lord Hamlet!

HAMLET  
What noise? Who's calling for Hamlet? Oh, here they come.

Enter Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.

ROSENCRANTZ  
What have you done, my lord, with the dead body?

HAMLET  
Mixed it back into the dust it's kin to.

ROSENCRANTZ  
Tell us where it is, so that we can take it from there  
and carry it to the chapel.

HAMLET  
Don't count on it.

ROSENCRANTZ  
Count on what?

HAMLET  
That I can keep your secrets and not my own. Besides, when a sponge puts the question to me—what answer should be given by the son of a king?

ROSENCRANTZ  
Do you take me for a sponge, my lord?

HAMLET  
Ay, sir—one that soaks up the King's favor, his rewards, his powers. But officers like that serve the King best in the end: he keeps them, like an ape keeps food, in the corner of his jaw—first into the mouth, last to be swallowed. When he needs what you've gathered up, it's only a matter of squeezing you, and then, sponge, you'll be dry again.

ROSENCRANTZ  
I don't understand you, my lord.

HAMLET  
I'm glad of it. A sly speech sleeps unheard in a fool's ear.

ROSENCRANTZ  
My lord, you must tell us where the body is and come with us to the King.

HAMLET  
The body is with the King, but the King is not with the body. The King is a thing—

GUILDENSTERN  
A thing, my lord!

HAMLET  
Of nothing. Bring me to him. Hide fox, and all after.

[*Exeunt.*]

SCENE III. Another room in the Castle.

Enter King, attended.

KING  
I've sent men to seek him out and to find the body.  
How dangerous it is that this man goes free!  
Yet we must not bring the full force of law down on him:  
he's loved by the bewildered crowd,  
who choose not by their judgment but their eyes;  
and where that's so, it's the offender's punishment they weigh,  
and never the offense. To keep it all smooth and even,  
this sudden sending-away of his must look like  
a considered decision. Desperate diseases  
are relieved by desperate remedies—  
or not at all.

Enter Rosencrantz.

Well? What's happened?

ROSENCRANTZ  
Where the dead body's been stowed, my lord,  
we cannot get out of him.

KING  
But where is he?

ROSENCRANTZ  
Outside, my lord, under guard, awaiting your pleasure.

KING  
Bring him before us.

ROSENCRANTZ  
Ho, Guildenstern! Bring in my lord.

Enter Hamlet and Guildenstern.

KING  
Now, Hamlet, where's Polonius?

HAMLET  
At supper.

KING  
At supper? Where?

HAMLET  
Not where he eats, but where he's being eaten. A certain assembly of statesmanlike worms is right now at work on him. Your worm is your only true emperor when it comes to diet. We fatten all other creatures to fatten ourselves, and we fatten ourselves for maggots. Your fat king and your lean beggar are just two courses—two dishes, but to one table. That's the end of it.

KING  
Alas, alas!

HAMLET  
A man may fish with a worm that has eaten of a king, and eat of the fish that has fed on that worm.

KING  
What do you mean by this?

HAMLET  
Nothing—only to show you how a king may go on a royal progress through the guts of a beggar.

KING  
Where is Polonius?

HAMLET  
In heaven. Send someone there to see. If your messenger doesn't find him there, look for him in the other place yourself. But really, if you don't find him within the month, you'll smell him as you go up the stairs into the lobby.

KING  
[*To some Attendants.*] Go seek him there.

HAMLET  
He'll wait till you come.

[*Exeunt Attendants.*]

KING  
Hamlet, this deed—for your own special safety,  
which we hold dear, even as we deeply grieve  
for what you've done—must send you away from here  
with fiery speed. So get yourself ready;  
the ship is waiting, and the wind stands fair,  
your companions are ready, and everything is set  
for England.

HAMLET  
For England?

KING  
Ay, Hamlet.

HAMLET  
Good.

KING  
So it is, if you knew our purposes.

HAMLET  
I see a cherub that sees them. But—come; off to England! Farewell, dear mother.

KING  
Your loving father, Hamlet.

HAMLET  
My mother. Father and mother are man and wife; man and wife are one flesh; and so—my mother. Come, off to England.

[*Exit.*]

KING  
Follow him close. Lure him aboard with speed;  
don't delay it; I'll have him gone tonight.  
Away—everything else that hangs on this affair  
is sealed and done. Please, make haste.

[*Exeunt Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.*]

And England—if you value my friendship at all  
(and my great power may well make you feel it,  
since your scar still looks raw and red  
after the Danish sword, and your free homage  
pays its respects to us)—you may not coldly slight  
our sovereign command, which fully demands,  
by letters pressing to that effect,  
the immediate death of Hamlet. Do it, England;  
for he rages in my blood like a fever,  
and you must cure me. Until I know it's done,  
whatever my fortunes, my joy has not begun.

[*Exit.*]

SCENE IV. A plain in Denmark.

Enter Fortinbras and Forces marching.

FORTINBRAS  
Go, Captain, and greet the Danish king for me.  
Tell him that, by his leave, Fortinbras  
requests the promised right of passage  
across his kingdom. You know the meeting place.  
If his Majesty would have anything of us,  
we'll pay our respects in person before him;  
and let him know as much.

CAPTAIN  
I'll do it, my lord.

FORTINBRAS  
Move on, gently.

[*Exeunt all but the Captain.*]

Enter Hamlet, Rosencrantz, Guildenstern &c.

HAMLET  
Good sir, whose forces are these?

CAPTAIN  
They're Norway's, sir.

HAMLET  
What's their purpose, sir, may I ask?

CAPTAIN  
Against some part of Poland.

HAMLET  
Who commands them, sir?

CAPTAIN  
The nephew of old Norway, Fortinbras.

HAMLET  
Is it against the heart of Poland, sir,  
or only some border region?

CAPTAIN  
To speak truly, and with nothing added,  
we go to win a little patch of ground  
that holds no profit in it but the name.  
I wouldn't rent it for five ducats—five;  
nor would it bring to Norway or the Pole  
a better price, if it were sold outright.

HAMLET  
Why, then the Pole will never bother to defend it.

CAPTAIN  
Yes—it's already garrisoned.

HAMLET  
Two thousand souls and twenty thousand ducats  
won't settle this quarrel over a straw!  
This is the abscess of too much wealth and peace,  
that breaks inward, and shows no outward cause  
for why the man dies. I humbly thank you, sir.

CAPTAIN  
God be with you, sir.

[*Exit.*]

ROSENCRANTZ  
Will it please you to go, my lord?

HAMLET  
I'll be right with you. Go on a little ahead.

[*Exeunt all but Hamlet.*]

How all occasions do inform against me,  
and spur my dull revenge. What is a man,  
if the best use and profit of his time  
is only to sleep and feed? A beast, no more.  
Surely he that made us with such broad reason,  
able to look before and after, did not give us  
that capability and godlike reason  
to let it mold in us, unused. Now whether it's  
beastlike forgetfulness, or some cowardly scruple  
of thinking too precisely on the outcome—  
a thought which, split in four, has one part wisdom  
and ever three parts coward—I do not know  
why I still live to say 'this thing's to do,'  
since I have cause, and will, and strength, and means  
to do it. Examples as plain as the earth urge me on:  
witness this army of such size and cost,  
led by a delicate and tender prince,  
whose spirit, swelled with godlike ambition,  
makes faces at the unknown outcome,  
exposing all that's mortal and unsure  
to everything that fortune, death, and danger dare,  
and all for an eggshell. To be truly great  
is not to stir without great cause,  
but greatly to find quarrel in a straw  
when honor's at the stake. Where do I stand, then—  
I, who have a father killed, a mother stained,  
spurs to my reason and my blood,  
and let it all sleep, while to my shame I see  
the imminent death of twenty thousand men  
who, for a fantasy and trick of fame,  
go to their graves like beds, fight for a plot  
on which the numbers cannot even contest the cause,  
which is not tomb enough, nor ground enough,  
to hide the slain? Oh, from this time forth,  
let my thoughts be bloody, or be nothing worth!

[*Exit.*]

SCENE V. Elsinore. A room in the Castle.

Enter Queen, Horatio and a Gentleman.

QUEEN  
I will not speak with her.

GENTLEMAN  
She insists, and indeed she's out of her mind.  
Her state can't help but stir your pity.

QUEEN  
What does she want?

GENTLEMAN  
She speaks much of her father; says she hears  
there are tricks in the world, and clears her throat, and beats her breast,  
takes spiteful offense at trifles, says things in riddles  
that make only half-sense. Her speech is nothing,  
yet the disordered use of it does move  
her hearers to draw their own conclusions; they guess at it,  
and patch her words together to fit their own thoughts—  
which, by her winks and nods and gestures,  
really would make one think there might be meaning there,  
nothing certain, yet much, and unhappily.  
It would be good if she were spoken with, for she may scatter  
dangerous suspicions in ill-meaning minds.

QUEEN  
Let her come in.

[*Exit Gentleman.*]

To my sick soul (as is the very nature of sin),  
each trifle seems the prologue to some great disaster.  
Guilt is so full of clumsy suspicion  
that it spills itself in the fear of being spilled.

Enter Ophelia.

OPHELIA  
Where is the beautiful Majesty of Denmark?

QUEEN  
How now, Ophelia?

OPHELIA  
[*Sings.*]  
How should I your true love know  
from any other one?  
By his cockle hat and staff  
and his sandal shoes.

QUEEN  
Alas, sweet lady, what does this song mean?

OPHELIA  
What's that? No—please, listen.  
[*Sings.*]  
He is dead and gone, lady,  
he is dead and gone;  
at his head a grass-green turf,  
at his heels a stone.

QUEEN  
No, but Ophelia—

OPHELIA  
Please, listen.  
[*Sings.*]  
White his shroud as the mountain snow—

Enter King.

QUEEN  
Alas, look here, my lord!

OPHELIA  
[*Sings.*]  
strewn all over with sweet flowers;  
which did not go to the grave bewept  
with true-love's showers.

KING  
How are you, pretty lady?

OPHELIA  
Well, God reward you! They say the owl was a baker's daughter. Lord, we know what we are, but know not what we may be. God be at your table!

KING  
Her mind is fixed on her father.

OPHELIA  
Please, let's have no talk of this; but when they ask you what it means, say this:  
[*Sings.*]  
Tomorrow is Saint Valentine's day,  
all in the morning betime,  
and I a maid at your window,  
to be your Valentine.

Then up he rose and put on his clothes,  
and opened the chamber door,  
let in the maid, that out a maid  
never departed more.

KING  
Pretty Ophelia!

OPHELIA  
Indeed, without an oath, I'll make an end of it.  
[*Sings.*]  
By Jesus and by Saint Charity,  
alas, and for shame!  
Young men will do it if they come to it;  
by Cock, they are to blame.

She said, before you tumbled me,  
you promised me to wed.  
So would I have done, by yonder sun,  
if you'd not come to my bed.

KING  
How long has she been like this?

OPHELIA  
I hope all will be well. We must be patient. But I can't help weeping, to think they would lay him in the cold ground. My brother shall know of it. And so I thank you for your good advice. Come, my coach! Good night, ladies; good night, sweet ladies; good night, good night.

[*Exit.*]

KING  
Follow her close; keep good watch on her, I beg you.

[*Exit Horatio.*]

Oh, this is the poison of deep grief; it springs  
entirely from her father's death. O Gertrude, Gertrude,  
when sorrows come, they come not single spies,  
but in battalions. First, her father slain;  
next, your son gone—and he the most violent cause  
of his own just removal; the people stirred up,  
muddied, and unwholesome in their thoughts and whispers  
about good Polonius's death. And we have acted rashly  
in burying him in such secret haste. Poor Ophelia,  
divided from herself and her sound judgment,  
without which we are mere pictures, or mere beasts.  
Last, and weighing as much as all of these,  
her brother has come secretly back from France,  
feeds on his amazement, keeps himself in shadow,  
and has no shortage of gossips to infect his ear  
with poisonous talk about his father's death—  
in which gossip, starved for facts,  
won't scruple to accuse our person,  
whispering ear to ear. O my dear Gertrude, all this,  
like grapeshot from a cannon, in many places  
deals me more deaths than I need.

[*A noise within.*]

QUEEN  
Alas, what noise is this?

KING  
Where are my Swiss guards? Let them guard the door.

Enter a Gentleman.

What's the matter?

GENTLEMAN  
Save yourself, my lord.  
The ocean, overreaching its shoreline,  
doesn't devour the flatlands with more violent haste  
than young Laertes, at the head of a riot,  
overpowers your officers. The rabble call him lord;  
and, as if the world were only now beginning,  
the past forgotten, custom unknown  
(the things that confirm and prop up every word),  
they cry 'Let us choose! Laertes shall be king!'  
Caps, hands, and tongues applaud it to the clouds:  
'Laertes shall be king, Laertes king!'

QUEEN  
How cheerfully they bay on the false trail!  
Oh, you're running backward, you false Danish dogs!

[*A noise within.*]

KING  
The doors are broken in.

Enter Laertes, armed; Danes following.

LAERTES  
Where is this king?—Sirs, all of you, stay outside.

DANES  
No, let's come in.

LAERTES  
I beg you, give me leave.

DANES  
We will, we will.

[*They retire without the door.*]

LAERTES  
I thank you. Guard the door. O you vile king,  
give me my father!

QUEEN  
Calmly, good Laertes.

LAERTES  
That drop of blood that's calm proclaims me bastard,  
cries 'cuckold' to my father, brands 'harlot'  
even here, between the chaste, unstained brow  
of my true mother.

KING  
What's the reason, Laertes,  
that your rebellion looks so monstrous?—  
Let him go, Gertrude. Do not fear for our person.  
There is such divinity that hedges a king  
that treason can only peep at what it wants,  
and acts little of its will.—Tell me, Laertes,  
why you're so enraged.—Let him go, Gertrude.—  
Speak, man.

LAERTES  
Where is my father?

KING  
Dead.

QUEEN  
But not by his hand.

KING  
Let him ask his fill.

LAERTES  
How did he come to die? I won't be played with.  
To hell with allegiance! Vows, to the blackest devil!  
Conscience and grace, down to the deepest pit!  
I dare damnation. Here's where I take my stand:  
that both worlds, this and the next, I count as nothing—  
let come what comes; I'll only be revenged,  
and most thoroughly, for my father.

KING  
Who shall stop you?

LAERTES  
My own will—not all the world.  
And as for my means, I'll manage them so well  
that they'll go far with little.

KING  
Good Laertes,  
if you want to know the truth  
of your dear father's death—is it written in your revenge  
that you'll sweep the board clean, drawing in friend and foe,  
winner and loser alike?

LAERTES  
None but his enemies.

KING  
Will you know them, then?

LAERTES  
To his good friends I'll open my arms this wide;  
and, like the loving, life-giving pelican,  
feed them with my own blood.

KING  
Why, now you speak  
like a good son and a true gentleman.  
That I am guiltless of your father's death,  
and grieve for it most deeply,  
shall come as clear to your judgment  
as daylight to your eye.

DANES  
[*Within.*] Let her come in.

LAERTES  
What now! What noise is that?

Re-enter Ophelia, fantastically dressed with straws and flowers.

O heat, dry up my brains! Tears seven times salt,  
burn out the sight and power of my eyes!  
By heaven, your madness shall be repaid by weight,  
till our scale tips the beam. O rose of May!  
Dear maid, kind sister, sweet Ophelia!  
O heavens, is it possible a young girl's wits  
should be as mortal as an old man's life?  
Nature is refined in love, and where it's refined,  
it sends some precious part of itself  
after the thing it loves.

OPHELIA  
[*Sings.*]  
They bore him barefaced on the bier,  
hey non nonny, nonny, hey nonny,  
and on his grave rained many a tear—  
Fare you well, my dove!

LAERTES  
If you had your wits, and were urging me to revenge,  
it could not move me more than this.

OPHELIA  
You must sing 'Down a-down,' and you call him 'a-down-a.' Oh, how the spinning-wheel suits it! It's the false steward, the one who stole his master's daughter.

LAERTES  
This nothing's more than sense.

OPHELIA  
There's rosemary, that's for remembrance; please, love, remember. And there are pansies, that's for thoughts.

LAERTES  
A lesson in madness—thoughts and remembrance, perfectly matched.

OPHELIA  
There's fennel for you, and columbines. There's rue for you, and here's some for me; we may call it herb of grace on Sundays. Oh, you must wear your rue with a difference. There's a daisy. I would give you some violets, but they all withered when my father died. They say he made a good end.  
[*Sings.*]  
For bonny sweet Robin is all my joy.

LAERTES  
Thought and affliction, suffering, hell itself—  
she turns it all to charm and prettiness.

OPHELIA  
[*Sings.*]  
And will he not come again?  
And will he not come again?  
No, no, he is dead;  
go to your death-bed;  
he never will come again.

His beard was as white as snow,  
all flaxen was his head.  
He is gone, he is gone,  
and we throw away our grief;  
God have mercy on his soul.

And on all Christian souls, I pray God. God be with you.

[*Exit.*]

LAERTES  
Do you see this, oh God?

KING  
Laertes, I must share in your grief,  
or you deny me my right. Go now and step aside;  
choose whichever of your wisest friends you will,  
and let them hear and judge between you and me.  
If, directly or by some indirect hand,  
they find us touched by this, we will give our kingdom,  
our crown, our life, and all that we call ours,  
to you, in payment. But if not,  
be content to lend your patience to us,  
and we shall work alongside your soul  
to give it the redress it deserves.

LAERTES  
Let it be so.  
His manner of death, his obscure burial—  
no trophy, no sword, no coat of arms over his bones,  
no noble rite, no formal ceremony—  
cry out to be heard, as if from heaven to earth,  
that I must call it into question.

KING  
And so you shall.  
And where the guilt lies, let the great axe fall.  
Please, come with me.

[*Exeunt.*]

SCENE VI. Another room in the Castle.

Enter Horatio and a Servant.

HORATIO  
Who are these men that want to speak with me?

SERVANT  
Sailors, sir. They say they have letters for you.

HORATIO  
Let them come in.

[*Exit Servant.*]

I don't know from what part of the world  
I'd be sent word, if not from Lord Hamlet.

Enter Sailors.

FIRST SAILOR  
God bless you, sir.

HORATIO  
Let him bless you too.

FIRST SAILOR  
He will, sir, if it please him. There's a letter for you, sir—it comes from the ambassador who was bound for England, if your name is Horatio, as I'm given to know it is.

HORATIO  
[*Reads.*] 'Horatio, when you've looked this over, give these fellows some way of reaching the King. They have letters for him. Before we were two days out at sea, a pirate ship, very heavily armed, gave us chase. Finding ourselves too slow to outsail them, we put on a forced courage, and in the grappling I boarded them. In that instant they got clear of our ship, so I alone became their prisoner. They have treated me like merciful thieves; but they knew what they were doing—I'm to do them a good turn in return. Let the King have the letters I've sent, and come to me with as much haste as you'd flee death. I have words to speak in your ear that will strike you dumb; and yet they're far too small for the size of the matter. These good fellows will bring you to where I am. Rosencrantz and Guildenstern hold their course for England: I have much to tell you about them. Farewell.  
He whom you know to be yours,  
HAMLET.'

Come, I'll find a way for you to deliver these letters of yours,  
and do it all the faster, so you can lead me  
to the man you brought them from.

[*Exeunt.*]

SCENE VII. Another room in the Castle.

Enter King and Laertes.

KING  
Now your own conscience must seal my acquittal,  
and you must take me into your heart as a friend,  
since you have heard, and with an understanding ear,  
that the man who slew your noble father  
was after my life as well.

LAERTES  
It's plain enough. But tell me  
why you didn't move against these crimes—  
so wicked and so capital in their nature—  
when by your own safety, your wisdom, and everything else,  
you were so strongly driven to it.

KING  
Oh, for two special reasons,  
which may, perhaps, seem weak to you,  
but are strong to me. The Queen, his mother,  
lives almost on his looks alone; and as for myself—  
whether it's my virtue or my curse, be it which it may—  
she is so bound up with my life and soul  
that, as a star can't move except within its sphere,  
I can't move except through her. The other reason  
why I might not go to a public reckoning  
is the great love the common people bear him,  
who, dipping all his faults in their affection,  
would (like the spring that turns wood to stone)  
convert his fetters to graces; so that my arrows,  
too lightly made for so strong a wind,  
would have flown back to my own bow again,  
and not where I had aimed them.

LAERTES  
And so I've lost a noble father,  
and had a sister driven to desperate straits—  
whose worth, if praise may look back at what she was,  
stood as the challenger on the height of the whole age  
for her perfections. But my revenge will come.

KING  
Lose no sleep over that. You mustn't think  
that we are made of stuff so flat and dull  
that we can let danger pluck at our beard  
and take it for sport. You'll soon hear more.  
I loved your father, and we love ourself,  
and that, I hope, will help you to imagine—

Enter a Messenger.

Well? What news?

MESSENGER  
Letters, my lord, from Hamlet.  
This to your Majesty; this to the Queen.

KING  
From Hamlet! Who brought them?

MESSENGER  
Sailors, my lord, so they say; I didn't see them.  
They were handed to me by Claudio, who got them  
from the man who brought them in.

KING  
Laertes, you shall hear them.  
Leave us.

[*Exit Messenger.*]

[*Reads.*] 'High and mighty, you shall know that I am set down naked in your kingdom. Tomorrow I shall beg leave to see your kingly eyes; when I do, after first asking your pardon for it, I will recount the reasons for my sudden and most strange return.  
HAMLET.'

What can this mean? Have all the rest come back?  
Or is it some trick, and no such thing?

LAERTES  
Do you know the handwriting?

KING  
It's Hamlet's own hand. 'Naked!'  
And in a postscript here he says 'alone.'  
Can you advise me?

LAERTES  
I'm lost in it, my lord. But let him come;  
it warms the very sickness in my heart  
that I shall live to tell him to his face,  
'This is how you die.'

KING  
If it must be so, Laertes—  
and how else should it be? How otherwise?—  
will you be ruled by me?

LAERTES  
Ay, my lord—  
so long as you don't rule me toward peace.

KING  
Toward your own peace. If he's now come back,  
recoiling from his voyage, and means  
to undertake it no more, I'll work him  
into an exploit, now ripe in my mind,  
under which he can't help but fall;  
and for his death no breath of suspicion shall stir,  
but even his mother shall clear it of foul play  
and call it an accident.

LAERTES  
My lord, I'll be ruled—  
all the more if you can arrange it so  
that I might be the instrument.

KING  
It falls out right.  
You've been much talked of since your travels,  
and in Hamlet's hearing, for a skill  
in which they say you shine. The whole sum of your gifts  
did not draw such envy from him  
as that one did—and that, to my mind,  
the least worthy of any.

LAERTES  
What skill is that, my lord?

KING  
A mere ribbon in the cap of youth,  
yet needful too; for youth is no less suited  
by the light and careless dress it wears  
than settled age is by its furs and dark robes,  
which speak of health and gravity. Two months ago  
there was a gentleman of Normandy here—  
I've seen, and fought against, the French myself,  
and they ride well on horseback; but this gallant  
had witchcraft in it. He grew into his seat,  
and brought his horse to such wondrous feats  
as if he'd been made one body, sharing half his nature,  
with the brave beast. He so far exceeded my imagining  
that I, in dreaming up shapes and tricks,  
fall short of what he actually did.

LAERTES  
A Norman, was it?

KING  
A Norman.

LAERTES  
Upon my life, Lamord.

KING  
The very same.

LAERTES  
I know him well. He is indeed the jewel  
and the gem of the whole nation.

KING  
He spoke openly of you,  
and gave you such a masterful report  
for your art and skill in self-defense—  
and for your rapier most especially—  
that he cried out it would be a sight indeed  
if anyone could match you. The fencers of his nation,  
he swore, had neither footwork, guard, nor eye,  
if you opposed them. Sir, this report of his  
so envenomed Hamlet with envy  
that he could do nothing but wish and beg  
for your sudden return, to play a match with him.  
Now, out of this—

LAERTES  
What out of this, my lord?

KING  
Laertes, was your father dear to you?  
Or are you like the painting of a sorrow—  
a face without a heart?

LAERTES  
Why do you ask this?

KING  
Not that I think you didn't love your father;  
but I know that love is born in time,  
and I've seen, in cases that prove it,  
that time wears down the spark and fire of it.  
There lives within the very flame of love  
a kind of wick, or charred snuff, that will dim it;  
and nothing stays forever at one even goodness,  
for goodness, swelling to excess,  
dies of its own too-much. What we would do,  
we should do when we would; for this 'would' changes,  
and has as many slackenings and delays  
as there are tongues, and hands, and chance events;  
and then this 'should' is like a spendthrift's sigh  
that hurts by easing. But to the quick of the ulcer:  
Hamlet's coming back. What would you undertake  
to show yourself your father's son in deed,  
more than in words?

LAERTES  
To cut his throat in church.

KING  
No place, indeed, should give murder sanctuary;  
revenge should know no bounds. But, good Laertes,  
if you'll do this, keep close inside your room.  
When Hamlet's back, he'll learn you've come home;  
we'll set on those who'll praise your excellence,  
and lay a double polish on the fame  
the Frenchman gave you, finally bring you together  
and wager on your two heads. He, being careless,  
most generous, and free from all scheming,  
won't inspect the swords; so that with ease,  
or with a little sleight of hand, you may pick out  
a blade without a button, and in a treacherous thrust  
repay him for your father.

LAERTES  
I'll do it.  
And for that purpose I'll anoint my sword.  
I bought an ointment from a quack,  
so deadly that, just dip a knife in it,  
and wherever it draws blood, no poultice so rare,  
gathered from all the healing herbs  
that grow under the moon, can save from death  
the thing it merely scratches. I'll touch my point  
with this contagion, so that if I graze him at all,  
it may be death.

KING  
Let's think on this further—  
weigh what conveniences of time and means  
might best fit our purpose. If this should fail,  
and our intent show through a botched performance,  
it would be better not attempted at all.  
So this plan should have a backup, a second,  
that might hold if the first should fail in trial. Soft—let me think.  
We'll lay a solemn wager on your skill—  
I have it! When, in the bout, you're hot and dry  
(and make your passes more violent to that end),  
and he calls for a drink, I'll have prepared  
a cup for the occasion; just a sip of it—  
should he by chance escape your poisoned thrust—  
and our purpose will hold there.

Enter Queen.

How now, sweet Queen?

QUEEN  
One woe treads upon another's heel,  
they follow so fast. Your sister's drowned, Laertes.

LAERTES  
Drowned! Oh, where?

QUEEN  
There is a willow that leans over a brook,  
showing its silver-gray leaves in the glassy stream.  
There she came with fantastic garlands she had woven  
of crowflowers, nettles, daisies, and long purples—  
which coarse-mouthed shepherds give a ruder name,  
but our cold maidens call them dead men's fingers.  
There, on the drooping branches, climbing to hang  
her crown of weeds, an envious sliver broke,  
and down her weedy trophies and herself  
fell into the weeping brook. Her clothes spread wide,  
and, mermaid-like, for a while they bore her up;  
during which time she sang scraps of old tunes,  
as one incapable of her own distress,  
or like a creature born and bred  
to that element. But it could not last long  
before her garments, heavy with their drink,  
pulled the poor wretch from her melodious song  
to a muddy death.

LAERTES  
Alas, then, she is drowned?

QUEEN  
Drowned, drowned.

LAERTES  
You've had too much water already, poor Ophelia,  
and so I hold back my tears. And yet  
it's our way; nature keeps her custom,  
let shame say what it will. When these are gone,  
the woman in me will be done. Goodbye, my lord;  
I have a speech of fire that would gladly blaze,  
but this foolish weeping puts it out.

[*Exit.*]

KING  
Let's follow, Gertrude.  
How much trouble I had to calm his rage!  
Now I fear this will set it going again;  
so let's follow.

[*Exeunt.*]
