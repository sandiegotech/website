The Dhammapada, Chapter XXVI

The True Brahmin

Chapter XXVI26 of 26

383. Cut off the stream of craving, Brahmin, and drive out your desires. Once you have understood the destruction of all that is made, you will know the Uncreated.

384. When the Brahmin has crossed to the far shore in both states, then for him who has reached this knowledge every bond falls away.

385. The one for whom there is no near shore, no far shore, nor any shore between, who is free of fear and free of every chain—him I call a true Brahmin.

386. The one who sits in meditation, free of dust, his work done, his passions gone, who has reached the highest goal—him I call a true Brahmin.

387. The sun is bright by day, the moon shines by night, the warrior is bright in his armor, the Brahmin is bright in his meditation; but the Buddha, the Awakened One, shines with splendor both day and night.

388. Because he has cast out evil, he is called a Brahmin; because he lives in peace, he is called a Samana, an ascetic; because he has driven out his own impurities, he is called Pabbajita, one gone forth.

389. No one should strike a Brahmin; and a Brahmin who is struck should not strike back in anger. Shame on the one who strikes a Brahmin, but greater shame on the Brahmin who strikes back.

390. It is no small good for a Brahmin to hold his mind back from the things it loves. The more the wish to harm dies away, the more his suffering comes to rest.

391. The one who does no wrong with body, speech, or mind, who keeps himself in check on all three—him I call a true Brahmin.

392. From whomever you have learned the teaching of the Awakened One, honor that person with all your heart, as a Brahmin honors the sacred fire.

393. Not by matted hair, not by lineage, not by birth does one become a Brahmin. The one in whom there is truth and righteousness—he is the pure one, he is the Brahmin.

394. What good is your matted hair, you fool? What good your cloak of goatskins? Outside you keep yourself clean, but inside you are a tangle of craving.

395. The one who wears cast-off rags, lean, his veins standing out, who lives alone in the forest and meditates—him I call a true Brahmin.

396. I do not call anyone a Brahmin merely because of the mother who bore him or the family he came from. If he is full of pride and clings to his possessions, he is a Brahmin in name only, one who says "sir" to others. But the one who owns nothing and clings to nothing—him I call a true Brahmin.

397. The one who has cut every fetter, who never trembles, who is beyond all ties and free of every chain—him I call a true Brahmin.

398. The one who has cut the strap and the rein, the rope and the harness with it, who has thrown off the bar across the gate and is awake—him I call a true Brahmin.

399. The one who endures abuse, blows, and prison without anger, though he has done no wrong, whose strength is his patience and whose army is his endurance—him I call a true Brahmin.

400. The one who is free of anger, faithful to his duty, virtuous, without craving, mastered, wearing this body for the last time—him I call a true Brahmin.

401. The one who does not cling to pleasures any more than water clings to a lotus leaf, or a mustard seed clings to the point of a needle—him I call a true Brahmin.

402. The one who knows, here and now, the end of his own suffering, who has laid down his burden and is free of every chain—him I call a true Brahmin.

403. The one whose wisdom is deep, who knows the way from the wrong way, and who has reached the highest goal—him I call a true Brahmin.

404. The one who keeps apart from householders and homeless alike, who wanders without a home and wants almost nothing—him I call a true Brahmin.

405. The one who has laid down all violence toward every living thing, weak or strong, who neither kills nor causes others to kill—him I call a true Brahmin.

406. The one who stays gentle among the hostile, peaceful among the violent, and free of clinging among those who cling—him I call a true Brahmin.

407. The one from whom anger and hatred, pride and contempt have fallen away, like a mustard seed from the point of a needle—him I call a true Brahmin.

408. The one who speaks the truth, words that teach and never wound, words that give offense to no one—him I call a true Brahmin.

409. The one who takes nothing in this world that is not given to him, be it long or short, small or large, fair or foul—him I call a true Brahmin.

410. The one who has no longing for this world or the next, who is free of all desire and every chain—him I call a true Brahmin.

411. The one who has nothing left to cling to, who has understood and is free of all doubt, with no more "how?" and "why?", who has plunged into the deathless—him I call a true Brahmin.

412. The one who has risen above both good and evil here, beyond the pull of either, untouched by sorrow, passion, and stain—him I call a true Brahmin.

413. The one who is bright as the moon, clear and still, untroubled, in whom all craving for becoming has burned out—him I call a true Brahmin.

414. The one who has crossed this swamp, this rough and trackless road of wandering and delusion, and reached the far shore—a meditator, free of craving and of doubt, clinging to nothing, perfectly at peace—him I call a true Brahmin.

415. The one who has left every desire behind, who wanders homeless in this world, in whom all hunger for pleasure has burned out—him I call a true Brahmin.

416. The one who has left every craving behind, who wanders homeless in this world, in whom all greed has burned out—him I call a true Brahmin.

417. The one who has thrown off every human bond and risen beyond every heavenly bond, free of all bonds whatever—him I call a true Brahmin.

418. The one who has left behind what gives pleasure and what gives pain, grown cool, with nothing left to seed another birth—the hero who has conquered every world—him I call a true Brahmin.

419. The one who knows how beings pass away and rise again, everywhere, who clings to nothing, who has gone the happy way and is awake—him I call a true Brahmin.

420. The one whose path the gods cannot trace, nor the spirits, nor men, in whom every passion has burned out, an Arhat, the worthy one—him I call a true Brahmin.

421. The one who calls nothing his own—not what is past, not what is to come, not what is now—who owns nothing and grasps at nothing—him I call a true Brahmin.

422. The bull among men, the noble one, the hero, the great sage, the conqueror, the one beyond all desire, washed clean, awake—him I call a true Brahmin.

423. The one who knows his former lives, who sees the heavens and the hells, who has come to the end of all births, a sage perfected in knowledge, whose work is wholly done—him I call a true Brahmin.
