# The Dhammapada, Chapter XII

Self

157. If you hold yourself dear, then guard yourself well. Through at least one of the three watches of the night, let the wise stay awake and watchful.

158. First set yourself in what is right; only then teach others. The wise who do this bring no blame upon themselves.

159. Do yourself what you teach others to do. Master yourself first, and you may master others; for the self is the hardest of all to master.

160. You are your own master; who else could be? With yourself well mastered, you have found a master few ever find.

161. The evil you do is your own; it is born of you, bred by you, and it grinds the fool down as a diamond grinds the gem it was cut from.

162. The one whose wickedness runs deep drags himself down to the very ruin his enemy would wish for him, as a strangling vine drags down the tree it has wrapped.

163. Things that harm you, and that do you no good, are all too easy to do. What is good and brings you benefit is the hardest thing of all to do.

164. The fool who scorns the teaching of the worthy ones, the Noble Ones, the upright, and follows a false doctrine instead, bears fruit only to his own destruction, like the reed whose fruiting is its own death.

165. By yourself the wrong is done; by yourself you are stained. By yourself the wrong is left undone; by yourself you are made pure. Purity and impurity are your own; no one can purify another.

166. However great another's good may be, do not neglect your own true good for it. Once you have seen clearly what your own good is, give yourself to it.
