The Dhammapada, Chapter XVII

Anger

Chapter XVII17 of 26

221. Give up anger, let go of pride, break free of every fetter. No suffering can fall on the one who clings to neither name nor form, and who calls nothing his own.

222. The one who holds back rising anger as a driver reins in a runaway chariot—him I call a true charioteer. The rest merely hold the reins.

223. Overcome anger by calm, evil by good; overcome the miser by giving, the liar by truth.

224. Speak the truth, do not give way to anger, give when you are asked, even if you have little. By these three steps you draw near to the gods.

225. The sages who harm no one, who keep their bodies always under control, go to the unchanging place, and once they have gone there, they suffer no more.

226. For those who are always wakeful, who train themselves day and night, whose whole aim is Nirvana, every craving comes to an end.

227. This is an old saying, Atula, not something new: they blame the one who sits silent, they blame the one who talks a great deal, and they blame the one who speaks in moderation. There is no one in the world who is not blamed.

228. There never was, and never will be, and is not now, anyone who is only blamed or only praised.

229, 230. But who would dare to blame the one whom the discerning praise day after day—flawless, wise, rich in understanding and in virtue, like a coin of gold from the Jambu river? Even the gods praise such a one; even Brahma praises him.

231. Guard against the anger of the body; keep your body under control. Give up the wrongs of the body, and with your body do what is good.

232. Guard against the anger of speech; keep your speech under control. Give up the wrongs of speech, and with your speech do what is good.

233. Guard against the anger of the mind; keep your mind under control. Give up the wrongs of the mind, and with your mind do what is good.

234. The wise who control the body, who control speech, who control the mind—they are the ones who are truly well controlled.
