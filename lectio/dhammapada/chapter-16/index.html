The Dhammapada, Chapter XVI

What We Hold Dear

Chapter XVI16 of 26

209. Whoever gives himself to what does not matter, and not to meditation, who lets go of the real aim and grabs at pleasure instead, will one day envy the person who held to the path.

210. Do not bind yourself to the pleasant or to the unpleasant. Not to see what you love is pain, and to see what you hate is pain.

211. So hold nothing dear, for to lose what you hold dear is misery. Those who hold nothing dear and hate nothing have no chains to bind them.

212. From what is dear comes grief; from what is dear comes fear. To one who holds nothing dear there is no grief, so where would fear come from?

213. From affection comes grief; from affection comes fear. To one free of affection there is no grief, so where would fear come from?

214. From attachment comes grief; from attachment comes fear. To one free of attachment there is no grief, so where would fear come from?

215. From lust comes grief; from lust comes fear. To one free of lust there is no grief, so where would fear come from?

216. From craving comes grief; from craving comes fear. To one free of craving there is no grief, so where would fear come from?

217. The one who is perfect in virtue and in insight, who is just, who speaks the truth, and who does the work that is his own to do—him the world holds dear.

218. The one in whom a longing for the Ineffable has awakened, whose mind is filled with it, and whose thoughts are no longer caught by the pleasures of the senses—that one is called a swimmer against the stream.

219. When a man long away from home returns safe from afar, his kinsmen, his friends, and those who love him welcome him back with joy.

220. In just this way, when one who has done good passes from this world to the next, his good deeds welcome him home—as kinsmen welcome a loved one come back at last.
