The Dhammapada, Chapter X

The Rod

Chapter X10 of 26

129. Everyone trembles at the rod; everyone is afraid of death. Knowing that others are just like yourself, do not kill, and do not make others kill.

130. Everyone trembles at the rod; everyone holds life dear. Knowing that others are just like yourself, do not kill, and do not make others kill.

131. Whoever, hunting his own happiness, harms with the rod other beings who long for happiness too, will find no happiness hereafter.

132. But whoever, hunting his own happiness, does not harm with the rod other beings who long for happiness too, will find happiness hereafter.

133. Speak harshly to no one, or those you wound will answer you in kind. Angry words are painful, and the rod will come back on you.

134. But if you can stay silent, like a gong that is cracked and gives no sound, then you have reached Nirvana, for there is no more strife in you.

135. As a cowherd with his staff drives the cattle out to pasture, so do old age and death drive the life of every living thing.

136. Even while he is doing it, the fool does not see the harm in his evil deeds; but later he is scorched by what he has done, as if burned by fire.

137. Whoever takes up the rod against those who are unarmed and harmless, who wrongs the innocent, will quickly come to one of these ten states:

138. sharp pain, or disaster, or injury to the body, or grave illness, or loss of his mind;

139. or trouble from those in power, or a terrible accusation, or the loss of his family, or the ruin of all he owns;

140. or fire will burn his house to the ground. And when his body breaks apart, the fool is reborn in hell.

141. Not going naked, not matted hair, not filth, not fasting, not sleeping on the bare ground, not smearing the body with dust, not crouching motionless—none of these can cleanse a person who has not gone beyond doubt.

142. But the one who is calm even though finely dressed, who is still, restrained, and self-controlled, who lives a pure life and has laid down the rod against every living thing—he is the true Brahmin, the true ascetic, the true monk.

143. Is there anywhere in the world a person so restrained by his own sense of shame that he gives no cause for blame, as a fine horse gives no cause for the whip?

144. Be like a fine horse that breaks into a run at the touch of the whip: be ardent and full of urgency. By faith, by virtue, by effort, by deep meditation, by clear judgment of the truth, by right knowledge and right conduct, with full awareness, you will leave this great suffering behind.

145. Farmers channel the water where they want it; arrow-makers straighten the shaft; carpenters shape the wood; and the good shape themselves.
