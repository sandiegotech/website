The Dhammapada, Chapter VI

The Wise

Chapter VI6 of 26

76. If you meet a discerning person who sees your faults and tells you of them, who shows you what to avoid and reproves you when you are wrong, follow that wise one as you would follow a guide to buried treasure. It will go better, never worse, for the one who follows him.

77. Let such a person counsel you, teach you, and hold you back from what is base. The good will love him for it; only the bad will hate him.

78. Do not make friends of those who do wrong; do not make friends of the mean and low. Make friends of the good; make friends of the best among people.

79. Whoever drinks deep of the teaching lives happily, with a mind at peace. The wise take joy, day after day, in the truth the Noble Ones have taught.

80. Farmers channel the water where they want it; arrow-makers straighten the shaft; carpenters shape the wood; and the wise shape themselves.

81. As a solid rock is not shaken by the wind, so the wise are not moved by praise or blame.

82. When the wise have listened to the teaching, they grow clear and still, like a lake that is deep, smooth, and undisturbed.

83. Good people let go wherever they are; they do not chatter on, hungry for pleasure. Touched by happiness or by sorrow, the wise show neither swelling joy nor sinking despair.

84. The one who would not, for his own sake or another's, crave a son, or wealth, or a kingdom, and who would not seek his own success by wrong means—that one is good, and wise, and upright.

85. Few among people ever cross to the other shore; the rest run up and down the near bank.

86. But those who follow the teaching, when it has been well taught to them, will cross beyond the reach of death, hard as that crossing is to make.

87. Leaving the dark way behind, let the wise take up the bright way. Let him go from home into homelessness, into the solitude that few find easy to enjoy.

88. There let him seek his joy, having given up every pleasure and called nothing his own. Let the wise cleanse himself of all that troubles the mind.

89. Those whose minds are firmly grounded in the factors of awakening, who cling to nothing and delight in letting go of all grasping, whose craving is mastered and who shine with light—they are set free, even here in this world.
