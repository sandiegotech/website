# The Dhammapada, Chapter XXI

Miscellaneous Verses

290. If by giving up a lesser happiness one may gain a greater, let the wise give up the lesser and look to the greater.

291. Whoever seeks his own happiness by causing pain to others is caught in the bonds of hatred, and from hatred he is never freed.

292. What ought to be done is left undone, and what ought not to be done is done; in those who are unruly and heedless the taints keep on growing.

293. But in those who are always mindful of the body, who do not do what ought not to be done and steadily do what ought to be done, the taints, in such mindful and clear-seeing people, come to an end.

294. Having killed mother and father and two warrior kings, and having destroyed a kingdom along with its treasurer, the true Brahmin goes on his way unharmed.

295. Having killed mother and father and two learned kings, and a tiger as the fifth, the true Brahmin goes on his way unharmed.

296. The followers of Gotama are always wide awake; day and night their thoughts are set on the Buddha.

297. The followers of Gotama are always wide awake; day and night their thoughts are set on the teaching.

298. The followers of Gotama are always wide awake; day and night their thoughts are set on the Order.

299. The followers of Gotama are always wide awake; day and night their thoughts are set on the body.

300. The followers of Gotama are always wide awake; day and night their minds delight in doing no harm.

301. The followers of Gotama are always wide awake; day and night their minds delight in meditation.

302. Hard it is to leave the world; hard it is to delight in that life. Hard and painful is life in a household; painful it is to live among those who are not your equals. And painful it is to keep wandering on through the round of birth and death—so do not be a wanderer who is hounded by that pain.

303. The one who has faith and virtue, who has a good name and means of his own, is honored wherever he goes.

304. The good are seen from far off, like the snowy peaks of the Himalayas; the bad pass unseen, like arrows shot in the night.

305. Sitting alone, sleeping alone, walking alone, never weary, taming himself alone—he will find his joy at the edge of the forest.
