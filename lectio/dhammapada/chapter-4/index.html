The Dhammapada, Chapter IV

Flowers

Chapter IV4 of 26

44. Who will master this earth, and the realm of Yama, and the world of the gods? Who will gather the well-taught path of virtue, as a skilled hand chooses the right flower?

45. The disciple in training will master this earth, and the realm of Yama, and the world of the gods. The disciple will gather the well-taught path of virtue, as a skilled hand chooses the right flower.

46. Whoever knows that this body is like foam, and sees that it is as fleeting as a mirage, will snap the flower-tipped arrows of Mara and pass beyond the sight of the King of Death.

47. Death sweeps away the one who is busy gathering flowers, his mind caught up in them, as a flood sweeps away a sleeping village.

48. Death overpowers the one who is busy gathering flowers, his mind caught up in them, before he has had his fill of pleasure.

49. As the bee takes the nectar and flies off without harming the flower, its color, or its scent, so let the sage move through the village.

50. Let the sage not dwell on the faults of others, on what they have done or left undone, but only on what he himself has done or left undone.

51. Like a lovely flower, full of color but with no scent, are fine words that bear no fruit in one who does not act on them.

52. But like a lovely flower, full of color and full of scent, are fine words that bear fruit in one who acts on them.

53. As many garlands can be woven from a heap of flowers, so should a mortal, once born, do many good things with this life.

54. The scent of flowers does not travel against the wind, nor the scent of sandalwood, of tagara, or of jasmine; but the fragrance of the good travels even against the wind. The fragrance of the good spreads in every direction.

55. Sandalwood and tagara, the lotus and the jasmine—sweet as all these perfumes are, the fragrance of virtue surpasses them all.

56. Faint is the scent of tagara and sandalwood; but the fragrance of those who keep virtue is the finest of all, and it rises even to the gods.

57. Mara never finds the path of those who keep these virtues, who live in heedfulness, and who are set free by true knowledge.

58, 59. As on a heap of rubbish tossed by the roadside a lotus may grow, sweet-scented and a delight to the heart, so among the rubbish of blind and ordinary people the disciple of the truly awakened Buddha shines out in wisdom.
