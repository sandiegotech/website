# The Dhammapada, Chapter IX

Evil

116. Be quick to do good, and hold your mind back from evil. The one who is slow to do good finds his mind taking its delight in evil.

117. If you do wrong, do not do it again; do not let your heart take pleasure in it. Heaping up evil ends in pain.

118. If you do good, do it again; let your heart take pleasure in it. Heaping up good ends in happiness.

119. Even the wrongdoer sees good days, as long as his evil has not yet ripened. But once that evil ripens, the wrongdoer meets his evil.

120. Even the good person sees hard days, as long as his good has not yet ripened. But once that good ripens, the good person meets his good days.

121. Do not think lightly of evil, telling yourself, "It will never come near me." Drop by drop the water-pot is filled; little by little the fool fills himself with evil.

122. Do not think lightly of good, telling yourself, "It will never come near me." Drop by drop the water-pot is filled; little by little the wise fill themselves with good.

123. Avoid evil deeds as a merchant carrying rich goods with few companions avoids a dangerous road, as one who loves life avoids poison.

124. A hand with no wound on it may carry poison unharmed, for poison cannot enter where there is no wound. In the same way, no evil touches the one who does no evil.

125. Whoever wrongs an innocent person—one who is harmless, pure, and blameless—the evil falls back on that fool, like fine dust thrown against the wind.

126. Some are born again into the womb; those who do evil go to hell; those who do right go to heaven; and those who are free of every desire reach Nirvana.

127, 128. Not in the sky, not in the middle of the sea, not deep in a mountain cave—nowhere in all the world is there a place where you can escape your own evil deed. Not in the sky, not in the middle of the sea, not deep in a mountain cave—nowhere in all the world is there a place where death cannot overtake you.
