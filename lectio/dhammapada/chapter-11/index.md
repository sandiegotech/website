# The Dhammapada, Chapter XI

Old Age

146. What laughter, what joy, when the world is forever burning? Wrapped in darkness as you are, why do you not seek a light?

147. Look at this body, all dressed up—a mass of wounds, a thing of patched-together parts, sickly, full of many thoughts, with nothing in it that lasts.

148. This body is worn out, a nest of disease, ready to fall apart. This rotting heap breaks to pieces; life ends in death.

149. These white bones, scattered like gourds thrown away in autumn—what pleasure is there in looking at them?

150. The body is a fortress built of bones and plastered over with flesh and blood, and inside it live old age and death, pride and deceit.

151. Even the gleaming chariots of kings wear out, and the body too is wearing toward its end. But the virtue of the good never wears out—so the good teach it to the good.

152. The person who has learned little grows old like an ox: his body grows heavier, but his wisdom does not grow.

153, 154. Through many births I ran on, seeking and never finding the builder of this house—and painful is birth, again and again. House-builder, now you are seen! You shall not build this house again. Your rafters are all broken, your ridgepole shattered. My mind has reached the unconditioned; I have come to the end of craving.

155. Those who have kept no discipline, and have gathered no treasure in their youth, waste away like old herons beside a lake that has no fish.

156. Those who have kept no discipline, and have gathered no treasure in their youth, lie like worn-out bows, sighing over the past.
