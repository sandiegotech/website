The Dhammapada, Chapter XX

The Path

Chapter XX20 of 26

273. Of all paths, the Eightfold Path is the best; of all truths, the Four Noble Truths; of all states, freedom from craving; and of all beings on two feet, the one who has eyes to see.

274. This is the Way; there is no other that leads to the purifying of vision. Follow it, and you will bewilder Mara.

275. Walk this Way and you will make an end of suffering. This is the Way I taught, once I had learned to draw out the arrow.

276. You yourself must make the effort. The Buddhas only point the way. Those who take up the path and walk it, deep in meditation, are freed from the bonds of Mara.

277. "All conditioned things are impermanent"—when you see this with wisdom, you turn away from suffering. This is the path to purity.

278. "All conditioned things are suffering"—when you see this with wisdom, you turn away from suffering. This is the path to purity.

279. "All things are without a self"—when you see this with wisdom, you turn away from suffering. This is the path to purity.

280. The one who will not rouse himself when it is time to rise, who is young and strong but full of sloth, weak in will and weak in thought, that lazy and idle man never finds the way to wisdom.

281. Guard your speech, hold your mind in check, and do no wrong with your body. Keep these three roads of action clean, and you will win the way the wise have taught.

282. From effort wisdom grows; without effort it withers away. Knowing this double path—the one that gains and the one that loses—set yourself so that wisdom grows.

283. Cut down the whole forest of desire, not just one tree—for out of that forest comes fear. Clear away the forest and the undergrowth, monks, and you will be free.

284. As long as the least desire of a man for a woman is not cut away, his mind is tied down, like a suckling calf to its mother.

285. Cut out the love of self, as you would pluck an autumn lotus with your hand. Tend the road to peace, to Nirvana, which the Buddha has shown.

286. "Here I will live through the rains, and here through winter and summer"—so the fool plans, never thinking of his death.

287. But death comes and sweeps that man away—proud of his children and his herds, his mind caught up in them—as a flood sweeps away a sleeping village.

288. Sons are no help then, nor a father, nor any of your kin; once death has seized you, there is no help in family.

289. Knowing this, the wise and disciplined should clear the road at once—the road that leads to Nirvana.
