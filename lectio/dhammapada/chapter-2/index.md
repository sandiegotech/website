# The Dhammapada, Chapter II

Heedfulness

21. Heedfulness is the path to the deathless; heedlessness is the path to death. The heedful do not die; the heedless are as if already dead.

22. Seeing this clearly, the wise hold heedfulness above all else. They take their joy in heedfulness, delighting in the ground the Noble Ones walk.

23. These are the meditators, steady, unrelenting in their effort; and these are the ones, strong and clear-minded, who reach Nirvana, the highest freedom, the highest peace.

24. The one who has roused himself and stays awake, whose deeds are clean, who acts with care, who holds himself in check and lives by what is right and heedful—his good name only grows.

25. By rousing himself, by heedfulness, by self-mastery and self-control, let the wise build an island that no flood can wash away.

26. Fools, the slow-witted, give themselves over to heedlessness. The wise guard their heedfulness as their most precious treasure.

27. Do not give yourself to heedlessness; do not give yourself to sensual delight. Heedful and absorbed in meditation, you will come into ample joy.

28. When the wise drive out heedlessness by heedfulness, they climb the high terraces of wisdom and, free of sorrow, look down on the sorrowing crowd—as one standing on a mountain looks down on those who stand on the plain.

29. Heedful among the heedless, wide awake among the sleeping, the wise leave them far behind, like a swift horse that leaves a weak one behind.

30. By heedfulness Maghavan rose to be lord of the gods. Heedfulness is forever praised; heedlessness is forever blamed.

31. The monk who delights in heedfulness and dreads heedlessness moves like a fire, burning away every fetter, small or great.

32. The monk who delights in heedfulness and dreads heedlessness cannot fall back—he is close to Nirvana.
