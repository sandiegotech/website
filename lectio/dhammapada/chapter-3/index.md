# The Dhammapada, Chapter III

The Mind

33. As an arrow-maker straightens his arrow, the wise straighten the mind—restless and unsteady, hard to guard, hard to hold back.

34. Like a fish pulled from the water and thrown on dry land, the mind thrashes and quivers as it struggles to escape the grip of Mara.

35. The mind is hard to hold down, light and quick, settling wherever it pleases. Good it is to tame it; a tamed mind brings happiness.

36. Let the wise watch over the mind, so hard to see, so subtle, settling wherever it pleases. A mind well guarded brings happiness.

37. The mind travels far, wanders alone, has no body, and hides in the cave of the heart. Those who keep it in check will be freed from the bonds of Mara.

38. If the mind is unsteady, if a person does not know the true teaching, if their peace is shaken, their wisdom will never come to fullness.

39. But for one whose mind is unshaken, untroubled, who has let go of both good and evil and stays awake, there is no fear.

40. Knowing this body to be fragile as a clay jar, make the mind a fortress, and with the weapon of wisdom go to war against Mara—then guard what you have won, and never rest at ease.

41. Before long, alas, this body will lie on the ground, cast aside, empty of awareness, like a useless log.

42. Whatever harm an enemy may do to an enemy, or a hater to one he hates, a misdirected mind will do you far worse.

43. Not a mother, not a father, not any kin can do for you what a rightly directed mind can do—far greater is its gift.
