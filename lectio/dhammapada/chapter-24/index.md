# The Dhammapada, Chapter XXIV

Craving

334. The craving of a heedless person grows like a creeping vine. He leaps from one life to the next, like a monkey swinging through the forest after fruit.

335. Whomever this fierce, poisonous craving overcomes in this world, his sorrows grow thick and fast, like wild grass after rain.

336. But whoever masters this fierce craving, so hard to overcome, finds his sorrows fall away from him, like water-drops sliding off a lotus leaf.

337. So here is the good word I give to all of you gathered here: dig up craving by the root, as one who wants the fragrant root digs up the grass above it—so that Mara may not break you again and again, as a flood breaks the reeds.

338. As a tree that has been cut down will grow again if its root is sound and deep, so suffering returns again and again until you dig out the very root of craving.

339. The man whose thirty-six streams of craving run strong toward pleasure is swept away: the flood of his own passionate longings carries him off.

340. These streams run everywhere; the vine of craving keeps springing up. When you see it sprouting, cut it at the root with wisdom.

341. People delight in pleasures, lavish and sweet. Sunk in their longing, chasing their pleasures, they go through birth and decay again and again.

342. Driven on by craving, people run round and round like a snared hare. Caught in fetters and bonds, they suffer, again and again, for a long time.

343. Driven on by craving, people run round and round like a snared hare. So let the monk drive out craving, by working toward freedom from craving for himself.

344. Look at the man who has cut his way out of the thicket of craving, only to run straight back into it—who, set free, runs back into bondage of his own accord.

345. The wise do not call the strongest chain the one made of iron, or wood, or rope. Far stronger is the pull of jewels and rings, of children and wife—

346. that is the chain the wise call strong: it drags you down, it gives a little but will not let go. Yet even this they cut, and go forth, wanting nothing, leaving every pleasure behind.

347. Those enslaved by their passions are swept along the stream of desire, as a spider runs down the web it spun itself. This too the wise cut through, and go forth, wanting nothing, leaving every sorrow behind.

348. Let go of the past, let go of the future, let go of the present, and cross to the far shore of existence. With your mind set free on every side, you will come no more to birth and decay.

349. If a man is tossed about by doubt, burning with desire, fixed only on what delights him, his craving grows and grows, and the chain he forges grows only stronger.

350. But if a man delights in stilling his doubts, ever watchful, dwelling on what is not pleasant to the craving mind, he will surely make an end—he will cut clean through the chain of Mara.

351. The one who has reached the goal, who is fearless, free of craving and free of fault, has broken off every thorn of life. This is his last body.

352. The one free of craving and free of clinging, who is skilled in language and its meanings, who knows how words are formed and ordered—he wears his last body. He is called the great sage, the great person.

353. "I have conquered all and I know all. In every state of life I am untouched. I have let go of everything, and through the end of craving I am free. Knowing this for myself, whom should I call my teacher?"

354. The gift of the teaching surpasses all gifts; the taste of the teaching surpasses all tastes; the delight in the teaching surpasses all delights; the end of craving conquers all sorrow.

355. Wealth destroys the fool who does not look for the far shore. Through his thirst for wealth the fool destroys himself, as if he were his own enemy.

356. Weeds ruin a field; craving ruins those who give in to it. So a gift to those who are free of craving bears great fruit.

357. Weeds ruin a field; hatred ruins those who give in to it. So a gift to those who are free of hatred bears great fruit.

358. Weeds ruin a field; delusion ruins those who give in to it. So a gift to those who are free of delusion bears great fruit.

359. Weeds ruin a field; longing ruins those who give in to it. So a gift to those who are free of longing bears great fruit.
