The Dhammapada, Chapter VII

The Arhat

Chapter VII7 of 26

90. For the one who has finished the journey, who has let go of grief, who is free on every side and has thrown off every chain, there is no more fever of pain.

91. The mindful keep moving; they take no comfort in settling down. Like swans that rise and leave the lake, they leave one resting place after another behind them.

92. They store up nothing; they understand the food they eat for what it is; they range in the freedom that is empty and without sign. Their track cannot be followed, like the path of birds across the sky.

93. The one whose cravings are stilled, who is not caught in any pleasure, whose range is the freedom that is empty and without sign, his track cannot be followed, like the path of birds across the sky.

94. Even the gods envy the one whose senses are calmed, like horses well broken by their driver, who has shed all pride and is free of every craving.

95. Such a one, true to his vows, bears all things and resents nothing, like the earth; he stands firm, like the great threshold-pillar at the city gate; he is clear, like a deep pool with no mud to cloud it. For him there are no more births to come.

96. Quiet is the mind of such a one, quiet his words, quiet his deeds, for he has won his freedom through true knowledge and come to rest.

97. The one who takes nothing on blind faith but knows the uncreated for himself, who has cut every tie, ended every chance of rebirth, and thrown off every desire—he is the greatest of all.

98. In a village or in the forest, in the valley or on the hill, wherever the venerable ones dwell—there the ground is a delight.

99. Delightful are the forests where the crowd finds no delight. There those who are free of craving will be glad, for they are not looking for pleasures.
