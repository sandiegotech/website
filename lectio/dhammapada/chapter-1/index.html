The Dhammapada, Chapter I

The Twin Verses

Chapter I1 of 26

1. All that we are is shaped by what we have thought. The mind goes before everything; the mind is chief of all; everything is made of the mind. If you speak or act with a corrupted mind, suffering follows you, as the wheel follows the foot of the ox that pulls the cart.

2. All that we are is shaped by what we have thought. The mind goes before everything; the mind is chief of all; everything is made of the mind. If you speak or act with a clear mind, happiness follows you, like a shadow that never leaves.

3. "He insulted me, he struck me, he got the better of me, he robbed me"—in those who hold on to such thoughts, hatred never ends.

4. "He insulted me, he struck me, he got the better of me, he robbed me"—in those who let go of such thoughts, hatred comes to an end.

5. Hatred is never ended by hatred. Hatred ends only by love—this is the ancient and unchanging law.

6. Most people never grasp that we are all bound to die here. But those who do grasp it lay down their quarrels at once.

7. Whoever lives chasing only pleasures, with his senses unguarded, eating without limit, lazy and weak—Mara will overthrow him as the wind overthrows a frail tree.

8. Whoever lives without chasing pleasures, with his senses well guarded, eating in moderation, faithful and strong—Mara will no more overthrow him than the wind can overthrow a mountain of rock.

9. Whoever puts on the saffron robe before he has cleansed himself of his own corruption, who has no self-restraint and no truth in him, is not worthy of the robe.

10. But whoever has cleansed himself of corruption, is firmly rooted in virtue, and holds to self-restraint and truth, he is truly worthy of the robe.

11. Those who take the false for the true, and see the true as false, never reach the truth; they wander after wrong intentions.

12. Those who know the true as true, and the false as false, do reach the truth; they follow right intentions.

13. As rain breaks through a poorly thatched house, so craving breaks through an undisciplined mind.

14. As rain cannot break through a well-thatched house, so craving cannot break through a well-disciplined mind.

15. The wrongdoer grieves in this world and grieves in the next; in both he grieves. He grieves and suffers when he sees the harm his own deeds have done.

16. The good person rejoices in this world and rejoices in the next; in both he rejoices. He rejoices and is glad when he sees the good his own deeds have done.

17. The wrongdoer suffers in this world and suffers in the next; in both he suffers. He suffers knowing, "I have done wrong," and he suffers still more on the path that wrongdoing leads to.

18. The good person is happy in this world and happy in the next; in both he is happy. He is happy knowing, "I have done good," and he is happier still on the path that good leads to.

19. Though a heedless man can recite a great deal of the teaching, if he does not live by it he has no share in the holy life—he is like a hired cowherd counting another man's cattle.

20. Though a man can recite only a little of the teaching, if he lives by it—if he has let go of craving, hatred, and delusion, if he has true knowledge and a mind set free, clinging to nothing in this world or the next—he has a real share in the holy life.
