# The Dhammapada, Chapter XXII

The Downward Course

306. Whoever says what is not so goes to hell, and so does the one who, having done a thing, says, "I did not do it." After death both are alike—they are people who carry their wrongdoing into the next world.

307. Many who wear the yellow robe around their shoulders are undisciplined and out of control; by their own wrongdoing such wrongdoers go to hell.

308. Better to swallow a red-hot iron ball, blazing like fire, than to live on the alms of the land while being immoral and undisciplined.

309. Four things come to the reckless man who goes after his neighbor's wife: he earns disgrace, his sleep is broken, third comes punishment, and last comes hell.

310. There is disgrace, and the road that leads downward, and only the brief pleasure of one frightened soul in the arms of another, and the king's heavy punishment. So let no man set his heart on his neighbor's wife.

311. Just as a blade of kusa grass, wrongly grasped, cuts the very hand that holds it, so the monk's life, wrongly lived, drags a man down to hell.

312. Anything done half-heartedly, a vow once broken, a discipline kept only grudgingly—none of it bears much fruit.

313. If a thing is worth doing, do it; throw yourself into it. A monk who wanders through his practice half-asleep only stirs up more of the dust of craving.

314. A wrong deed is better left undone, for afterward it brings regret; a right deed is better done, for having done it, you never look back with regret.

315. Like a frontier fort guarded inside and out, so guard yourself. Do not let a single moment slip past, for those who let the right moment pass suffer for it when they come to hell.

316. Those who are ashamed of what is no shame, and feel no shame at what truly should shame them—taking up wrong views, such people go down the wrong road.

317. Those who are afraid of what is no danger, and unafraid of what is truly dangerous—taking up wrong views, such people go down the wrong road.

318. Those who see fault where there is none, and see no fault where the fault is real—taking up wrong views, such people go down the wrong road.

319. But those who see fault as fault, and what is faultless as faultless—holding to right views, such people go down the right road.
