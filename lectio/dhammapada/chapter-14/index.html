The Dhammapada, Chapter XIV

The Awakened One

Chapter XIV14 of 26

179. The one whose conquest cannot be undone, into whose conquest no craving in this world can enter—by what track will you trace him, the Awakened One, whose knowing is endless, who leaves no trail at all?

180. The one whom no desire, with all its nets and poisons, can lead anywhere astray—by what track will you trace him, the Awakened One, whose knowing is endless, who leaves no trail at all?

181. Even the gods envy the wise—those who are awake and never heedless, who give themselves to meditation, who delight in the peace of letting the world go.

182. Hard it is to be born a human being; hard is the life of mortals; hard it is to hear the true teaching; and hard is the arising of a Buddha, an Awakened One.

183. Not to do any evil; to cultivate good; to purify one's own mind—this is the teaching of all the Buddhas, the Awakened Ones.

184. Patience and endurance are the highest austerity; "Nirvana is the highest," say the Buddhas. The one who strikes another is no true renouncer; the one who wounds another is no true ascetic.

185. To speak no blame and do no harm; to live restrained under the rule of the teaching; to eat in moderation; to keep to a solitary bed and seat; and to give the mind to the highest things—this is the teaching of the Awakened.

186. There is no satisfying the craving for pleasure, not even with a downpour of gold. "Pleasures are sweet for a moment and end in pain"—the wise know this.

187. So the disciple who is fully awake takes no delight even in the pleasures of heaven; he delights only in the ending of all craving.

188. Driven by fear, people run for refuge to many places—to mountains and forests, to groves and sacred trees.

189. But that is not the safe refuge, that is not the highest refuge; taking refuge there, you are not freed from all suffering.

190. The one who takes refuge in the Buddha, in the teaching, and in the Order, and who sees with right wisdom the Four Noble Truths:

191. that there is suffering; that suffering has a cause; that suffering can be brought to an end; and that there is the Noble Eightfold Path that leads to the end of suffering—

192. that is the safe refuge, that is the highest refuge; taking that refuge, you are freed from all suffering.

193. A great one, a Buddha, is hard to find; he is not born just anywhere. But wherever such a sage is born, that people flourishes.

194. Happy is the arising of the Awakened; happy is the proclaiming of the true teaching; happy is the harmony of the Order; happy is the practice of those who live in harmony.

195, 196. Whoever honors those worthy of honor—whether the Awakened Buddhas or their disciples—who have left every obstacle behind and crossed beyond all grief and lament; whoever honors such ones, set free and past all fear, his merit is beyond all measuring; no one can reckon how great it is.
