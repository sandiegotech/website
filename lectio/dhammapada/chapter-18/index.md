# The Dhammapada, Chapter XVIII

Impurity

235. You are now like a withered leaf; the messengers of death are already at hand. You stand at the threshold of departure, and you have packed no provisions for the road.

236. Make an island of yourself. Strive hard; be wise. Once you have blown away your impurities and are free of all stain, you will enter the heavenly realm of the Noble Ones.

237. Your life has run its course; you have come face to face with death. There is no resting place for you along the way, and you have packed no provisions for the road.

238. Make an island of yourself. Strive hard; be wise. Once you have blown away your impurities and are free of all stain, you will not enter again into birth and decay.

239. Let the wise blow away their own impurities, one by one, little by little, moment by moment, as a smith blows the dross from silver.

240. Just as rust, springing from the iron itself, eats away the very iron that bred it, so a transgressor's own deeds lead him down to ruin.

241. Scripture is tainted when it goes unrecited, a house when it goes unrepaired; sloth is the taint of beauty, and heedlessness the taint of a watchman.

242. Misconduct is the taint of a woman; stinginess is the taint of a giver. Tainted are all evil ways, in this world and the next.

243. But there is a taint worse than all these: ignorance is the greatest taint of all. Cast off that taint, monks, and become taintless.

244. Life is easy for the shameless one, brazen as a crow, a meddler, a slanderer, insolent and corrupt.

245. But life is hard for the modest one, who always seeks what is pure, who is detached, unassuming, clean, and clear-sighted.

246. Whoever destroys life, speaks falsehood, takes in this world what is not given, and goes to another man's wife;

247. and whoever gives himself to strong drink—that person, even here in this world, digs up his own roots.

248. Know this, friend: those who cannot hold themselves in check come to a bad end. Do not let greed and vice drag you into long suffering.

249. People give according to their faith, according to what pleases them. But whoever frets over the food and drink given to others will find no peace, by day or by night.

250. The one in whom that fretting is destroyed, torn out by the very root, finds peace by day and by night.

251. There is no fire like craving, no grip like hatred, no snare like delusion, no torrent like greed.

252. The faults of others are easy to see; our own are hard to see. We winnow the faults of our neighbors like chaff, but hide our own, as a cheat hides the losing throw from the gambler.

253. Whoever watches for the faults of others, forever taking offense, only feeds his own cravings; he comes no nearer to their end.

254. There is no path through the sky; there is no true ascetic outside this teaching. People delight in what holds them back, but the Tathagatas are free of all such hindrance.

255. There is no path through the sky; there is no true ascetic outside this teaching. Nothing made of conditions lasts, but the Buddhas are never shaken.
