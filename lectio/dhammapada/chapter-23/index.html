The Dhammapada, Chapter XXIII

The Elephant

Chapter XXIII23 of 26

320. I will bear abuse in silence, as the elephant in battle bears the arrow shot from the bow; for many people are ill-natured.

321. They lead a tamed elephant into battle; the king mounts a tamed elephant. The one who is tamed, who bears abuse in silence, is the best among people.

322. Good are tamed mules, and thoroughbred horses of Sindh, and great tusked elephants; but better than any of them is the one who has tamed himself.

323. For on none of these animals can you reach the untrodden country. Only the one who has tamed himself goes there—tamed, riding his own well-tamed self.

324. The elephant called Dhanapalaka, in rut, his temples streaming, and hard to hold, will not eat a mouthful once he is tied up: the elephant is longing for the elephant-grove.

325. The man who is slack and overeats, who is drowsy and rolls about like a great hog fattened on slop, that fool is born again and again.

326. In the past this mind of mine wandered wherever it wished, wherever it liked, wherever it pleased. Now I will hold it firmly in check, as the trainer with his hook holds in a raging elephant.

327. Delight in heedfulness; guard your own mind. Pull yourself out of the wrong path, as an elephant pulls itself out of the mud.

328. If you find a wise companion to walk with you, one who is prudent and lives well, then walk with them, glad and mindful, overcoming every danger.

329. If you find no wise companion to walk with you, none who is prudent and lives well, then walk alone—like a king who has left behind the kingdom he had won, like a Matanga elephant roaming the forest.

330. It is better to walk alone; there is no companionship with a fool. Walk alone and do no wrong, wanting little, like a Matanga elephant in the forest.

331. Friends are a joy when the need for them arises. Contentment is a joy, whatever brings it. A good deed done is a joy at the hour of death. And the laying down of all sorrow is a joy.

332. To honor your mother is a joy in this world, and to honor your father is a joy; to honor ascetics is a joy, and to honor Brahmins is a joy.

333. A joy is virtue that lasts into old age; a joy is faith firmly rooted; a joy is the winning of wisdom; and a joy is to do no evil.
