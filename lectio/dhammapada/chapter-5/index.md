# The Dhammapada, Chapter V

The Fool

60. Long is the night to one who lies awake; long is the road to one who is weary; long is the round of rebirth to the fool who does not know the true teaching.

61. If on his journey a traveler meets no one better than himself, or even his equal, let him hold firmly to his solitary road; there is no companionship with a fool.

62. "These sons are mine, this wealth is mine"—with such thoughts the fool torments himself. He does not even belong to himself; how then can sons or wealth be his?

63. The fool who knows he is a fool is wise at least in that. But the fool who thinks himself wise—he is the fool indeed.

64. Even if a fool keeps company with a wise man all his life, he no more grasps the truth than a spoon tastes the flavor of the soup.

65. But if a clear-minded man keeps company with a wise man for even a moment, he grasps the truth at once, as the tongue tastes the flavor of the soup.

66. Fools, dull of understanding, are their own worst enemies, for they do evil deeds that bear bitter fruit.

67. That deed is not well done which a man regrets afterward, whose fruit he receives weeping, with tears on his face.

68. But that deed is well done which a man does not regret, whose fruit he receives with delight and a happy heart.

69. As long as the evil deed has not yet ripened, the fool thinks it sweet as honey; but when it ripens, the fool comes to grief.

70. Month after month a fool may eat his food with the tip of a blade of kusa grass, like an ascetic—yet he is not worth a sixteenth part of those who have truly understood the teaching.

71. An evil deed does not curdle at once, like fresh milk; it follows the fool smoldering, like a fire hidden under ashes.

72. And when at last it comes to light, that evil deed brings the fool only sorrow; it shatters his good fortune and splits his very head.

73. The fool wants a reputation he has not earned, precedence among the monks, authority in the monasteries, and honor from people everywhere.

74. "Let both layfolk and monks think this was done by me; let them defer to me in all things, great and small"—such is the mind of the fool, and his craving and his pride only swell.

75. One road leads to worldly gain, another leads to Nirvana. Knowing this, let the monk, the follower of the Buddha, take no delight in honor, but give himself to solitude instead.
