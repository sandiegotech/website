The Dhammapada, Chapter XIX

The Just

Chapter XIX19 of 26

256. You are not just because you settle a case in haste. The wise weigh both sides, the right and the wrong, before they decide.

257. The one who judges others not by force but fairly and by law, who is guarded by the law and discerning—that one is called just, one who stands in the law.

258. You are not wise because you talk a great deal. The one who is patient, free of hatred, free of fear—that one is called wise.

259. You do not uphold the teaching because you talk a great deal. Even one who has learned only a little, but sees the truth in his own person, truly upholds the teaching—he is the one who never turns his back on it.

260. You are not an elder because your head is gray. To be ripe in years and no more than that is to be old in vain.

261. But the one in whom there is truth and virtue, who does no harm, who is restrained and master of himself, the wise one who has washed out every stain—he is the one who deserves to be called an elder.

262. No amount of fine talk, no beauty of face, will make a man worthy of respect if he is envious, grasping, and false.

263. But the one in whom all this is cut down, torn out by the very root—the one who has let go of hatred and grown wise—he is the one worthy of respect.

264. A shaved head does not make a man an ascetic if he has no discipline and speaks lies. How can anyone still gripped by craving and greed be an ascetic?

265. But the one who quiets every evil in himself, great or small, is an ascetic indeed, for he has stilled all evil.

266. You are not a monk simply because you beg your food from others. Whoever merely begs is not a monk; it is the one who takes up the whole teaching, and lives by it, who is.

267. The one who has risen above both good and evil, who lives a pure life and walks through the world with true understanding—he is the one truly called a monk.

268, 269. Silence does not make a sage of a man who is dull and ignorant. But the one who is wise, who holds up the scales and chooses the good and turns away from evil—he is a sage, and a sage for that very reason. The one who weighs both worlds is the one called a sage.

270. You are not noble if you do harm to living things. It is because he does no harm to any living thing that a man is called noble.

271, 272. Not by rules and vows alone, not by great learning, not by the depths of meditation, not by sleeping apart from others, do I touch the freedom and peace that no ordinary person knows. Monk, do not rest content until you have reached the end of craving.
