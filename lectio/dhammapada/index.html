The Dhammapada

Early Buddhist tradition

  • 300–100 BCE
  • Eastern tradition
  • Religion
  • Lectio Edition 2026

About four hundred short verses, sayings of the Buddha gathered by theme. It is the best loved and most quoted book in Buddhism. The verses were made to be learned by heart.

Why this editionGoal and purpose

Proverbs should sound like proverbs.

F. Max Müller's translation of 1881 was made for the Sacred Books of the East. It is a scholar's version in Victorian prose. It says "tabernacle" for a house and "sin" for harm, and it explains terms in parentheses in the middle of a verse.

Some of it is simply wrong. Müller has "All forms are unreal" where the Pali says all things are without a self.

The Lectio Edition gives the twenty-six chapters in plain English that keeps the weight of a saying. It follows the Pali where Müller departs from it. Müller stays in the app beside it.

The openingFrom the Lectio Edition

How it begins.

Chapter I. The Twin-Verses · The Twin Verses

1. All that we are is shaped by what we have thought. The mind goes before everything; the mind is chief of all; everything is made of the mind. If you speak or act with a corrupted mind, suffering follows you, as the wheel follows the foot of the ox that pulls the cart.

2. All that we are is shaped by what we have thought. The mind goes before everything; the mind is chief of all; everything is made of the mind. If you speak or act with a clear mind, happiness follows you, like a shadow that never leaves.

3. "He insulted me, he struck me, he got the better of me, he robbed me"—in those who hold on to such thoughts, hatred never ends.

4. "He insulted me, he struck me, he got the better of me, he robbed me"—in those who let go of such thoughts, hatred comes to an end.

The choicesPolicies and divergences

What this edition decided.

The review set these rules and swept the whole work for each.

  • Nothing in a verse explains the verse. "Nirvana" appears only where the Pali has the word. Müller's parenthetical glosses, which the draft had written into the lines, were removed.
  • Where Müller is wrong, the edition follows the Pali. Brahma the god is not "Brahman".
  • Each Pali term has one English word. Appamada is "heedfulness" and its opposite is "heedlessness". The Buddha's dhamma is "the teaching". The sangha is "the Order". A bhikkhu is a monk.
  • "Evil" or "harm", not "sin". The Pali word carries no guilt before a god.
  • The verses keep Müller's numbering, so the two can be compared.

Where it differs from the older translations

The edition records 79 divergences: places where it departs from the older English, each with its reason. Here are 7. The full record is in the app, one tap from the text.

manopubbangama dhamma

Verses 1 and 2

Older
"All that we are is the result of what we have thought" (Müller)
Lectio
the mind is chief of all

Müller's opening is loved and it interprets. The edition keeps his cadence as the first line, then gives the Pali's three claims in order: the mind goes first, the mind is chief, things are made of mind. The draft had said the mind "makes" everything. The Pali does not, and the review took it out.

esa dhammo sanantano

Verse 5

Older
"this is an old rule" (Müller)
Lectio
Hatred ends only by love—this is the ancient and unchanging law.

The Pali word means timeless, a law that has always held. "An old rule" is too small for it.

appamada

Verses 21 to 32

Older
"earnestness" (Müller)
Lectio
heedfulness

The chapter turns on this one word. It means unbroken watchfulness. "Earnestness" sounds like sincerity.

gahakaraka

Verses 153 and 154

Older
"maker of the tabernacle" (Müller)
Lectio
House-builder, now you are seen!

By tradition these are the Buddha's words on awakening. The Pali image is a house: builder, rafters, ridgepole. "Tabernacle" brings in a Biblical word the Pali does not have.

atta hi attano natho

Verse 160

Older
"Self is the lord of self, who else could be the lord?" (Müller)
Lectio
You are your own master; who else could be?

The Pali speaks to the listener. Müller makes it an abstract remark about the self.

sabbapapassa akaranam

Verse 183

Older
"Not to commit any sin, to do good" (Müller)
Lectio
Not to do any evil; to cultivate good; to purify one's own mind

The verse Buddhists call the summary of the teaching. "Sin" imports guilt before a god.

sabbe dhamma anatta

Verses 277 to 279

Older
"All forms are unreal" (Müller)
Lectio
All things are without a self

These are the three marks of existence: impermanent, suffering, without a self. Müller's third is a mistranslation, and the edition corrects it.

Still open

What the first review left for the editor to decide or restore.

  • The editor's own read of all twenty-six chapters.
  • The edition's overview still speaks of "the mind that makes the world". That sits a little against the correction to verses 1 and 2.
  • Verse 128, which was missing, has been restored. The provenance should say so.
  • Müller's text in the bundle runs verses 127 and 128 together. That is a fault in how his edition was brought in, to be fixed there.
