The Gospel of Mark, Chapter 12

The Vineyard, the Coin, and the Widow

Chapter 1212 of 16

And he began to speak to them in parables. “A man planted a vineyard. He put a wall around it, dug a pit for the winepress, and built a watchtower. Then he leased it to tenant farmers and went abroad. At harvest time he sent a servant to the tenants to collect his share of the crop. They grabbed him, beat him, and sent him away with nothing. So he sent them another servant. That one they struck on the head and humiliated. He sent another, and that one they killed — and many others, beating some and killing some. He had one left to send, a son he loved. He sent him to them last of all, saying, ‘They'll respect my son.’ But those tenants said to each other, ‘This is the heir. Come on — let's kill him, and the inheritance will be ours.’ So they took him and killed him and threw him out of the vineyard. What will the owner of the vineyard do? He will come and destroy those tenants and give the vineyard to others.

“Haven't you read this scripture? ‘The stone the builders threw away has become the cornerstone. This is the Lord's doing, and it is a wonder in our eyes.’” They wanted to arrest him, because they knew he had told the parable against them — but they were afraid of the crowd. So they left him and went away.

They sent some of the Pharisees and Herodians to him, to trap him in something he said. They came and said to him, “Teacher, we know you're honest and that you don't defer to anyone, because you don't look at people's faces — you teach the way of God truthfully. Is it lawful to pay the tax to Caesar or not? Should we pay, or shouldn't we?” He saw through their act and said to them, “Why are you testing me? Bring me a denarius and let me look at it.” They brought one, and he said to them, “Whose image is this? Whose inscription?” They said to him, “Caesar's.” And Jesus said to them, “Give Caesar back what belongs to Caesar, and give God what belongs to God.” And they were amazed at him.

Then the Sadducees came to him — the ones who say there is no resurrection — and they asked him, “Teacher, Moses wrote for us that if a man's brother dies and leaves a wife but no child, the man should marry the widow and raise up offspring for his brother. There were seven brothers. The first married and died without leaving offspring. The second married her and died, leaving no offspring; and the third the same. None of the seven left offspring. Last of all the woman died too. In the resurrection, when they rise, whose wife will she be? All seven had her.” Jesus said to them, “Isn't this exactly why you're wrong — because you don't know the scriptures or the power of God? When people rise from the dead they don't marry and aren't given in marriage; they are like the angels in heaven. And about the dead being raised — haven't you read in the book of Moses, in the passage about the bush, how God said to him, ‘I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob’? He is not the God of the dead but of the living. You are badly wrong.”

One of the scribes came up and heard them arguing. He saw that Jesus had answered them well, and he asked him, “Which commandment is first of all?” Jesus answered, “The first is: ‘Listen, Israel — the LORD our God, the LORD is one. And you shall love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind and with all your strength.’ The second is this: ‘You shall love your neighbor as yourself.’ There is no commandment greater than these.”

And the scribe said to him, “Well said, teacher. You're right that he is one, and there is no other besides him — and to love him with all your heart and all your understanding and all your strength, and to love your neighbor as yourself, is worth far more than all the burnt offerings and sacrifices.” When Jesus saw that he had answered thoughtfully, he said to him, “You are not far from the kingdom of God.” And after that no one dared to ask him anything.

While he was teaching in the temple, Jesus said, “How can the scribes say that the Messiah is David's son? David himself, by the Holy Spirit, said: ‘The LORD said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand until I put your enemies under your feet.’ David himself calls him Lord — so how can he be his son?” And the large crowd listened to him gladly.

In his teaching he said, “Watch out for the scribes. They like to walk around in long robes, and to be greeted in the marketplaces, and to have the best seats in the synagogues and the places of honor at banquets. They eat up widows' houses and say long prayers for show. They will get a heavier sentence.”

He sat down across from the treasury and watched the crowd putting money into it. Many rich people put in a great deal. And one poor widow came and put in two lepta, which make a quadrans. He called his disciples over and said to them, “I'm telling you the truth: this poor widow has put in more than everyone else putting money into the treasury. They all gave out of what they had left over. She, out of her need, gave everything she had — her whole living.”
