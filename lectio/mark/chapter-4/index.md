# The Gospel of Mark, Chapter 4

What the Sower Went Out to Do

Again he began to teach beside the sea. A crowd gathered around him so large that he got into a boat and sat there on the water, with the whole crowd on the shore facing him. He taught them many things in parables, and in his teaching he said: “Listen. A sower went out to sow. And as he sowed, some seed fell along the path, and the birds came and ate it up. Some fell on rocky ground, where it didn't have much soil, and it came up right away because the soil wasn't deep — but when the sun rose it was scorched, and because it had no root it withered. Some fell among thorns, and the thorns came up and choked it, and it produced nothing. And some fell into good soil, and it came up, and grew, and produced a crop — thirty times over, sixty times over, a hundred times over.” And he said, “If you have ears to hear with, hear.”

When he was alone, the people around him and the twelve asked him about the parables. He said to them, “To you the secret of the kingdom of God has been given. To those outside, everything comes in parables, so that they may look and look and never see, and listen and listen and never understand — so that they won't turn back and be forgiven.”

And he said to them, “You don't understand this parable? Then how will you understand any of them? The sower sows the word. Some people are the path where the word is sown: as soon as they hear it, Satan comes right away and takes away the word that was sown in them. Others are the rocky ground: they hear the word and take it in gladly, right away — but they have no root in themselves, they last a short time, and then, when pressure or persecution comes because of the word, right away they fall away. Others are the ones sown among thorns: they hear the word, but the worries of this life, the lie that wealth tells, and the craving for everything else come in and choke the word, and it produces nothing. And the ones sown in good soil are those who hear the word and take it in and bear a crop — thirty times over, sixty times over, a hundred times over.”

And he said to them, “Does anyone bring in a lamp to put it under a basket, or under the bed? Doesn't it go on a stand? Nothing is hidden except to be brought out; nothing is covered up except to come into the open. If you have ears to hear with, hear.”

And he said to them, “Pay attention to what you hear. The measure you use will be used on you, and more besides. Whoever has will be given more; whoever has nothing will lose even what he has.”

And he said, “The kingdom of God is like this. A man scatters seed on the ground. He sleeps and gets up, night after night, day after day, and the seed sprouts and grows — he has no idea how. The earth produces a crop on its own: first the shoot, then the head, then the full grain in the head. And when the crop is ready, right away he puts in the sickle, because the harvest has come.”

And he said, “What can we compare the kingdom of God to? What picture can we use for it? It's like a mustard seed. When it's sown in the ground it's the smallest of all the seeds on earth — but once it's sown it comes up and becomes the largest of all the garden plants, and puts out branches so big that the birds of the sky can nest in its shade.”

He spoke the word to them in many parables like these, as much as they could take in. He didn't speak to them without a parable, but privately, with his own disciples, he explained everything.

That day, when evening came, he said to them, “Let's cross over to the other side.” So they left the crowd and took him along, just as he was, in the boat. Other boats were with him. A fierce squall came up, and the waves were breaking over the boat, so that it was already filling. He was in the stern, asleep on the cushion. They woke him and said, “Teacher, don't you care that we're going down?” He got up, gave an order to the wind, and said to the sea: “Quiet. Be muzzled.” The wind dropped, and everything went dead calm. He said to them, “Why are you so afraid? Do you still have no faith?” And they were terrified, and said to each other, “Who is this? Even the wind and the sea do what he says.”
