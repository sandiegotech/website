The Gospel of Mark, Chapter 14

The Supper, the Garden, the Courtyard

Chapter 1414 of 16

It was two days before the Passover and the Festival of Unleavened Bread, and the chief priests and the scribes were looking for a way to arrest him by stealth and kill him. They kept saying, “Not during the festival — the people might riot.”

He was in Bethany, at the house of Simon the leper, reclining at the table, when a woman came in with an alabaster jar of pure nard, very costly. She broke the jar and poured it over his head. Some of them were indignant, and said to each other: “What was the point of wasting the perfume? It could have been sold for more than three hundred denarii and given to the poor.” And they scolded her. But Jesus said, “Leave her alone. Why are you giving her trouble? She has done a fine thing for me. You always have the poor with you, and you can do good for them whenever you want. You will not always have me. She did what she could. She anointed my body ahead of time, for burial. I'm telling you the truth: wherever the good news is announced in the whole world, what she has done will be told too, in memory of her.”

And Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve, went off to the chief priests to hand him over to them. When they heard it they were delighted, and promised to give him money. And he started looking for the right moment to hand him over.

On the first day of Unleavened Bread, when the Passover lamb was killed, his disciples said to him, “Where do you want us to go and get things ready for you to eat the Passover?” He sent two of his disciples and said to them, “Go into the city, and a man carrying a jar of water will meet you. Follow him. Wherever he goes in, tell the owner of the house, ‘The Teacher says: Where is my guest room, where I can eat the Passover with my disciples?’ He'll show you a large upstairs room, furnished and ready. Get things ready for us there.” The disciples went out, came into the city, found everything just as he had told them, and got the Passover ready.

When evening came he arrived with the twelve. And while they were reclining at the table eating, Jesus said, “I'm telling you the truth: one of you is going to hand me over — one who is eating with me.” They started to grieve, and to say to him one after another, “Not me?” He said to them, “One of the twelve — one dipping into the bowl with me. The Son of Man goes as it is written about him. But it will be terrible for the man who hands the Son of Man over. It would have been better for him if he had never been born.”

While they were eating, he took bread, blessed it, broke it, and gave it to them, and said, “Take it. This is my body.” And he took a cup, gave thanks, and gave it to them, and they all drank from it. And he said to them, “This is my blood of the covenant, poured out for many. I'm telling you the truth: I will never drink from the fruit of the vine again until the day I drink it new in the kingdom of God.” And after singing the hymn they went out to the Mount of Olives.

And Jesus said to them, “Every one of you will fall away, because it is written: ‘I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep will be scattered.’ But after I am raised, I will go ahead of you into Galilee.” Peter said to him, “Even if everyone else falls away, I won't.” And Jesus said to him, “I'm telling you the truth: today, tonight, before the rooster crows twice, you will disown me three times.” But Peter kept insisting, “Even if I have to die with you, I will never disown you.” And they all said the same.

They came to a place named Gethsemane, and he said to his disciples, “Sit here while I pray.” He took Peter and James and John with him, and he began to be shaken and deeply distressed. And he said to them, “My soul is crushed with grief, to the point of death. Stay here and keep watch.” He went on a little farther, fell on the ground, and prayed that if it were possible the hour might pass from him. And he said, “Abba — Father — everything is possible for you. Take this cup away from me. But not what I want. What you want.”

He came back and found them sleeping, and said to Peter, “Simon, are you asleep? You couldn't keep watch for one hour? Stay awake and pray, so you won't come to the test. The spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak.” He went away again and prayed, saying the same words. And when he came back he found them sleeping again — their eyes were heavy — and they didn't know what to say to him. He came a third time and said to them, “Are you still sleeping and resting? That's enough. The hour has come. Look — the Son of Man is being handed over into the hands of sinners. Get up, let's go. Look, the one handing me over is here.”

Right away, while he was still speaking, Judas came — one of the twelve — and with him a crowd carrying swords and clubs, sent from the chief priests and the scribes and the elders. The one handing him over had arranged a signal with them: “The one I kiss is the man. Arrest him and lead him away under guard.” When he arrived, right away he went up to Jesus and said, “Rabbi,” and kissed him. And they laid hands on him and arrested him. One of the bystanders drew a sword and struck the high priest's servant, cutting off his ear. And Jesus said to them, “Did you have to come out with swords and clubs to take me, as if I were a bandit? Day after day I was with you in the temple teaching, and you didn't arrest me. But let the scriptures be fulfilled.” And they all abandoned him and ran.

One young man was following him, wearing nothing but a linen cloth. They grabbed him, and he left the linen cloth behind and ran away naked.

They led Jesus away to the high priest, and all the chief priests and the elders and the scribes came together. Peter followed him at a distance, right into the high priest's courtyard, and he sat with the guards, warming himself at the fire. The chief priests and the whole council were looking for testimony against Jesus to put him to death, and they couldn't find any. Many gave false testimony against him, and their testimony didn't agree. Some stood up and gave false testimony against him: “We heard him say, ‘I will destroy this temple made with hands, and in three days I will build another, not made with hands.’” And even on that their testimony didn't agree.

Then the high priest stood up in front of them all and questioned Jesus: “Have you no answer? What is this testimony they're bringing against you?” But he was silent and answered nothing. Again the high priest questioned him: “Are you the Messiah, the Son of the Blessed One?” And Jesus said, “I am. And you will see the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of the Power, and coming with the clouds of heaven.” The high priest tore his clothes and said, “What further need do we have of witnesses? You heard the blasphemy. What is your verdict?” And they all condemned him as deserving death. Some began to spit on him, and to cover his face and beat him and say, “Prophesy!” And the guards took him and struck him.

While Peter was down in the courtyard, one of the high priest's servant girls came by. When she saw Peter warming himself she looked straight at him and said, “You were with Jesus too — the man from Nazareth.” But he denied it: “I don't know him. I don't understand what you're talking about.” And he went out to the entryway. The servant girl saw him and started telling the bystanders again, “This man is one of them.” And again he denied it. A little later the bystanders said to Peter again, “You really are one of them. You're a Galilean.” And he began to curse and swear, “I don't know this man you're talking about.” And right away a rooster crowed a second time. And Peter remembered what Jesus had said to him: “Before the rooster crows twice, you will disown me three times.” And he broke down and wept.
