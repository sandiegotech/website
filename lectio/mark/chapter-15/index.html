The Gospel of Mark, Chapter 15

The Cross

Chapter 1515 of 16

Right away in the morning the chief priests made their plans with the elders and the scribes and the whole council. They tied Jesus up, led him away, and handed him over to Pilate. And Pilate questioned him: “Are you the King of the Jews?” He answered him, “You say so.” The chief priests kept accusing him of many things. So Pilate questioned him again: “Have you no answer? Look how many charges they're bringing against you.” But Jesus said nothing more, and Pilate was amazed.

At the festival he used to release for them one prisoner, whichever one they asked for. There was a man called Barabbas, in prison with the rebels who had committed murder during the uprising. The crowd came up and began asking Pilate to do what he usually did for them. Pilate answered them, “Do you want me to release the King of the Jews for you?” — because he knew the chief priests had handed him over out of envy. But the chief priests stirred up the crowd to have him release Barabbas for them instead. Pilate answered them again, “Then what do you want me to do with the man you call the King of the Jews?” And they shouted back, “Crucify him!” Pilate said to them, “Why? What has he done wrong?” And they shouted all the louder, “Crucify him!” So Pilate, wanting to satisfy the crowd, released Barabbas for them; and after having Jesus flogged, he handed him over to be crucified.

The soldiers led him away into the courtyard — that is, the governor's headquarters — and called the whole company together. They dressed him in purple, twisted together a crown of thorns, and put it on him. And they started saluting him: “Hail, King of the Jews!” They kept striking his head with a reed and spitting on him, and they knelt down and bowed to him. And when they had finished mocking him, they took the purple off him and put his own clothes back on him. Then they led him out to crucify him.

They forced a passerby coming in from the fields to carry his cross — Simon of Cyrene, the father of Alexander and Rufus. And they brought him to the place called Golgotha, which means Place of the Skull. They offered him wine mixed with myrrh, but he wouldn't take it. And they crucified him, and divided up his clothes, casting lots for them to decide who would take what. It was the third hour when they crucified him. The inscription of the charge against him read: THE KING OF THE JEWS. And with him they crucified two bandits, one on his right and one on his left.

The people passing by hurled insults at him, shaking their heads and saying, “Ha! You who would destroy the temple and build it in three days — save yourself and come down from that cross!” In the same way the chief priests, with the scribes, mocked him among themselves: “He saved others. He can't save himself. Let the Messiah, the King of Israel, come down from the cross now, so we can see it and believe.” And the men crucified with him taunted him too.

When the sixth hour came, darkness fell over the whole land until the ninth hour. And at the ninth hour Jesus cried out in a loud voice, “Eloi, Eloi, lema sabachthani?” — which means, “My God, my God, why have you abandoned me?” Some of the bystanders heard it and said, “Listen — he's calling Elijah.” Someone ran and filled a sponge with sour wine, put it on a stick, and gave it to him to drink, saying, “Let's see whether Elijah comes to take him down.” And Jesus let out a loud cry and breathed his last.

And the curtain of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom. And when the centurion standing there facing him saw the way he breathed his last, he said, “Truly this man was God's Son.” Some women were watching from a distance. Among them were Mary Magdalene, and Mary the mother of James the younger and of Joses, and Salome — who had followed him and looked after him when he was in Galilee — and many other women who had come up with him to Jerusalem.

It was already evening, and since it was the day of Preparation — the day before the Sabbath — Joseph of Arimathea, a respected member of the council who was himself waiting for the kingdom of God, went boldly to Pilate and asked for the body of Jesus. Pilate was surprised that he was already dead. He summoned the centurion and asked him whether Jesus had been dead for long, and when he learned it from the centurion, he granted the body to Joseph. Joseph bought a linen cloth, took him down, wrapped him in the linen, and laid him in a tomb cut out of rock, and rolled a stone against the entrance of the tomb. And Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses were watching where he was laid.
