The Gospel of Mark, Chapter 11

Into the City, and Into the Temple

Chapter 1111 of 16

When they were getting close to Jerusalem, near Bethphage and Bethany at the Mount of Olives, he sent two of his disciples ahead and said to them, “Go into the village over there. As you enter it, right away you'll find a colt tied up that nobody has ever ridden. Untie it and bring it. And if anyone says to you, ‘Why are you doing that?’ — say, ‘The Lord needs it, and he'll send it back here right away.’” They went and found a colt out in the street, tied at a doorway, and they untied it. Some of the people standing there said, “What are you doing, untying that colt?” They said what Jesus had told them to say, and the people let them go.

They brought the colt to Jesus and threw their coats over it, and he sat on it. Many people spread their coats on the road, and others spread branches they had cut in the fields. And the ones in front and the ones behind were shouting: “Hosanna! Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord! Blessed is the coming kingdom of our father David! Hosanna in the highest!” And he came into Jerusalem, into the temple. He looked around at everything, and since it was already late, he went out to Bethany with the twelve.

The next day, as they were leaving Bethany, he was hungry. He saw a fig tree in the distance with leaves on it, and he went to see whether he could find anything on it. When he got there he found nothing but leaves — it wasn't the season for figs. And he said to it, “May no one ever eat fruit from you again.” And his disciples heard him.

They came to Jerusalem, and he went into the temple and began driving out the ones selling and buying there. He overturned the tables of the money changers and the seats of the ones selling doves, and he wouldn't let anyone carry goods through the temple. And he taught them: “Isn't it written, ‘My house will be called a house of prayer for all the nations’? But you have made it a hideout for bandits.” The chief priests and the scribes heard it, and they started looking for a way to destroy him — because they were afraid of him, since the whole crowd was astonished at his teaching. And when evening came, they went out of the city.

In the morning, as they were passing by, they saw the fig tree withered from the roots. Peter remembered and said to him, “Rabbi — look, the fig tree you cursed has withered.” And Jesus said to them, “Have faith in God. I'm telling you the truth: if anyone says to this mountain, ‘Get up and throw yourself into the sea,’ and doesn't doubt in his heart but trusts that what he says will happen — it will be done for him. So I'm telling you: whatever you ask for in prayer, trust that you have received it, and it will be yours. And whenever you stand praying, if you have anything against anyone, forgive — so that your Father in heaven will forgive you your wrongs too.”

They came into Jerusalem again, and as he was walking in the temple the chief priests and the scribes and the elders came up to him and said, “By what authority are you doing these things? Who gave you the authority to do them?” Jesus said to them, “I'll ask you one question. Answer me, and I'll tell you by what authority I do these things. John's baptism — was it from heaven, or from human beings? Answer me.” And they argued it out among themselves: “If we say ‘From heaven,’ he'll say, ‘Then why didn't you believe him?’ But if we say ‘From human beings’ —” they were afraid of the crowd, because everyone held that John was really a prophet. So they answered Jesus, “We don't know.” And Jesus said to them, “Then I won't tell you by what authority I do these things either.”
