# The Gospel of Mark, Chapter 6

No Honor at Home; Bread in the Wilderness

He left there and went to his hometown, and his disciples followed him. When the Sabbath came he began teaching in the synagogue, and many who heard him were astonished. “Where does this man get all this? What's this wisdom he's been given, and these miracles his hands are doing? Isn't this the carpenter, Mary's son, the brother of James and Joses and Judas and Simon? Aren't his sisters here with us?” And they took offense at him. Jesus said to them, “A prophet is not without honor — except in his hometown, among his own relatives, and in his own house.” And he couldn't do any miracle there, except that he put his hands on a few sick people and healed them. He was amazed at their lack of trust. And he went around the villages teaching.

He called the twelve and began sending them out two by two, and he gave them authority over unclean spirits. He told them to take nothing for the road but a staff — no bread, no bag, no money in their belts — to wear sandals but not to put on a second shirt. And he said to them, “Wherever you go into a house, stay there until you leave that place. And if anywhere won't welcome you and won't listen to you, when you leave, shake the dust off your feet as a testimony against them.” So they went out and announced that people should turn their lives around. They drove out many demons, and anointed many sick people with oil and healed them.

King Herod heard about it, because Jesus' name had become well known. People were saying, “John the Baptizer has been raised from the dead — that's why these powers are at work in him.” Others said, “He's Elijah.” Others said, “He's a prophet, like one of the old prophets.” But when Herod heard it he said, “John, the one I beheaded — he's been raised.”

Herod himself had sent men to arrest John and had him tied up in prison, because of Herodias, his brother Philip's wife, whom Herod had married. John had been telling Herod, “You're not allowed to have your brother's wife.” So Herodias had a grudge against him and wanted to kill him, and couldn't — because Herod was afraid of John. He knew John was a righteous and holy man, and he protected him. When he listened to him he was deeply disturbed, and yet he liked listening to him.

The opportunity came on Herod's birthday, when he gave a banquet for his officials and officers and the leading men of Galilee. Herodias's daughter came in and danced, and she delighted Herod and his guests. The king said to the girl, “Ask me for anything you want, and I'll give it to you.” And he swore to her, “Whatever you ask me for, I'll give you — up to half my kingdom.” She went out and said to her mother, “What should I ask for?” And she said, “The head of John the Baptizer.” Right away she hurried back in to the king and made her request: “I want you to give me, right now, the head of John the Baptizer on a platter.”

The king was heartsick. But because of his oaths and his guests he didn't want to refuse her. Right away the king sent an executioner with orders to bring John's head. The man went and beheaded him in the prison, brought his head on a platter, and gave it to the girl, and the girl gave it to her mother. When John's disciples heard about it, they came and took his body and laid it in a tomb.

The apostles gathered around Jesus and reported to him everything they had done and everything they had taught. He said to them, “Come away by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a while” — because so many people were coming and going that they didn't even have a chance to eat. So they went away in the boat to a deserted place by themselves. But people saw them going, and many recognized them, and they ran there on foot from all the towns and got there ahead of them. When Jesus came ashore he saw a huge crowd, and his heart went out to them, because they were like sheep without a shepherd. And he began to teach them many things.

It was getting late, and his disciples came to him and said, “This is a deserted place, and it's already late. Send them away, so they can go to the farms and villages around here and buy themselves something to eat.” He answered them, “You give them something to eat.” They said to him, “Are we supposed to go and spend two hundred denarii on bread and give it to them to eat?” He said to them, “How many loaves do you have? Go and see.” When they found out they said, “Five — and two fish.”

He told them to get everyone to sit down in groups on the green grass. So they sat down in rows, in hundreds and fifties. He took the five loaves and the two fish, looked up to the sky, blessed and broke the loaves, and kept giving them to his disciples to set in front of the people; and he divided the two fish among them all. Everyone ate and had enough. And they picked up twelve baskets full of broken pieces, and of the fish. There were five thousand men who had eaten the loaves.

Right away he made his disciples get into the boat and go on ahead of him to Bethsaida, while he sent the crowd away. After saying goodbye to them he went up the mountain to pray. When evening came the boat was out in the middle of the sea, and he was alone on land. He saw them straining at the oars, because the wind was against them. And somewhere around the fourth watch of the night he came toward them, walking on the sea. He meant to pass by them. But when they saw him walking on the sea they thought he was a ghost, and they cried out — all of them saw him, and they were terrified. Right away he spoke to them: “Take heart. It's me. Don't be afraid.” And he got into the boat with them, and the wind dropped. They were completely astounded, because they hadn't understood about the loaves; their hearts were hardened.

When they had crossed over they came ashore at Gennesaret and tied up. When they got out of the boat, people recognized him right away. They ran all over that whole region and began carrying the sick on mats to wherever they heard he was. And wherever he went — villages, towns, farms — they laid the sick in the marketplaces and begged him to let them touch even the edge of his coat. And everyone who touched it was saved.
