The Gospel of Mark, Chapter 9

The Mountain, and the Argument on the Road

Chapter 99 of 16

And he said to them, “I'm telling you the truth: some of the people standing here will not taste death before they see the kingdom of God come in power.” Six days later Jesus took Peter and James and John and led them up a high mountain by themselves, alone. And he was transformed in front of them. His clothes became a blinding white, whiter than anyone on earth could bleach them. And Elijah appeared to them, with Moses, and they were talking with Jesus. Peter spoke up and said to Jesus, “Rabbi, it's good that we're here. Let us put up three shelters — one for you, one for Moses, one for Elijah.” He didn't know what to say; they were terrified. Then a cloud came and overshadowed them, and a voice came out of the cloud: “This is my Son, the one I love. Listen to him.” And suddenly, looking around, they saw no one with them anymore — only Jesus.

On the way down the mountain he ordered them not to tell anyone what they had seen until the Son of Man had risen from the dead. They held on to that, arguing with each other about what rising from the dead could mean. And they asked him, “Why do the scribes say Elijah has to come first?” He said to them, “Elijah does come first, and he restores everything. Then why is it written that the Son of Man must suffer a great deal and be treated as nothing? But I'm telling you, Elijah has already come, and they did whatever they wanted to him — just as it is written about him.”

When they came back to the other disciples they saw a large crowd around them, and scribes arguing with them. Right away, as soon as the whole crowd saw Jesus, they were overcome with awe, and they ran to greet him. He asked them, “What are you arguing about with them?” Someone in the crowd answered him, “Teacher, I brought you my son. He has a spirit that has taken his speech. Wherever it seizes him it throws him down, and he foams at the mouth and grinds his teeth and goes rigid. I asked your disciples to drive it out, and they couldn't.” He answered them, “You faithless generation — how long do I have to be with you? How long do I have to put up with you? Bring him to me.” They brought the boy to him, and when the spirit saw Jesus, right away it convulsed him, and he fell on the ground and rolled around, foaming.

Jesus asked his father, “How long has this been happening to him?” He said, “Since he was a child. It has often thrown him into fire and into water to destroy him. But if you can do anything, have pity on us and help us.” Jesus said to him, “‘If you can’ — everything is possible for the one who trusts.” Right away the boy's father cried out, “I do trust. Help me where I don't.”

When Jesus saw a crowd running together, he rebuked the unclean spirit: “You spirit that takes speech and hearing, I order you — come out of him, and never go into him again.” It shrieked, convulsed him violently, and came out. And the boy looked like a corpse, so that most of them said he was dead. But Jesus took him by the hand and lifted him up, and he stood. When Jesus had gone into the house, his disciples asked him privately, “Why couldn't we drive it out?” He said to them, “This kind can't be driven out by anything but prayer.”

They left there and went on through Galilee, and he didn't want anyone to know — because he was teaching his disciples. He was telling them, “The Son of Man is being handed over into human hands. They will kill him, and three days after he is killed he will rise.” But they didn't understand what he was saying, and they were afraid to ask him.

They came to Capernaum. When he was in the house he asked them, “What were you arguing about on the road?” They kept quiet — because on the road they had been arguing with each other about who was the greatest. He sat down, called the twelve, and said to them, “If anyone wants to be first, he has to be last of all, and the servant of all.” And he took a child and stood him in the middle of them, and he put his arms around the child and said to them, “Whoever welcomes one child like this in my name welcomes me; and whoever welcomes me isn't welcoming me but the one who sent me.”

John said to him, “Teacher, we saw someone driving out demons in your name, and we tried to stop him, because he wasn't following us.” Jesus said, “Don't stop him. Nobody who does a miracle in my name can turn around and speak evil of me. Whoever isn't against us is for us. And whoever gives you a cup of water to drink because you belong to the Messiah — I'm telling you the truth, he will not lose his reward.”

“And whoever trips up one of these little ones who trust me — it would be better for him if he were thrown into the sea with a millstone around his neck. If your hand trips you up, cut it off. It's better to enter life crippled than to have two hands and go into Gehenna, into the fire that never goes out. If your foot trips you up, cut it off. It's better to enter life lame than to have two feet and be thrown into Gehenna. And if your eye trips you up, tear it out. It's better to enter the kingdom of God with one eye than to have two eyes and be thrown into Gehenna, where their worm does not die and the fire is not put out. Everyone will be salted with fire. Salt is good — but if the salt goes flat, what will you season it with? Have salt in yourselves, and be at peace with each other.”
