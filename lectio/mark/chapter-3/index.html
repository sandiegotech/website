The Gospel of Mark, Chapter 3

The Twelve, and the Divided House

Chapter 33 of 16

He went into the synagogue again, and a man with a withered hand was there. They were watching him closely to see whether he would heal the man on the Sabbath, so they could bring a charge against him. He said to the man with the withered hand, “Stand up, here in front.” Then he said to them, “Is it allowed on the Sabbath to do good, or to do harm? To save a life, or to kill?” They said nothing. He looked around at them in anger, grieved at how hard their hearts were, and he said to the man, “Stretch out your hand.” He stretched it out, and his hand was restored. And the Pharisees went out and right away started plotting with the Herodians how to destroy him.

Jesus withdrew with his disciples to the sea, and a great crowd followed from Galilee. And from Judea, Jerusalem, Idumea, the far side of the Jordan, and the region around Tyre and Sidon — a great crowd came to him, because they had heard what he was doing. He told his disciples to keep a small boat ready for him, so the crowd wouldn't crush him. He had healed so many that everyone with an affliction was pushing forward to touch him. And whenever the unclean spirits saw him, they fell down in front of him and screamed, “You are the Son of God!” And he warned them sharply not to make him known.

He went up the mountain and called the ones he wanted, and they came to him. He appointed twelve — he named them apostles — to be with him, and to be sent out to preach, and to have authority to drive out demons. So he appointed the twelve: Simon, to whom he gave the name Peter; James son of Zebedee and his brother John, to whom he gave the name Boanerges, which means Sons of Thunder; Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Cananaean, and Judas Iscariot, the one who handed him over.

Then he went home, and the crowd gathered again, so that they couldn't even eat. When his family heard about it, they went out to seize him — people were saying he was out of his mind.

And the scribes who had come down from Jerusalem were saying, “He has Beelzebul,” and, “He drives out demons by the ruler of the demons.” So he called them over and spoke to them in parables: “How can Satan drive out Satan? If a kingdom is divided against itself, that kingdom can't stand. And if a house is divided against itself, that house can't stand. If Satan has risen against himself and is divided, he can't stand — that's the end of him. But no one can walk into a strong man's house and take his things unless he first ties up the strong man. Then he can rob the house.”

“I'm telling you the truth: people will be forgiven everything — every sin, and whatever blasphemies they speak. But whoever blasphemes against the Holy Spirit never has forgiveness; he is guilty of an eternal sin.” He said this because they were saying, “He has an unclean spirit.”

Then his mother and his brothers came. They stood outside and sent word in to him, calling for him. A crowd was sitting around him, and they told him, “Look — your mother and your brothers are outside, asking for you.” He answered them, “Who is my mother? Who are my brothers?” And looking around at the people sitting in a circle about him, he said, “Here is my mother, here are my brothers. Whoever does what God wants is my brother and sister and mother.”
