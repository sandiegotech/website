# The Gospel of Mark, Chapter 7

What Actually Makes a Person Unclean

The Pharisees and some of the scribes who had come from Jerusalem gathered around him, and they saw that some of his disciples were eating with defiled hands — that is, unwashed. (The Pharisees, and all the Jews, don't eat unless they wash their hands a certain way, keeping the tradition of the elders; and when they come from the market they don't eat unless they wash. There are many other traditions they keep — the washing of cups and jugs and copper pots.) So the Pharisees and the scribes asked him, “Why don't your disciples live according to the tradition of the elders, instead of eating with defiled hands?”

He said to them, “Isaiah prophesied rightly about you hypocrites. As it stands written: ‘These people honor me with their lips, but their hearts are far away from me. What they teach as doctrine is nothing but human rules.’ You let go of God's command and hold on to human tradition.”

And he said to them, “You have a fine way of setting aside God's command in order to keep your own tradition. Moses said, ‘Honor your father and your mother,’ and, ‘Anyone who speaks evil of father or mother must die.’ But you say that if a man tells his father or mother, ‘Whatever help you might have had from me is Corban’ — that is, an offering — then you no longer let him do anything for his father or mother. So you cancel God's word with the tradition you've handed down. And you do plenty of things like that.”

He called the crowd to him again and said, “All of you, listen to me, and understand. There is nothing outside a person that can make him unclean by going into him. What comes out of a person is what makes him unclean.”

When he had left the crowd and gone into the house, his disciples asked him about the parable. He said to them, “Are you as slow as the rest? Don't you see that nothing that goes into a person from outside can make him unclean, because it doesn't go into his heart but into his stomach, and then out into the latrine?” (Saying this, he declared all foods clean.)

And he said, “What comes out of a person is what makes him unclean. From inside — out of the human heart — come the evil thoughts: sexual immorality, theft, murder, adultery, greed, malice, deceit, indecency, envy, slander, arrogance, folly. All these evils come from inside, and they make a person unclean.”

He got up and left there for the region of Tyre. He went into a house and didn't want anyone to know, but he couldn't stay hidden. Right away a woman whose little daughter had an unclean spirit heard about him and came and fell at his feet. The woman was Greek, Syrophoenician by birth, and she kept asking him to drive the demon out of her daughter.

He said to her, “Let the children eat their fill first. It isn't right to take the children's bread and throw it to the dogs.” She answered him, “Lord — even the dogs under the table eat the children's crumbs.” And he said to her, “For saying that, go — the demon has left your daughter.” She went home and found the child lying on the bed, and the demon gone.

Then he left the region of Tyre and went through Sidon to the Sea of Galilee, right through the middle of the Decapolis region. They brought him a man who was deaf and could barely speak, and they begged him to put his hand on him. He took the man away from the crowd, off by himself, put his fingers in the man's ears, spat, and touched his tongue. He looked up to the sky, groaned, and said to him, “Ephphatha” — that is, “Be opened.” And the man's ears were opened, the knot of his tongue was untied, and he began speaking clearly. Jesus ordered them not to tell anyone. But the more he ordered them, the more they kept spreading it. They were astounded beyond measure. “He has done everything well,” they said. “He even makes the deaf hear and the mute speak.”
