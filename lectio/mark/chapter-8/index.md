# The Gospel of Mark, Chapter 8

Who Do You Say I Am

In those days there was another large crowd with nothing to eat. Jesus called his disciples and said to them, “My heart goes out to these people. They've been with me three days now and they have nothing to eat. If I send them home hungry they'll collapse on the road — some of them have come a long way.” His disciples answered him, “Where out here in this wilderness could anyone get enough bread to feed them?” He asked them, “How many loaves do you have?” They said, “Seven.” He told the crowd to sit down on the ground. He took the seven loaves, gave thanks, broke them, and kept giving them to his disciples to set out, and they set them in front of the crowd. They also had a few small fish, and after blessing them he told the disciples to set those out too. The people ate and had enough, and they picked up seven basketfuls of leftover pieces. There were about four thousand of them. He sent them away.

Right away he got into the boat with his disciples and went to the district of Dalmanutha. The Pharisees came out and started arguing with him. They were testing him, demanding a sign from heaven. He sighed from deep inside and said, “Why does this generation demand a sign? I'm telling you the truth: no sign will be given to this generation.” And he left them, got back in the boat, and went to the other side.

The disciples had forgotten to bring bread; they had only one loaf with them in the boat. And he warned them, “Watch out — keep your eyes open for the yeast of the Pharisees and the yeast of Herod.” They started arguing with each other about having no bread. Jesus knew it and said to them, “Why are you arguing about having no bread? Do you still not see it? Do you still not understand? Are your hearts hardened? You have eyes — don't you see? You have ears — don't you hear? Don't you remember? When I broke the five loaves for the five thousand, how many basketfuls of pieces did you pick up?” They said, “Twelve.” “And when I broke the seven for the four thousand, how many basketfuls did you pick up?” They said, “Seven.” And he said to them, “Do you still not understand?”

They came to Bethsaida, and people brought him a blind man and begged Jesus to touch him. He took the blind man by the hand and led him outside the village. He spat on the man's eyes, put his hands on him, and asked him, “Do you see anything?” The man looked up and said, “I see people — they look like trees, walking around.” Then Jesus put his hands on the man's eyes again, and he looked hard, and his sight came back, and he saw everything clearly. Jesus sent him home and said, “Don't even go into the village.”

Jesus went off with his disciples to the villages around Caesarea Philippi, and on the road he asked them, “Who do people say I am?” They told him, “John the Baptizer. Others say Elijah. Others say one of the prophets.” And he asked them, “But you — who do you say I am?” Peter answered, “You are the Messiah.” And he warned them not to tell anyone about him.

And he began to teach them that the Son of Man had to suffer a great deal, and be rejected by the elders and the chief priests and the scribes, and be killed, and after three days rise. He said all this out in the open. And Peter took him aside and started rebuking him. But Jesus turned around, looked at his disciples, and rebuked Peter: “Get behind me, Satan. You're not thinking God's thoughts — you're thinking human ones.”

He called the crowd to him, along with his disciples, and said to them, “If anyone wants to come after me, he has to deny himself, and pick up his cross, and follow me. Whoever wants to save his life will lose it, and whoever loses his life for me and for the good news will save it. What good is it for a man to gain the whole world and forfeit his life? What could a man give in exchange for his life? If anyone is ashamed of me and my words in this adulterous and sinful generation, the Son of Man will be ashamed of him too, when he comes in his Father's glory with the holy angels.”
