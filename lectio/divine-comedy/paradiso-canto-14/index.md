# The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto XIV

Water in a round bowl moves from the center  
to the rim, and from the rim back to the center,  
struck either from without or from within.

This dropped into my mind the very moment  
Thomas fell silent and his glorious life  
gave up its speaking;

for the same likeness carried on, passing from his words  
into the words of Beatrice, whom it pleased,  
after him, to begin this way:

"This man has need — he does not tell you so,  
not with his voice, not even in his thought —  
of tracing one more truth down to its root.

Tell him: the light with which your substance flowers,  
will it stay with you eternally  
exactly as it shines on you right now?

And if it stays, then tell him in what way,  
once you are clothed in your bodies again  
and made visible, it will not wound your sight."

The way dancers in a ring, caught up  
and carried by a sudden surge of gladness,  
will lift their voices and quicken in their step —

so, at that prompt and reverent request,  
the holy circles showed a fresh delight  
in how they wheeled, in the wonder of their song.

Anyone who grieves that here we die  
so that we may live up there has never seen  
the refreshment of the eternal rain.

That One and Two and Three who lives forever  
and reigns forever in Three and Two and One,  
without bound, and binding all things round —

three separate times was sung by each of those  
bright spirits, and to such a melody  
it would be just reward for any merit.

And in the brightest light of the inner ring,  
the smaller one, I heard a modest voice —  
perhaps the way the Angel's was to Mary —

answer: "As long as the festival of Heaven  
shall last, so long our love will keep  
radiating this garment around us.

Its brightness keeps the measure of our ardor,  
the ardor the measure of our seeing, and the seeing  
as great as the grace it is given beyond its worth.

When our flesh, made holy and made glorious,  
is put back on, then what we are will please  
still more, being at last entire and whole;

for the light the Highest Good gives us for free —  
the light that lets us look on Him — will grow,  
and with it grows whatever we can hold;

and so the seeing cannot help but increase,  
and the ardor kindled from that seeing increase,  
and the radiance pouring out of that increase.

But like a coal that throws a living flame  
and by its own white core outshines the flame,  
so that the coal stays plainly what it is —

just so this brightness wrapping us right now  
will be outshone, in look, by the very flesh  
that to this day the earth still keeps covered.

Nor will so great a splendor wear us out:  
the body's organs will be strong enough  
for everything that has the power to please us."

So quick and eager, both choirs seemed to me  
to say their "Amen" that they plainly showed  
how much they longed for their own dead bodies —

and not for themselves alone, perhaps, but for  
the mothers, for the fathers, for the others  
dear to them before they turned to deathless flame.

And look — all around, in even brightness,  
a new radiance rose above the rest,  
like a horizon slowly clearing toward dawn.

And as, at the first rise of evening, new  
appearances begin across the sky,  
so the sight seems real, and seems unreal,

it seemed to me that new presences began  
to come into view there, forming a circle  
outside the bounds of the other two.

O the true sparkling of the Holy Spirit —  
how sudden, how white-hot it grew  
before my eyes, that beaten could not bear it!

But Beatrice, so beautiful and smiling,  
appeared to me, that I must leave her  
among the sights my memory could not keep.

There my eyes recovered strength to lift  
themselves again, and I beheld myself  
borne up to higher blessedness, my Lady alone with me.

I knew well that I had been raised higher  
by the kindled smiling of the star itself,  
which seemed to me redder than before.

With all my heart, and in that one tongue  
that is the same in all, I made to God  
such burnt offering as the new grace asked of me;

and the fire of that sacrifice was not yet  
spent within my breast before I knew  
the offering had been received, and the omen good —

for splendors flashed before me, in twin rays,  
with so much luster, and so red, that I said:  
"O Helios who decks them out like this!"

As the Galaxy, set with lights both lesser  
and greater, glimmers between the world's two poles  
and makes even wise men doubt what they are seeing,

so, constellated deep within the body of Mars,  
those rays traced out the venerable Sign  
that quartering lines make when they cross in a circle.

Here my memory outruns my skill;  
for on that cross Christ flashed out like lightning,  
and I can find no worthy likeness for it —

but anyone who takes his cross and follows Christ  
will yet forgive me what I leave unsaid,  
seeing that dawn break, and Christ lightening through it.

From arm to arm, between the top and base,  
lights were moving, flaring keenly bright  
as they crossed and passed each other on the way.

Just so, level and slanting, fast and slow,  
we watch the motes of bodies, long and short,  
renewing always how they meet the eye,

drift across the sunbeam that sometimes streaks the shade  
that people, to defend themselves,  
contrive with craft and art.

And as a lute and harp, tuned to agree  
with many strings, will make a sweet faint chiming  
for the one who cannot pick the single notes,

so from the lights that there appeared to me  
a melody gathered up along the cross  
and rapt me away, though I caught no hymn.

I knew well it was lofty praise, because  
there came to me "Rise" and "Conquer" — coming  
as to one who hears and cannot understand.

I fell so deep in love with it just then  
that up to that hour there had been nothing  
that ever bound me with such sweet bonds.

Maybe my words seem a little too bold,  
putting off the delight of those fair eyes  
in which, gazing, all my longing comes to rest;

but whoever considers that the living seals  
of every beauty gain in power the higher they rise,  
and that there I had not yet turned back to those,

can pardon me for what I accuse myself of  
to excuse myself, and see that I speak true:  
for the holy joy is not left out of this —

it is only that, rising higher, it grows more pure.
