The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto X

Paradiso: Canto X77 of 100

Gazing upon his Son with all the Love
that each of them breathes out eternally,
the first and unspeakable Power

made everything that turns through mind and space
with such an order that no one can look
and not taste something of him in the sight.

Lift up your eyes with me, then, Reader — lift them
to the high wheeling spheres, to that one place
where the one motion crosses on the other,

and there begin to take your joy in watching
the art of that great Master, who so loves
his own work that his gaze never leaves it.

See how, from there, the slanting circle branches —
the band that carries the planets on their rounds
to answer the world that calls on them for help;

and if their road were not bent on this tilt,
much of the power up in heaven would be wasted,
and nearly every force down here struck dead.

And if the slant fell short or ran too far
from the straight line, then much would go missing
in the world's order, above and underneath.

Stay where you are now, Reader, on your bench,
and think your way along what you've just tasted,
if you would rather be glad than worn out.

I've set the dish in front of you — feed yourself
from here on; the matter I'm made the scribe of
turns all my care back onto its own work.

The greatest of the ministers of nature,
who stamps the world with the strength of heaven
and measures out our time with his own light,

was wheeling along the spirals, joined to that point
remembered above, where day by day
he shows himself a little earlier;

and I was with him — but of the climbing
I knew no more than a man knows the first thought
rising in him, in the instant before it comes;

and Beatrice — she who is seen to pass
from good to better, and so suddenly
that time can put no measure to the act —

how bright in herself she must have been! And what
was there inside the sun, where I had entered,
showing not by color but by light alone,

I could not tell it so that it could be imagined,
though I call on genius, art, and practice — but believe it,
and ache to see it for yourself.

And if our minds run too low for a height
so great, it is no wonder: no eye ever
climbed past the sun and looked beyond it.

Such, in that place, was the fourth family
of the high Father, who forever fills it,
showing how he breathes forth and how begets.

And Beatrice began: "Give thanks, give thanks
to the Sun of the angels, who by his grace
has raised you up to this, the sun you see."

No mortal heart was ever set so wholly
on worship, so ready to give itself
to God with all its gratitude at once,

as mine became at those words; and my love
was so absorbed in him that Beatrice
slipped, eclipsed, into oblivion.

This did not displease her — she smiled at it,
and the brightness of her laughing eyes
broke my one mind across a scatter of things.

I saw lights, many of them, vivid, triumphant,
make us their center and themselves a ring,
sweeter in voice than they were bright to see.

Just so we sometimes see Latona's daughter
belted round, when the air hangs thick enough
to hold the thread that makes her halo's band.

In the court of Heaven, the place I have come back from,
there are jewels so fair and so precious
that they can never be carried out of the realm;

and the singing of those lights was one of them.
Whoever does not grow wings to fly up there
can wait for word of it from a man struck dumb.

When those burning suns, still singing so,
had whirled three times in a circle around us,
like stars that wheel close by the steadfast poles,

they seemed like dancers not yet done with dancing,
but who stop short and stand in silence, listening,
until they have caught the next strain of the tune.

And inside one of them I heard begin:
"Since the radiance of grace, by which true love
is kindled, and which then grows by loving,

shines in you, multiplied so bright that it
leads you up the stair where no one comes back down
who will not climb it again,

whoever would refuse the wine in his flask
to your thirst would be no freer than water
that will not run downhill to the sea.

You want to know what blossoms make this garland
that circles with delight the lovely Lady
who strengthens you for heaven. So — listen.

I was one of the lambs of the holy flock
that Dominic leads down a road where a man
grows fat and sound, if only he does not stray.

The one nearest me here, at my right hand,
was my brother and my master: Albert
of Cologne. I am Thomas of Aquino.

If you would be sure of all the others too,
follow my words with your eyes, and trace your way
up and around the blessed garland as it turns.

That next glow breaks from the smile of Gratian,
who served both courts of law so well
that his work gives pleasure here in Paradise.

The other that adorns our choir nearby
was that Peter who, like
the poor widow, gave his whole treasure to the Church.

The fifth light, the most beautiful among us,
breathes from a love so great that all the world
down there is hungry for some word of it:

inside it is the lofty mind where wisdom
so deep was set that, if the truth be true,
no second ever rose to see so far.

Next you see the brightness of that candle
who, in the flesh below, looked deepest into
the nature of the angels and their work.

And in that other little light there smiles
the advocate of the Christian centuries,
whose rhetoric supplied even Augustine.

Now if you walk your mind's eye on, from light
to light, the way my praise has led it, already
you thirst, and wait, to know the eighth of them.

Seeing every good, that sainted soul rejoices —
the one who shows the lying world for what it is,
to anyone who listens to him well;

the body it was driven out of lies
down in Cieldauro; from martyrdom
and exile it came home into this peace.

See, further on, the burning breath of Isidore
flame out, and Bede, and Richard, who in his
contemplation rose to more than human.

This one, from whom your look comes back to me,
is the light of a spirit who, in his grave
and weighty thinking, found that death came slow.

It is the eternal light of Siger, who
lectured down in the Street of Straw, and there
reasoned out truths that earned him hate.

Then, like a clock that calls us up to wake
at the hour the Bride of God is rising
to sing her morning praise, to win her Spouse —

the kind where one part pulls and drives the next,
ting, ting, ringing out a note so sweet
the well-tuned spirit swells with love at it —

just so I watched that glorious wheel turn round
and answer voice to voice, in harmony
and sweetness past all understanding,

except up there, where joy is made to last forever.
