# The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto XXX

Perhaps six thousand miles away from us  
the sixth hour burns, and our own world by now  
is tilting its long shadow nearly flat,

while the mid-height of heaven, far above,  
begins to change for us, until a star  
here and there stops reaching down to this depth;

and as the sun's most radiant handmaid comes  
still farther on, the sky shuts light by light,  
closing them out until the loveliest goes —

so too the Triumph that forever plays  
in rings around the Point that conquered me,  
seeming enclosed by what it holds enclosed,

faded from my sight, little by little;  
and seeing nothing, and my love alike,  
drove me to turn my eyes to Beatrice.

If all that I have said of her till now  
were gathered up into a single praise,  
it would fall short of serving this one turn.

The beauty I beheld goes past all measure —  
not only beyond us; I truly believe  
that only her own Maker tastes it whole.

Here I confess myself defeated, more  
than ever comic or tragic poet was  
overwhelmed by some hard place in his theme;

for, like the sun in eyes that tremble most,  
so the mere memory of that sweet smile  
strips my mind clean away from my own self.

From the first day I saw her face on earth,  
in this our life, up to this present sight,  
my song has never once been cut from her;

but now I have to let my verse give up  
on following her beauty any farther,  
as every artist must, at his outermost.

So I leave her now to a greater herald  
than my own trumpet, which is bringing  
the hard matter of its labor to a close;

and she, with the bearing and the voice of a guide  
who wastes no step, began again: "We have come  
out of the largest sphere into the heaven that is pure light:

light of the intellect, brimming with love,  
love of the true good, brimming with joy,  
a joy that goes past every sweetness there can be.

Here you will see both armies of Paradise,  
and one of them in the same shapes you'll see  
on the last day, when the great Judgment comes."

As a sudden flash of lightning scatters  
the spirits of the eye, so that it robs  
the eye of taking in the strongest things,

so a living light blazed all around me  
and left me wound so tightly in the veil  
of its own brilliance that I saw nothing.

"The Love that keeps this heaven at rest  
always welcomes a soul into itself with such a greeting,  
to make the candle fit to take its flame."

No sooner had these few brief words  
found their way down into me than I knew  
myself lifted up beyond what I had power to do;

and I caught fire with a new sight, so clear  
that there is no light so pure  
my eyes could not have stood it.

And I saw light that took the form of a river,  
running amber-gold with its own blaze  
between two banks painted with wondrous spring.

Out of that current living sparks leapt up  
and settled on the flowers everywhere,  
like rubies that a setting of gold surrounds;

then, as if drunk upon the fragrances,  
they plunged back down into the marvelous flood,  
and as one entered, another rose to leave.

"The high desire that now inflames and drives you  
to understand the thing you see before you  
pleases me all the more, the more it swells;

but you must drink first of this water here  
before so great a thirst in you is stilled."  
So said to me the sunlight of my eyes,

and added: "The river and the topazes  
going in and out, the laughter of the grass —  
all these are shadowed prefaces to their truth.

Not that the things themselves are unripe, raw;  
the failing is on your side, in your eyes,  
which have not yet been raised to sight so high."

No infant ever lunged so suddenly,  
face toward the milk, if he should wake to find  
the hour run far past his accustomed time,

as I bent down — to make still better mirrors  
of my own eyes — stooping above the wave  
that pours along so we are bettered by it;

and the moment the eaves of my eyelids drank,  
it seemed to me the river changed its shape,  
out of its length becoming round, a ring.

Then, like a crowd of people who had worn  
masks, and look other than they did before  
once they strip off the borrowed face they vanished in,

so, into a greater festival for me,  
the flowers and the sparks were changed, and I saw  
both Courts of Heaven made plain before my eyes.

O splendor of God, by which I came to see  
the lofty triumph of the truthful realm,  
give me the power to tell how I saw it!

There is a light up there that makes the Maker  
visible to every creature whose one peace  
lies only in the sight of Him;

and it spreads itself out into a circle so wide  
that its circumference would be a belt  
too large by far to gird around the sun.

Its whole appearance is made of a ray  
flung back off the crown of the First Moving Sphere,  
which draws from that its life and all its power.

And as a hillside mirrors itself in water  
at its own foot, as if to see its beauty  
when it is richest in green and flowers,

so, ranged high above the light, ring upon ring —  
more than a thousand — I saw mirrored there  
all those of us who have gone back above.

And if the lowest tier can hold within it  
so great a light, then how vast is the breadth  
of this Rose where its outermost petals spread!

My sight, in all that width and all that height,  
did not get lost, but took in everything —  
the whole measure and the whole kind of that gladness.

There, near and far neither add nor take away;  
for there, where God governs with nothing between,  
the law of nature counts for nothing at all.

Into the gold of the eternal Rose,  
that opens out in rising tiers and breathes  
a fragrance of praise up to the ever-spring Sun,

Beatrice drew me — like one who would speak  
and holds his silence — and she said: "Behold  
how vast the gathering of the white robes is!

See how far our city sweeps around;  
see how our seats are filled so near to full  
that few souls now are wanted to complete it.

And on that great throne where you fix your eyes  
because of the crown already set above it,  
before you sup here at this wedding feast,

there shall sit the soul — to be Augustus  
on earth — of noble Henry, who will come  
to set Italy straight before she is ready.

The blind greed that has cast its spell on you  
has made you like the little child who dies  
of hunger and yet drives away the nurse.

And in the holy court there will preside,  
by then, such a man that openly  
or in secret he will not walk one road with Henry.

But God will not endure him long  
in sacred office; he shall be thrust down  
where Simon Magus is, for what he earned,

and drive the one from Anagni lower still."
