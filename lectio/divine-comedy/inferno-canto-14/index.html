The Divine Comedy, Inferno: Canto XIV

Inferno: Canto XIV14 of 100

Because the love of my own birthplace moved me,
I gathered up the scattered leaves
and gave them back to him, whose voice had failed.

Then we came to the border where the second
ring divides from the third, and where you see
a terrible form that justice takes.

To make these strange new things clear,
I'll say that we came out onto a plain
that throws off every plant from its bed.

The forest of grief is a wreath around it,
the way the dismal moat rings the forest;
and right at its edge we stopped our feet.

The ground was a dry and packed-down sand,
not made in any other fashion than the waste
that Cato's feet once trod across.

O vengeance of God, how much you ought
to be feared by every one who reads
what now was laid plain before my eyes!

I saw many herds of naked souls,
all of them weeping wretchedly,
and a different law seemed laid on each.

Some were lying flat on their backs on the ground;
some were sitting all hunched up together;
and others kept moving without a stop.

Those going around were the most by far,
and fewer were the ones laid down to suffer —
but their tongues were looser in their grief.

Over all that waste of sand, slowly,
flakes of fire came raining down, spread wide,
like snow in the mountains when there is no wind.

The way Alexander, in the burning regions
of India, saw flames fall on his army,
whole and unbroken, all the way to the ground,

so that he ordered his men to tramp the soil,
because the burning was put out more easily
while each flake still fell on its own —

that was how the eternal heat came down,
setting the sand alight, like tinder under flint,
to double the pain there.

Never resting, the dance went on and on
of miserable hands, now here, now there,
beating off the fresh flakes as they fell.

"Master," I began, "you who overcome
all things except the savage demons
that rushed against us at the gate —

who is that huge one who seems to pay
no heed to the fire, lying there scornful and twisted,
so the rain seems not to soften him at all?"

And that very soul, when he caught on
that I was asking my guide about him,
cried out: "What I was living, that am I dead.

Let Jove wear out his blacksmith — the one
from whom in his fury he seized the sharp bolt
that struck me through on my last day;

let him wear out the others, shift by shift,
in Mongibello at the black forge,
shouting, 'Help, good Vulcan, help me!'

the way he did at the battle of Phlegra,
and let him fire his bolts at me with all his force —
still he'd have no joy out of his revenge."

Then my leader spoke with such great force
that I had never heard him speak so loud:
"O Capaneus, in that your pride

is not put out, you are punished all the more.
No torment but your own raging
would be a pain to match your fury."

Then he turned to me with a gentler mouth,
and said: "That was one of the Seven Kings
who laid siege to Thebes, and he held — still holds —

God in contempt, and prizes him but little;
but, as I told him, his own spite
is the fittest ornament for his breast.

Now follow me, and take care not to set
your feet, not yet, on the burning sand,
but keep them always close against the wood."

Saying nothing, we came to where a little
stream wells up out of the forest,
whose redness still makes my hair stand on end.

The way the brook runs out of the Bulicame
that the sinful women share downstream among them,
so this one ran down across the sand.

Its bed, and both its sloping banks,
were made of stone, and so were the margins on each side;
and I saw that the crossing was right there.

"In everything else I have shown you
since we came in through the gate
whose threshold is refused to no one,

your eyes have found nothing so worth your notice
as this stream you see in front of you,
which puts out all the little flames above it."

These were my leader's words; and so I begged him
to grant me the meal he had given me
such a hunger for.

"In the middle of the sea lies a ruined land,"
he said then, "that goes by the name of Crete,
under whose king the world was once chaste.

There is a mountain there that once was glad
with waters and with leaves, and was called Ida;
now it is deserted, like a thing worn out.

Rhea chose it once as the safe cradle
for her own son, and to hide him the better,
whenever he cried she had a din raised there.

Inside the mountain stands a great old man,
upright, his back turned toward Damietta,
gazing at Rome as if it were his mirror.

His head is fashioned of fine gold,
and of pure silver are his arms and breast;
then he is brass as far down as the fork.

From there on down he is all chosen iron,
except the right foot, which is baked clay,
and he rests on that one more than on the other.

Every part except the gold is split
by a crack that drips with tears,
which gathered together bore through the cave.

From rock to rock they fall into this valley:
Acheron, Styx, and Phlegethon they form;
then they go down through this tight channel,

to the point past which there is no more descending.
They form Cocytus — and what that pool is
you will see for yourself, so I won't tell it here."

And I said to him: "If this present stream
runs down to us this way out of our world,
why does it only show itself at this rim?"

And he to me: "You know the place is round,
and though you have come a long way already,
always turning to the left, descending toward the bottom,

you have not yet gone all the way around the circle.
So if something new appears to us,
it shouldn't bring such wonder to your face."

And I again: "Master, where do we find
Lethe and Phlegethon? You say nothing of one,
and say the other is made of this rain."

"In all your questions you truly please me,"
he answered, "but the boiling of the red
water might well have answered one of them.

You will see Lethe, but outside this pit,
there where the souls go to wash themselves
once their repented sin has been lifted away."

Then he said: "It is time now to leave
the wood; see that you come close behind me.
The margins make a path, the ones not burning,

and above them every falling flame goes out."
