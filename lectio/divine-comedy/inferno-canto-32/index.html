The Divine Comedy, Inferno: Canto XXXII

Inferno: Canto XXXII32 of 100

If I had rhymes harsh enough and hoarse enough
to suit this miserable hole
on which all the other rocks of Hell bear down,

I could press the juice of my meaning
out to the full. But I don't have them,
and it's not without fear that I bring myself to speak —

because describing the bottom of the whole universe
is no task to take on lightly,
nor one for a tongue that still says Mama and Daddy.

But let those Ladies aid my verse —
the ones who helped Amphion wall in Thebes —
so the words won't fall short of the fact.

You rabble, born worse than any other,
penned in the place that is hardest to speak of:
better for you had you been sheep or goats on earth.

When we had climbed down into the dark well,
far below the giant's feet,
and I was still gazing up at the high wall,

I heard a voice say to me: "Watch how you walk.
Take care you don't trample under your soles
the heads of these wretched, worn-out brothers."

At that I turned and saw before me
and under my feet a lake so frozen
it looked like glass, not water.

Never in winter did the Danube
in Austria veil its current with so thick a sheet,
nor the Don, off under its frigid sky,

as the ice was here. If Tambernic itself
had crashed down on it, or Pietrapana,
not even at the edge would it have cracked.

And the way a frog will sit and croak
with its muzzle out of the water, in the season
when the peasant girl dreams of her gleaning —

so, livid, sunk to the line where shame shows in the face,
the wretched shades stood locked in the ice,
setting their teeth to the note a stork's bill clatters.

Each one kept his face bent downward.
From the mouth, the cold; from the eyes, the grieving heart —
among them each bears witness to itself.

When I had looked a little around me,
I turned my gaze down and saw two so close
the hair on their heads was tangled together.

"You who press your chests so hard one to the other,"
I said, "tell me — who are you?" They bent their necks,
and when they had lifted their faces to me,

their eyes, which had only been wet inside,
overflowed at the lids, and the frost froze
the tears between them and sealed them shut again.

No clamp ever fastened wood to wood
so tight. At that, the two of them, like a pair of he-goats,
butted heads — such fury overcame them.

And one, who had lost both ears to the cold,
his face still bent down, said:
"Why do you stare at us as if you were a mirror?

If you want to know who these two are —
the valley the Bisenzio runs down
belonged to them and to their father Albert.

They came from one body. Search all of Caïna,
and you won't find a shade more fit
to be set in this gelatin —

not the one whose breast and shadow were run through
by a single stroke of Arthur's hand;
not Focaccia; not this one here whose head

blocks my view ahead with his own,
and who was called Sassol Mascheroni —
if you're a Tuscan, you know well who he was.

And so you put me to no more talking,
know that I was Camicion de' Pazzi,
and I'm waiting for Carlino to make me look clean."

After that I saw a thousand faces
purpled by the cold — and a shudder runs through me,
and always will, at the sight of frozen ponds.

And as we were moving toward the center,
where all the weight of the world draws together,
and I was shivering in that eternal shade,

whether it was will, or fate, or chance,
I don't know — but walking among the heads,
I struck my foot hard against one face.

Weeping, it snarled: "Why do you trample me?
Unless you've come to add to the vengeance
for Montaperti — why do you torment me?"

And I: "My Master, wait here for me a moment,
so I can clear up a doubt through this one;
then you can hurry me on as you please."

My Leader stopped. And to that shade,
still cursing me with all its breath, I said:
"Who are you, snapping at others like this?"

"And who are you," he answered, "going through Antenora
kicking other people's cheeks —
so hard that, if you were alive, it would be too much?"

"I am alive," was my reply, "and it could be worth your while,
if what you want is fame,
for me to set your name down with the rest."

And he to me: "It's the opposite I long for.
Get away from here and stop bothering me —
you don't know how to flatter down in this pit."

Then I grabbed him by the scruff behind
and said: "You're going to name yourself,
or not a hair will be left on you here."

And he: "Even if you tear out every hair,
I won't tell you who I am, or show you,
not if you come down on my head a thousand times."

I already had his hair twisted in my fist
and had yanked out more than one fistful,
he barking, his eyes held hard to the ground,

when another cried out: "What's wrong with you, Bocca?
Isn't it enough to clatter your jaws —
you have to bark too? What devil's got into you?"

"Now," I said, "I don't need you to talk at all,
you damned traitor — because to your shame
I'll carry back true news of you."

"Get away," he answered, "and tell what you like.
But if you make it out of here, don't keep quiet
about the one whose tongue was so quick just now.

He's down here weeping over French silver.
'I saw,' you can say, 'the one from Duera,
there where the sinners stand packed in the cold.'

And if you're asked who else was there —
you've got the man of Beccaria right beside you,
the one whose throat Florence cut.

Gianni del Soldanier is over there, I think,
with Ganelon and Tebaldello,
who threw Faenza's gates open while it slept."

We had already left him behind
when I saw two frozen in a single hole,
so that one head was a cap to the other,

and the way a starving man tears into bread,
the one on top set his teeth into the other
right where the brain joins the nape of the neck.

No differently did Tydeus, in his fury,
gnaw at the temples of Menalippus
than this one did the skull and the rest of it.

"You there, showing by such a brutal sign
your hatred for the one you're eating —
tell me why," I said, "on these terms:

that if you have just cause to complain of him,
then knowing who you are and what he did,
I'll pay you back for it up in the world above —

if the tongue I speak with doesn't dry up first."
