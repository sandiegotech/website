# The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto XXXII

Rapt in his own delight, that contemplative  
freely took up the teacher's office now,  
and gave beginning to these holy words:

"The wound that Mary closed again and healed,  
she who sits there so lovely at her feet  
is the one who first opened it and pierced.

Within the order that the third tier makes,  
beneath her, Rachel sits with Beatrice  
beside her, just as you can see.

Sarah, Rebecca, Judith, and the one  
who was great-grandmother to that singer  
who, grieving his great sin, cried 'Miserere mei' —

all these you can behold from sill to sill  
descending down by grade, as I myself  
go down the Rose by name from leaf to leaf.

And from the seventh row on down, just as  
above it, the Hebrew women follow on,  
parting the flower through all its flowing hair;

because, according to the way their faith  
looked toward Christ, they form the very wall  
that splits the sacred stairways left and right.

On this side, where the flower stands complete  
in every one of its unbroken petals,  
are seated those who believed in Christ to come;

and on the other side, where the half-rings  
are cut across by gaps and empty seats,  
are those who turned their faces to Christ come.

And as, on this side, the resplendent throne  
of Heaven's Lady, and the lesser thrones  
below her, make so great a dividing line,

so, opposite, does the seat of the great John —  
holy always, who endured the desert  
and martyrdom, and then two years in Hell;

and under him, chosen to share the dividing,  
sit Francis, Benedict, and Augustine,  
and down to us the rest, from round to round.

Now see how high the providence of God:  
for both ways of beholding the one faith  
shall fill this garden out in equal measure.

And know that downward from the rank that cleaves  
straight through the middle of the two divisions,  
the blessed there are seated for no merit of their own,

but for another's, under set terms:  
for these are spirits all absolved and freed  
before they ever made a real choice.

You can make this out plainly in their faces,  
and in their voices too, still childlike, thin,  
if you look at them closely and listen well.

Now you have doubts, and doubting you stay silent;  
but I will loosen for you the tight knot  
in which your subtle thinking holds you bound.

Within the vast expanse of this domain  
no point can fall by chance and find a place,  
any more than grief can here, or thirst, or hunger:

for everything you see has been laid down  
by an eternal law, so that exactly  
the ring is fitted to the finger here.

And so this people, hastened on so soon  
to the true life, are not sine causa  
more and less excellent among themselves.

The King through whom this kingdom rests at ease  
in so much love and in so deep a joy  
that no will dares to ask for any more,

creating every mind within his glad  
and radiant gaze, endows them, at his pleasure,  
with grace in varying measure; here let the result suffice.

And this is set down plain and clear  
for you in Holy Scripture, in those twins  
whose anger stirred while still within the womb.

And so, according to the color of the hair  
those souls were born with, the supreme high light  
must fittingly bestow its crowning grace.

Without, then, any merit of their deeds,  
they are assigned to differing degrees,  
differing only in their first keen sight.

True, in the early centuries, with innocence,  
the parents' faith alone was enough  
to win salvation for their children;

once those first ages had completed their course,  
the boys, to give their innocent wings their strength,  
had to add virtue through the circumcising rite;

but after the age of grace had come at last,  
without the full baptism Christ requires,  
such innocence was held back, down below.

Look now upon the face that most of all  
resembles Christ, for only its bright clearness  
has power to make you fit to look on Christ."

I saw such gladness raining down on her,  
borne to her in the holy minds created  
to fly across the heights of that great altitude,

that nothing I had seen in all the rest  
had ever held me hung in such amazement,  
or showed me such a likeness of God himself;

and that same Love who first came down to her,  
singing 'Ave, Maria, gratia plena,'  
spread wide his wings before her where she sat.

From every quarter the beatified court  
sang answer to the song that he had sung,  
so that each face grew serener for the sound.

"O holy father, who endure for me  
to be down here, leaving the sweet place where  
you take your seat by everlasting lot,

who is that Angel who, with such delight,  
looks straight into the eyes of our great Queen,  
so deep in love he seems to be made of fire?"

So once again I had recourse for help  
to the teaching of the one who drank his beauty  
from Mary, as the morning star from the sun.

And he to me: "Whatever boldness, grace,  
and bearing can exist in angel or soul,  
all is in him — and so we wish it to be,

because he is the one who bore the palm  
down unto Mary, in the hour the Son  
of God resolved to take our weight as his own.

But come now, let your eyes follow along  
as I go speaking, and observe the great  
patricians of this just and gracious empire.

Those two who sit up there in highest bliss,  
for being nearest to our great Augusta,  
are like the two roots from which this Rose has grown:

the one who fits beside her on the left  
is the father by whose reckless taste  
the human race tastes bitterness so deep;

and on the right you see that ancient father  
of Holy Church, into whose keeping Christ  
entrusted the keys to this lovely flower.

And he who saw, before he came to die,  
all the hard days ahead of the fair Bride  
won with the spear and with the driven nails,

sits next to him; and by the other rests  
that leader under whom there lived on manna  
the people thankless, fickle, and stiff-necked.

Across from Peter you see Anna seated,  
so well content to gaze upon her daughter  
that she does not stir her eyes while singing Hosanna;

and facing the great father of the household,  
Lucia sits — she who first sent your Lady,  
the hour you bent your brows, about to fall.

But since the time runs out that holds you dreaming,  
here we will make full stop, like a good tailor  
who cuts the gown to fit the cloth he has,

and we will turn our eyes to the first Love,  
so that, gazing toward him, you may pierce  
as deep as may be through his radiance.

And truly, lest perhaps you should fall back,  
beating your wings and thinking you advance,  
you must obtain by prayer the grace you need —

grace from the one who has the power to help you;  
and you shall follow me with all your heart's  
affection, that it not stray from my words."

And so he began this holy orison:
