The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto XXIII

Paradiso: Canto XXIII90 of 100

Like a bird among the leaves she loves,
settled on the nest of her sweet young
through the night that hides all things from us,

who, longing to look on their faces again
and to find the food to feed them with —
labor that, for her, is a glad burden —

gets ahead of the hour on an open branch,
and with a burning eagerness waits the sun,
staring hard for the first break of dawn:

so my Lady stood, upright and intent,
turned toward the quarter of the sky
beneath which the sun moves least in haste;

so that, seeing her so rapt and yearning,
I became like a man who wants a thing
he does not have, and hopes, and is content to wait.

But the space was short from the one moment
to the next — from my waiting, I mean, to the seeing
of the heavens kindling, brighter and brighter still.

And Beatrice cried out: "Look — there,
the armies of Christ in His triumph, and all
the harvest these turning spheres brought in!"

It seemed to me her whole face was on fire,
and her eyes were so full of ecstasy
that I have to go on and leave it undescribed.

As on the clear nights of the full moon
Trivia smiles among the eternal nymphs
who paint the sky across all its depths,

so, above thousands upon thousands of lamps,
I saw one Sun that set them all ablaze,
as ours lights up the lights we see above —

and through that living radiance the shining
substance of it broke on my eyes so clear,
so blinding, that I could not hold the sight.

O Beatrice, my gentle, my beloved guide!
She said to me: "What overwhelms you now
is a power nothing can defend itself against.

Here are the Wisdom and the Power that opened
the roads between heaven and earth at last,
the roads men had hungered for so long."

As fire breaks free of a cloud, swelling
until it has no room to stay inside,
and falls to earth against its own nature,

so my mind, growing greater on that feast,
went out beyond itself — and what it became
in that moment, it can no longer recall.

"Open your eyes, and look at what I am.
You have seen such things that you have grown
strong enough now to bear my smile."

I was like a man who keeps the trace
of a forgotten dream, and strains in vain
to bring it back into his mind again,

when I heard this invitation — one that earns
such gratitude it will never fade
out of the book that keeps the record of the past.

If at this moment every tongue should sound
that Polyhymnia and her sisters fed
to fullest sweetness on their richest milk,

to help me, it would not reach one-thousandth
of the truth, if it tried to sing that holy smile
and how it lit her holy face.

And so, in setting Paradise on the page,
the sacred poem has no choice but to leap,
like a man who finds the road has given out.

But anyone who weighs the crushing theme,
and the mortal shoulder loaded down with it,
should lay no blame on it if it trembles here.

This is no crossing for a little boat,
the water my bold prow goes cutting through,
nor for a pilot who would spare himself.

"Why does my face hold you so spellbound
that you will not turn to the beautiful garden
flowering there under the rays of Christ?

There is the Rose in which the Word of God
became flesh; there are the lilies
by whose scent the good road was found."

So Beatrice; and I, who was all eagerness
to do as she advised, gave myself again
to the struggle of my weak and failing eyes.

As, under sunlight pouring clean and clear
through a torn cloud, my eyes — though held
in shadow — have once seen a meadow of flowers,

so I saw a host of brilliances on every side,
lit from above by burning rays of light,
without my seeing the source the brightness came from.

O kindly Power that set Your stamp on them!
You lifted Yourself higher, to give more room
there to my eyes, that were too weak to hold You.

The name of that fair flower I call upon
morning and evening took my whole soul
and fixed it utterly on the greatest fire.

And when the glory and the greatness of that living
star — which conquers there as it conquered here —
stood painted in both my eyes,

down through the heavens a small flame came,
shaped in a circle like a crown,
and ringed her round, and wheeled about her.

Whatever melody sounds sweetest here on earth
and draws the soul most strongly to itself
would seem a thundercloud, torn open and roaring,

set beside the sounding of that lyre
with which the lovely sapphire was crowned —
the sapphire that gives the clearest heaven its blue.

"I am angelic Love, and I circle round
the high joy that breathes out of the womb
that was the dwelling-place of our Desire;

and I will circle, Lady of Heaven, as long
as you follow your Son, and make the highest sphere
more holy still by entering there."

So the circling melody sealed itself,
and all the other lights, each one of them,
were sounding out the name of Mary.

The royal mantle that wraps the turning volumes
of that whole world — the one most fervent, most alive
with the breath of God, His works, His ways —

held its inner border so far above us,
so distant, that from where I stood
its look did not yet reach me at all.

And so my eyes had not the strength to follow
the flame in its crown as it climbed upward,
close behind the One who was born of her.

And as a little child, when it has taken
the milk, reaches its arms toward its mother,
its love flaring out into the open,

so each of those white fires stretched its summit
upward, reaching — so that the deep love
they had for Mary was made plain to me.

Then they stayed there before my sight,
singing Regina coeli with such sweetness
that the delight of it has never left me.

Oh, what abundance is gathered up
inside those richest coffers — souls who were
good sowers of the seed down here below!

There they have their joy, and live upon the treasure
they won while weeping in the exile
of Babylon, where they left the gold behind.

There, beneath the exalted Son of God
and Mary, in His victory, with the ancient
council and the new, one triumphs:

he who holds the keys of all that glory.
