The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto VII

Paradiso: Canto VII74 of 100

"Osanna, sanctus Deus sabaòth,
superillustrans claritate tua
felices ignes horum malacòth!"

So, turning again to the music of his own song,
that soul sang — the one on whom a double light
redoubles itself — and as I watched,

he and the others moved into their dance,
and like the swiftest sparks flung up from a fire
they veiled themselves from me in sudden distance.

I stood in doubt, and inwardly I said,
"Tell her, tell her" — "speak to my Lady,
who slakes my thirst with her sweet falling rain" —

and yet that reverence which masters all of me
at the mere sound of BE or of ICE
bent my head down again, like a man half-asleep.

Beatrice let me stay that way only a moment;
then she began, and lit me with a smile
that would make a man happy in the heart of fire:

"By my sure reckoning, which cannot fail,
how a just vengeance could be justly avenged
is the question that has set you wondering.

But I will quickly loosen the knot in your mind.
Listen — for what I am about to say
will hand you a great teaching as a gift.

By refusing to bear the curb that the will of God
set on his power for his own good, that man
who never was born damned himself, and all his line with him.

So the human race lay sick down here below,
century after century, lost in deep error,
until it pleased the Word of God to descend

and join to himself, in his own person, the nature
that had wandered away from its own Maker —
and he did it by the sole act of his eternal love.

Now turn your sight to what I am about to say.
This nature, when it was first joined to its Maker,
was, as created, pure and good and whole;

but by its own act alone it was driven out
of Paradise, because it turned aside
from the road of truth, away from its true life.

So if you measure the penalty of the cross
against the human nature Christ took on,
no punishment ever bit with greater justice;

and none was ever so deep an injustice,
if you consider who the Person was
that suffered, with that nature drawn into him.

So from one act came two opposite things:
one death was pleasing both to God and to the Jews;
the earth shook at it, and Heaven was thrown open.

It should no longer seem so hard to you, then,
when someone says a just revenge was later
justly avenged before a rightful court.

But now I see your mind is tangled up,
knotting itself from thought to thought, and waiting
with great longing to work itself free.

You are saying: 'I follow well what I have heard;
but why God willed our redemption to come
in this one way, and no other — that is hidden from me.'

This decree, brother, lies buried out of reach
from the eyes of everyone whose love
has not yet ripened to its full grown flame.

Still — since men stare hard at this very mark
and make out little of it — I will tell you
why this way was the worthiest way of all.

The Goodness of God, which spurns all envy from itself,
burning within itself, throws off such sparks
that it unfolds the beauties that never end.

Whatever rains straight down from it, with nothing
between, has no end after — for its imprint,
once that seal is set, can never be moved.

Whatever falls straight down from it, with nothing
between, is wholly free, because it is not
subject to the pull of newer, changing things.

The closer a thing is shaped to it, the more it pleases;
for the holy fire that lights up everything
burns most alive in what is most like itself.

With all of these gifts the human creature was favored;
and if even one of them is taken away,
it must fall down out of its high estate.

Sin alone is what strips him of his rights,
and makes him unlike the Supreme Good, so that
little of that light is left to whiten him;

and he never climbs back to his old dignity
unless he fills, where his trespass emptied him,
with just suffering set against his guilty pleasures.

When your whole nature sinned, down in its seed,
it was driven out of these high dignities
the same way it was driven out of Paradise;

nor could it win them back — if you weigh this
with your finest care — by any road
except by crossing one of these two fords:

either God, out of mercy and nothing more,
forgives the debt outright — or man himself
makes good, and pays in full for his own folly.

Fix your eye now, and drive it deep into
the abyss of the eternal plan, and hold
as fast as you can to every word I say.

Man, with his limits, could never make it good:
he could not sink in humble obedience now
as far below as, in his pride, he reached

to rise above by disobeying then —
and this is why man was shut out from the power
to satisfy the debt by his own act.

So it fell to God, in his own ways, to bring
man back to his full and perfect life — by one
of the two roads, I mean, or else by both.

But since the deed of the doer pleases more
the more it shows, in what it does, the goodness
of the heart that the deed comes streaming out of,

the Goodness of God, which sets its seal on the world,
was glad to move along its every road,
by each and all of them, to raise you again.

Between the first day and the final night
there never was, nor will be, a deed so high
and magnificent as this, by one road or the other;

for God, in giving his own self, was more openhanded
toward making man able to lift himself again
than if he had simply, of himself, forgiven;

and every other way fell short of justice,
unless the Son of God had humbled himself
to take on flesh and be born into the world.

Now, to fill every desire of yours completely,
I will go back and make one point
as clear to you as it is to me.

You are saying: 'I see the air, I see the fire,
the water and the earth, and all that is mixed from them,
come to ruin and last only a little while;

and yet these things were created.' And so, you reason,
if what I said is true, they too should stand
secure against corruption, and not decay.

The angels, brother, and this unspoiled country
you are standing in, can rightly be called created
exactly as they are, in their whole being;

but the elements you have just named, and all
the things that are made out of them, are shaped
and given form by a created power.

The matter that they have was made; the forming
power was made — the influence that lives
within these stars that wheel and turn around them.

The soul of every animal, and of the plants,
is drawn out of matter's own potential, pulled
by the ray and the motion of the holy lights;

but your own life the Supreme Goodness breathes
into you directly, and so enamors it
of himself that ever after it longs for him.

And from this you can reason out one thing more —
your own resurrection — if you think back
to how the flesh of the first two was first made,

back when both of the first parents were created."
