# The Divine Comedy, Purgatorio: Canto XXIX

Singing like a woman in love,  
she finished her words and went straight on:  
"Beati quorum tecta sunt peccata."

And as nymphs once walked alone  
through the woodland shade — one keeping to the dark,  
another turning to find the sun —

so she moved upstream along the bank,  
and I kept pace beside her, matching  
my small steps to her small steps.

Between her steps and mine were not a hundred  
when both banks took an even turn,  
and I found myself facing east.

Nor had we gone much farther on  
before she turned full around to me  
and said, "Brother — look, and listen."

And suddenly a brightness swept  
across the whole wide forest, on every side,  
so quick I thought at first it was lightning.

But lightning is gone as soon as it comes,  
and this only grew, and went on brightening,  
until I asked myself, "What is this?"

And a sweet melody ran on  
through the glowing air — and a holy anger  
rose in me against the rashness of Eve:

for there, where earth and heaven obeyed,  
one woman, alone, and only just made,  
could not bear to stay under any veil.

Had she stayed beneath it, faithfully,  
I would have tasted those joys past telling  
sooner, and for far longer.

While I walked on among such first-fruits  
of the eternal gladness, lost in it all  
and still longing for more delight,

the air ahead of us, beneath the green boughs,  
turned to a kindled fire,  
and the sweet sound was clearly singing now.

O holy Virgins — if ever for your sake  
I bore hunger, or cold, or sleepless nights,  
now is my reason to ask my reward.

Now let Helicon pour out its springs for me,  
let Urania help me with her choir  
to put in verse things hard even to think.

A little farther on, seven trees of gold  
were what the long stretch of distance  
still lying between us seemed to make them;

but once I had come so near  
that the shape, which the senses cheat us with,  
lost none of its features to the gap,

the power that fits speech to reason  
grasped that they were candlesticks,  
and caught, in the voices of the song, "Hosanna."

Above them the lovely flames blazed,  
far brighter than the moon in a clear  
midnight sky, at the middle of her month.

I turned, filled with wonder,  
to good Virgil, and he gave me back  
a face no less amazed than mine.

Then I turned my eyes again to those high things,  
which came toward us so slowly  
a new-married bride would have outpaced them.

The lady chided me: "Why burn  
with such longing only for the living lights,  
and never look at what comes behind them?"

Then I saw, like men behind their leaders,  
people coming after them, robed in white —  
a whiteness never seen on earth.

The water on my left shone bright,  
and gave me back my own left side,  
as a mirror would, if I looked in.

When I had reached the point on my bank  
where only the stream stood between us,  
I halted my steps to see the better;

and I watched the little flames move on,  
leaving the air behind them painted,  
so they looked like trailing banners,

and the sky above stayed marked  
with seven bands, all in the colors  
the sun makes in its bow, and Delia in her ring.

These standards streamed back farther  
than I could see; and as I judged it,  
the outermost were ten paces apart.

Under so fair a sky as I describe,  
twenty-four elders, two by two,  
came on, crowned with lilies.

And all of them were singing: "Blessed are you  
among the daughters of Adam, and blessed  
for all time shall your loveliness be."

When the flowers and the fresh new grass  
on the far bank in front of me  
were clear of that chosen company,

as in heaven star follows after star,  
four living creatures came close behind,  
each one crowned with green leaves.

Every one was feathered with six wings,  
the plumage full of eyes — the eyes of Argus,  
were he alive, would be like these.

Reader, I'll spend no more rhymes  
tracing their shapes; other costs press me  
so hard I can't be lavish here.

But read Ezekiel, who paints them  
as he saw them coming out of the cold north  
with wind, and cloud, and fire;

and just as you'll find them in his pages,  
so they were here — except that in the wings  
John is with me, and parts from him.

The space those four enclosed held  
a triumphal chariot on two wheels,  
drawn at the neck of a Griffin.

He stretched both his wings straight up,  
between the middle band and the three and three,  
so that he split none by cleaving through.

They rose so high they were lost to sight;  
his limbs were gold, the part of him that was bird,  
and the rest was white shot through with red.

Rome never gladdened Africanus,  
or even Augustus, with a car so splendid;  
beside it, the Sun's own chariot would be poor,

the Sun's chariot that swerved and burned away  
at Earth's desperate prayer,  
when Jove, in his hidden way, was just.

Three ladies came dancing in a ring  
at the right wheel; one so red  
you'd hardly have marked her inside a fire.

The second looked as if her flesh and bone  
had all been wrought from emerald;  
the third, like snow just fallen.

Now they seemed led by the white,  
now by the red, and from her song  
the others took their step, slow or quick.

At the left wheel, four kept holiday,  
robed in purple, following the measure  
of one of them who had three eyes in her head.

Behind the whole company I've described,  
I saw two old men, unlike in dress,  
but alike in bearing, grave and sure.

One showed himself a follower  
of great Hippocrates, whom nature made  
for the creatures she holds dearest;

the other showed the opposite care,  
with a sword so bright and sharp  
it struck me with fear from across the river.

Then I saw four of humble look,  
and behind them all, one old man alone,  
walking in a dream, his face keen.

And these seven were robed  
like the first company; but no lily  
circled their heads in a garland —

instead the rose, and other red flowers;  
a little way off you'd have sworn  
that above their brows they were all aflame.

And when the chariot drew level with me,  
a thunderclap was heard; and that noble host  
seemed forbidden to go on,

halting there, with the standards in the lead.
