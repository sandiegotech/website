The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto XI

Paradiso: Canto XI78 of 100

O senseless striving of the human race,
how crooked are the reasonings
that drag your wings down toward the ground!

One was off chasing law, another medicine,
another running after the priesthood,
another to rule by force or clever lies,

one bent on plunder, one on public office,
one tangled in the pleasures of the flesh
and worn out by it, one given over to sloth —

while I, set free of all these things,
was there above in heaven with Beatrice,
received into such surpassing glory.

When each of the lights had circled back
to the point along the ring where it had stood,
it held still, like a candle in its stand.

And from within the brightness that had spoken
to me before, I heard it start again,
smiling, and growing clearer as it spoke:

"Just as I take my shining from its ray,
so, gazing into the Eternal Light,
I can read where your thoughts are coming from.

You are unsure, and you want me to sift again
in words plain and wide enough
that your mind can take them in,

where I said just now, 'where a man grows fat,'
and where I said, 'no second ever rose.'
Here we must draw the line with care.

The Providence that governs all the world
with wisdom so deep that every created sight
is beaten back before it finds the floor —

so that the bride of Him who, with a loud cry,
married her with His own consecrated blood,
might go to her Beloved

secure in herself and all the more faithful —
ordained two princes on her behalf,
to guide her, one on either hand.

The one was all seraphic in his ardor;
the other, for his wisdom here on earth,
was a splendor of cherubic light.

I will speak of one, but praising either one
is praising both, whichever you take up,
because their labors aimed at a single end.

Between Tupino and the stream that falls
down from the hill blessed Ubaldo chose,
a fertile slope hangs from a lofty mountain.

From it Perugia feels the cold and heat
through Porta Sole, while behind it grieve
Gualdo and Nocera under their heavy yoke.

From that slope, there where it breaks
most steeply, a sun rose upon the world,
the way our sun climbs sometimes from the Ganges.

So let whoever speaks of that place
not say Assisi — that would say too little —
but the East, if he means to name it right.

He was not yet far risen above the horizon
before he began to let the earth
feel some comfort from his mighty power.

For as a young man he drew his father's rage
for the sake of a certain Lady to whom,
as to death itself, no one unbars the door of joy.

And before his bishop's court, in his father's presence,
he was joined to her — et coram patre —
and day by day he loved her more.

She, robbed of her first husband, scorned, unknown,
for eleven hundred years and more
had waited with no suitor, until he came.

It did no good to tell how, with Amyclas,
she was found unshaken at the voice of him
who once struck terror into all the world;

it did no good that she stood firm and fearless,
so that, when even Mary stayed below,
she climbed the cross to be with Christ.

But so I won't go on too dark and folded,
take Francis and Poverty, from here on,
as the two lovers I have been describing.

Their harmony, the gladness on their faces,
the love, the wonder, the sweet looks they traded,
set other minds to holy thoughts —

so much that venerable Bernard
first went barefoot and ran toward such peace,
and running, thought himself too slow.

O unsuspected riches, O true good!
Giles goes barefoot, and Sylvester goes barefoot,
after the bridegroom — the bride pleases them so.

Then off goes that father and that master,
he and his Lady and the family
that was already cinching on the humble cord.

No cowardice weighed down his brow
for being the son of Pietro Bernardone,
nor for the wondering scorn he drew;

but like a king he laid his stern resolve
before Pope Innocent, and from his hands
received the first seal set upon his Order.

Then, as the poor men multiplied
behind this man, whose marvelous life
would best be sung in the glory of the heavens,

the Eternal Spirit crowned a second time,
through Pope Honorius, the holy will
of this great shepherd of the flock.

And when, athirst for martyrdom,
he had preached Christ and those who came with Him
in the proud presence of the Sultan,

and finding the people there too green
to be converted, and unwilling to waste the time,
he came back to harvest the Italian fields,

on the bare rock between the Tiber and the Arno
he took from Christ the final seal,
which his body wore for two whole years.

When He who had singled him out for so much good
was pleased to lift him to the reward
he had earned by making himself low,

to his friars, as to his rightful heirs,
he commended his most beloved Lady,
and charged them to love her faithfully;

and from her arms the shining soul
chose to set out, returning to its kingdom,
and for its body asked no other bier.

Think now what kind of man this was,
fit to be the partner who, across high seas,
keeps Peter's boat steady on its course.

This was our Patriarch — so whoever
follows him as he commands
can see he is loaded with good cargo.

But his flock has grown so greedy
for new pasture that there is no way
it won't go scattering over far-off fields;

and the more his sheep go straying
and wandering away from him,
the emptier of milk they come back to the fold.

True, there are some who fear the harm
and keep close to the shepherd — but so few
it takes little cloth to make their hoods.

Now, if my words have not come muffled,
if you have given them your full attention,
if you call back to mind the things I said,

your wish will be in part fulfilled,
for you will see the plant being stripped away,
and the rebuke that hides inside the words,

'where a man grows fat, if he does not stray.'"
