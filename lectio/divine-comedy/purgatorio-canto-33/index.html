The Divine Comedy, Purgatorio: Canto XXXIII

Purgatorio: Canto XXXIII67 of 100

"Deus, venerunt gentes" — now three voices, now four,
the maidens began their sweet alternating psalm,
weeping as they sang the lament aloud.

And Beatrice listened, sighing, full of grief,
her face so changed by what she heard
that Mary at the cross was scarcely more transfigured.

But when the other virgins gave her room to speak,
she rose to her feet, the color of fire,
and answered them:

"Modicum, et non videbitis me;
et iterum — my beloved sisters —
modicum, et vos videbitis me."

Then she set all seven of them ahead of her,
and behind her, with nothing but a glance,
she drew me on, and the lady, and the sage who stayed.

So she moved forward. And I do not think
her tenth step had come down upon the ground
when her eyes struck straight into my eyes,

and with a quiet face she said to me,
"Come quicker — so that, if I speak with you,
you will be near enough to hear me well."

And once I had drawn up beside her as I should,
she said: "Brother, why do you not dare
to ask me anything, now that you walk with me?"

Like those too full of reverence, who stand
before their betters and cannot bring
a living word all the way up to their teeth,

so it was with me. Without my voice fully formed
I began: "My lady, you already know
my need — and what would answer it."

And she to me: "From here on I will have you
strip yourself of fear and of shame,
so that you stop speaking like a man asleep.

Know this: the vessel the serpent broke
was, and is not. But let the guilty one
be sure God's vengeance fears no sop of bread.

The Eagle that left its feathers on the car —
so that the car turned monster, then turned prey —
will not be heirless forever.

For clearly I see, and so I tell it now:
the stars already draw the hour near,
safe from every block and every bar,

in which a Five hundred, Ten, and Five,
sent down from God, will kill the thieving harlot
and the giant who sins there at her side.

And maybe my dark telling, like the Sphinx,
like Themis, will persuade you less,
since in their way it clouds the mind;

but soon the facts themselves will be the Naiads
that solve this hard enigma for you —
with no destruction of the flocks and grain.

Mark it. And just as I have spoken them,
carry these words to those who live
the life that is a running toward death.

And when you write them down, remember this:
do not hide what you have seen the tree become —
the tree that twice now has been stripped bare.

Whoever robs that tree, whoever breaks it,
offends God with a blasphemy of the deed —
God, who made it holy for his use alone.

For biting it, the first-born soul
longed five thousand years and more, in pain,
for Him who paid the bite upon himself.

Your wit is sleeping if it does not see
some special reason why that tree stands
so tall, and so inverted at its crown.

And if your empty thoughts had not been
the petrifying water of Elsa around your mind,
their pleasure a Pyramus to the mulberry,

then by these signs alone — their number, their strangeness —
you would have read, set morally in that tree,
the justice of the ban that God laid down.

But since I see your mind has turned to stone,
and stone so stained that the light of what I say
leaves you only dazzled,

I want you to carry it within you — if not written,
then at least painted, the way a pilgrim brings
his staff back home, bound round with palm."

And I: "As wax that holds the seal
and does not change the figure pressed in it,
so my brain is stamped now by your words.

But why does your longed-for speech soar up
so far past anything my sight can reach,
that the harder I strain, the more I lose it?"

"So that you may know," she said, "the school
you followed — and may see how far
its teaching trails behind my words;

and may see your human path fall short of God's
as far as the earth lies under that highest heaven
which wheels the fastest of them all."

And I answered her: "I do not remember
ever having strayed away from you,
nor does my conscience accuse me of it."

"And if you cannot call it back to mind,"
she answered, smiling, "then remember this:
that you drank, this very day, from Lethe.

And as fire is argued from its smoke,
so this forgetting plainly proves
your will was bent, somewhere, on something wrong.

But truly, from this hour, my words will go
as bare as is fitting to lay them open
before your still-unpracticed sight."

And brighter now, and with a slower step,
the sun was holding to the noon-day arc,
which shifts its place with where the watcher stands,

when — the way one halts who walks ahead
as escort to a company, and stops
if he comes on something strange along the road —

the seven ladies halted at a shadow's edge,
the kind a cold Alp casts upon its border
beneath green leaves and branches black.

And there, ahead of them, I seemed to see
the Tigris and Euphrates rise from one spring
and part, like friends, slowly, each from the other.

"O light, O glory of the human race,
what water is this, that opens here
from a single source, then draws away from itself?"

To this prayer it was said to me, "Ask Matelda —
let her tell you," and the lovely lady answered,
like one who clears herself of any blame:

"This, and other things, I told him already,
and I am sure the water of Lethe
has hidden none of them from him."

And Beatrice: "Perhaps a heavier care,
the kind that often robs the memory,
has dimmed the sight of his mind.

But look — there rises Eunoë.
Lead him to it, and as you always do,
revive again the half-dead virtue in him."

Like a gentle soul that makes no excuse
but takes another's will to be its own
the instant it is shown by any sign,

so, when she had taken me by the hand,
the lovely lady moved, and to Statius
said, in her womanly way, "Come with him."

Reader, if I had more room left to write,
I would sing on, at least in part,
of that sweet draught I never could drink my fill of;

but since all the pages set aside
for this second canticle are full,
the curb of art will let me go no further.

From those most holy waters I came back
remade, the way young plants are made new
when they put out new leaves,
pure and ready to rise to the stars.
