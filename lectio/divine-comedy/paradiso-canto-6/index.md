# The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto VI

"After Constantine turned the eagle back  
against the course of heaven, which it had kept  
behind the ancient who took Lavinia for his bride,

two hundred years and more the bird of God  
stayed at the far edge of Europe, close  
to the mountains it had first risen from;

and there, beneath the shadow of its sacred wings,  
it ruled the world, passing from hand to hand,  
and so, by changes, came at last to mine.

Caesar I was, and am Justinian,  
who, by the will of the first Love I feel,  
stripped from the laws what was excess and idle.

And before I gave myself to that great work,  
I held that in Christ one nature lived, no more,  
and with that faith I was content.

But the blessed Agapetus, who was then  
the supreme shepherd, pointed me the way  
to the true faith with words that were his own.

I believed him; and what he then declared  
I now see clearly, the way you yourself see  
that of two contradictory claims, one is false and one true.

The moment I had set out with the Church,  
it pleased God in his grace to breathe into me  
this high task, and I gave myself wholly to it,

and to my Belisarius I entrusted  
the armies, to which heaven's right hand was joined  
so closely it was a sign I should stand down.

Here, then, my answer to your first question ends;  
but the nature of it forces me to follow on  
with something more;

so that you see with how much right men move  
against the sacred standard, both the ones who seize it  
for themselves and the ones who set themselves against it.

See how great a power has made it worthy  
of reverence, beginning from the hour  
when Pallas died to give it sovereignty.

You know it made its home in Alba  
three hundred years and more, until the end,  
when three against three fought for it once again.

You know what it achieved, from the wrong done  
to the Sabine women to Lucretia's grief,  
beating down the nations all around it under seven kings;

you know what it achieved, borne by the Romans,  
that shining people, against Brennus, against Pyrrhus,  
against the other princes and their leagues.

By it Torquatus and that Quinctius named  
for his unkempt hair, the Decii and the Fabii,  
won the fame I gladly keep alive.

It struck to earth the pride of the Arabs  
who followed Hannibal across the Alpine ridges,  
down from which, O Po, your waters slide.

Under it, while they were still young, triumphed  
Scipio and Pompey; and it pressed hard  
on that very hill beneath which you were born.

Then, near the time when heaven had willed to bring  
the whole world to its own untroubled calm,  
Caesar, at Rome's will, took the eagle up.

And what it did from the Var to the Rhine  
the Isère saw, the Saône, the Seine,  
and every valley that pours into the Rhone.

What it did once it had left Ravenna  
and leaped the Rubicon was such a flight  
that neither tongue nor pen could follow it.

It wheeled its legions round toward Spain,  
then toward Durazzo, and struck at Pharsalia  
so hard the pain was felt by the warm Nile.

Antandros and the Simois, where it had begun,  
it saw again, and the ground where Hector lies,  
and then, ill for Ptolemy, it roused itself.

From there it came down like lightning on Juba,  
then wheeled once more into your western lands,  
where it heard the Pompeian trumpet sounding.

For what it did under the next who bore it,  
Brutus and Cassius howl in Hell together,  
and Modena and Perugia grieved.

For it the grieving Cleopatra still weeps,  
who, fleeing from before it, took from the adder  
a sudden and a black death.

With him it ran as far as the Red Sea shore;  
with him it set the world in such deep peace  
that the temple of Janus was shut.

But all the standard that has made me speak  
achieved before, and all it would achieve  
throughout the mortal realm that lies beneath it,

seems small and dim in the telling, if you look  
with a clear eye and an undivided heart  
at what it did in the third Caesar's hand,

because the living Justice that inspires me  
granted it, in the hand of him I speak of,  
the glory of avenging its own wrath.

Now marvel at what I tell you next:  
later it ran, with Titus, to take vengeance  
on the vengeance for the ancient sin.

And when the teeth of the Lombards bit  
the Holy Church, then under its wings  
Charlemagne came, victorious, to her rescue.

Now you have the power to judge those men  
I accused above, and judge their crimes,  
which are the cause of all your miseries.

Against the standard that belongs to all,  
one sets the yellow lilies; the other claims it  
for his party, till it is hard to say which sins more.

Let the Ghibellines, let them ply their trade  
beneath some other standard; for whoever  
cuts it from justice follows it badly forever.

And let this new Charles never bring it down,  
he and his Guelfs, but let him fear the talons  
that have torn the hide from a greater lion.

Many times before now the sons have wept  
the father's crime; and let him not believe  
God will trade his blazon for the lilies.

This little planet adorns itself  
with the good spirits who were busy in the world,  
that fame and honor might come after them;

and when desires climb toward such ends,  
swerving so, the rays of the true love  
must climb less brightly upward.

But part of our joy lies in the measuring  
of our wages against what we deserved,  
for we see them neither less nor greater.

And in this the living Justice sweetens  
our love so wholly that it can never, ever  
be twisted toward any wrong.

Different voices make the sweeter music;  
and so, in this our life, the different ranks  
yield a sweet harmony among these spheres.

And here within this present pearl there shines  
the light of Romeo, whose great and lovely work  
was paid back badly in the end.

But the Provençals who worked against him  
have not had the last laugh; he walks a bad road  
who turns another's good deeds to his own harm.

Four daughters, each of them a queen,  
Raymond Berenger had, and this was the work  
of Romeo, a poor man and a pilgrim.

Then spiteful tongues moved Raymond to demand  
a reckoning from this just man, who had handed back  
twelve for every ten that he was given.

So he left, old and poor, and went his way;  
and if the world could know the heart he carried,  
begging his bread a crust at a time,

much as it praises him, it would praise him more."
