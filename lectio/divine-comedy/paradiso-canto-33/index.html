The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto XXXIII

Paradiso: Canto XXXIII100 of 100

"Virgin Mother, daughter of your own Son,
humbler and higher than any creature is,
the fixed goal of the eternal plan,

you are the one who so ennobled
our human nature that the One who made it
did not refuse to be made of it himself.

Within your womb the love was kindled again
by whose warmth, in the eternal peace,
this flower has bloomed into what it is.

Here, for us, you are the noonday torch
of love; and down below, among the living,
you are the wellspring of their hope, never dry.

Lady, you are so great, your power so sure,
that whoever longs for grace and does not come to you
wants his desire to fly with no wings at all.

Your kindness does not only run to the one
who asks; again and again, freely,
it is there before the asking is begun.

In you is mercy, in you is pity,
in you is every greatness; in you is gathered
all the good that any creature holds.

Now here is a man who, from the deepest pit
of the universe to this height, has seen
one by one the lives of every soul,

and begs you, by your grace, for strength enough
to lift his eyes still higher with his own sight,
toward the ultimate salvation.

And I, who never burned to see for myself
more than I burn for him to see, lay down
all of my prayers — and pray they fall not short —

that you would scatter from him every cloud
of his mortal nature with your prayers,
so the Highest Joy may open to his eyes.

And still I pray you, Queen, who can do
whatever you will: keep his affections whole
and pure once such a vision has gone by.

Let your guard prevail over what is merely human.
See Beatrice, and all the blessed with her,
folding their hands to second what I ask."

The eyes that God so loves and reveres,
fixed on the one who prayed, made plain to us
how welcome to her are prayers offered in faith;

then they turned to the Eternal Light, into which
no creature's eye, we must believe,
could ever look with sight so clear.

And I, who was drawing near to the end
of all desiring, brought — as I should —
the burning of my own longing to its close.

Bernard was signaling to me, and smiling,
that I should look up; but already, on my own,
I was what he was asking me to be,

for my sight, growing clean and clear,
was entering deeper and deeper into the ray
of the High Light that is true of itself.

From that point on, what I saw was greater
than speech can hold — speech fails before such sight,
and memory fails before so great a flood.

As a man who sees something while he dreams,
and after the dream the feeling it pressed in him
stays, and the rest will not come back to mind —

so am I now: my vision almost wholly
gone, and yet the sweetness that was born of it
still drips, even now, into my heart.

So the snow comes unsealed under the sun;
so, on the wind, in the weightless leaves,
the Sibyl's oracles were scattered and lost.

O Light Supreme, lifted so far beyond
the reach of mortal minds, lend back to mine
a little of how you appeared to me,

and make my tongue so strong that it may leave
one single spark of all your glory
to the people who are still to come;

for if some part returns into my memory
and sounds, however faintly, in these lines,
more of your triumph will be understood.

I think the keenness of that living ray
I bore would have struck me blind, had I once
turned my eyes aside and let them slip from it;

and I remember that, for just this reason,
I grew bold enough to bear it, until
I joined my gaze with the Infinite Worth.

O grace overflowing, by which I dared
to fix my eyes upon the Eternal Light
so long that I spent all my seeing there!

Deep in its depths I saw, gathered in
and bound by love into a single book,
the scattered leaves of all the universe:

substance and accident and how they act,
fused together in such a way that all
I speak of is one simple flame of light.

The universal form of this great knot
I think I saw — because, in saying so,
I feel the joy within me widen and rise.

One instant brings me deeper into forgetting
than twenty-five centuries dimmed the voyage
that made Neptune marvel at the shadow of the Argo.

So my mind, wholly held in suspense,
stood gazing, still, unmoving, fixed,
and grew, from the gazing, ever more on fire.

Before that light a man is so transformed
that to turn from it toward any other sight
is a thing he could never bring himself to do;

for the good, which is the object of the will,
is gathered whole in this, and outside it
whatever is perfect there falls short.

Now, even of what I do remember, my words
will fall shorter than the babble of an infant
who still wets his tongue at the breast.

Not that more than one single face was there
in the living light I looked into —
it is forever what it always was —

but through my sight, which gathered strength
in me as I looked, that one unchanging vision
seemed to change for me as I myself changed.

Within the deep and shining substance
of the High Light, three circles came to me,
of three colors and of one dimension;

and the first seemed mirrored by the second
as rainbow throws back rainbow, and the third
seemed fire breathed equally from the other two.

Oh how my words are weak, how far they fall
beneath what I conceived — and that, against
what I saw, is too small even to call small!

O Light Eternal, dwelling in yourself alone,
alone you know yourself, and, known to yourself
and knowing, you love and smile upon yourself!

That circling, which conceived this way appeared
in you as light thrown back, when I had looked
upon it for a while with my own eyes,

within itself, and in its very color,
seemed to be painted with our human likeness,
and for this all my sight was drowned in it.

Like the geometer who bends his whole mind
to measure the circle, and cannot find,
for all his thinking, the principle he needs,

so was I at that strange new sight: I wanted
to see how the image fit into the circle,
and how it found its place and held it there;

but my own wings were not enough for that —
except that then my mind was struck through
by a flash of lightning, and its wish came in that blaze.

Here power failed the high imagining;
but already my desire and will were turning,
like a wheel that is moved evenly,

by the Love that moves the sun and the other stars.
