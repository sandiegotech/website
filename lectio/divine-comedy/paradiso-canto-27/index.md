# The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto XXVII

"Glory to the Father, and to the Son,  
and to the Holy Spirit!" all of Paradise  
began to sing, until the sweetness made me drunk.

What I was seeing seemed to me a smile  
of the whole universe — so that my rapture  
came flooding in through hearing and through sight.

Oh joy! Oh gladness beyond any word!  
Oh life made whole of love and peace alike!  
Oh wealth so sure it leaves no room for wanting!

Before my eyes the four great torches stood,  
still burning, and the one that came to me first  
began to make itself more luminous.

And it became, in its look, what Jupiter  
would be, if he and Mars were birds and traded  
the feathers off each other's wings.

That Providence which here assigns to each  
its season and its task had laid a silence  
across the blessed choir on every side,

when I heard this: "Do not be amazed  
if I change color while I speak; for soon  
you'll see them all change color here with me.

He who usurps my place on earth — my place,  
my place, my place, which now stands empty  
in the sight of the Son of God —

has turned my burial-ground into a sewer  
of blood and stench, so that the Evil One,  
who fell from here, takes comfort there below."

With that same color which, when the sun stands  
against it, paints the clouds at dusk or dawn,  
I saw the whole of heaven now suffused.

And as a decent woman, sure of herself,  
will grow uneasy at another's fault  
merely from hearing it named aloud,

so Beatrice changed in her face as well;  
and such an eclipse, I think, was in the heavens  
when the supreme Power suffered on the cross.

Then he went on, and the words came forth  
in a voice so altered from the one before  
that his face itself had not changed more.

"The Bride of Christ was never raised and fed  
on blood of mine, of Linus and of Cletus,  
to be spent in the buying-up of gold;

but to win this life of joy, it was their blood —  
Sixtus and Pius, Urban and Calixtus —  
that they poured out after long suffering.

We never meant that part of Christian people  
should sit at the right hand of our successors  
and part be set against them on the left;

nor that the keys once handed to my care  
should turn into the emblem on a banner  
waging war on those already baptized;

nor that my face should be the stamp on a seal  
for lying privileges put up for sale,  
at which I often redden and flash with fire.

Dressed up as shepherds, ravenous wolves  
are seen from here in every pasture below.  
O wrath of God, why do you sleep so long?

Men of Cahors and Gascony are getting ready  
to drink our blood. O good beginning, what  
a vile end you are falling toward!

But the high Providence that once, with Scipio,  
defended the world's glory there at Rome  
will bring its rescue soon, as I foresee.

And you, my son, who will go down again  
weighed by your mortal body — open your mouth;  
hide nothing of what I do not hide."

As our air sheds its frozen vapor downward  
in flakes, in the season when the heavens' Goat  
brushes its horn against the sun,

so I saw the upper air now flaked and graced  
with those triumphant souls who, climbing up,  
had stayed there with us for a while.

My eyes kept following where their bright shapes went,  
and followed them until the stretch between us  
grew too vast and took them from my sight.

Then the Lady, who saw me freed at last  
from gazing upward, said: "Now turn your eyes  
down, and see how far you have wheeled around."

Since the first time I had looked down below,  
I saw I had passed through the whole arc that runs  
from the middle of the first zone to its end —

so that I saw, beyond Cadiz, the mad  
track of Ulysses, and on the near side, almost  
to the shore where Europa made herself sweet cargo.

More of this little threshing-floor would have  
been bared to me, but the sun was moving on  
beneath my feet, a sign and more, ahead.

And my mind, in love, forever lingering close  
to my Lady, burned more than ever to bring  
my eyes back home to her.

And if Art or Nature ever made a lure  
to catch the eyes and so possess the mind —  
in living flesh, or painted in a picture —

all of it together would have looked like nothing  
beside the divine delight that shone on me  
when I turned to her smiling face again.

The power her look gave me tore me loose  
from the lovely nest of Leda, and drove me up  
into the swiftest of the heavens.

Its regions, brimming with life and lifted high,  
are all so uniform that I cannot say  
which one Beatrice chose to set me in.

But she, who knew already what I longed for,  
began — and smiled with such a joy  
that God himself seemed glad within her face:

"The nature of the motion that holds still  
the center while it turns all else around it  
begins from here, as from its starting point.

And this heaven has no other Where except  
the Mind of God, in which is kindled  
the love that turns it and the power it rains.

One circle of light and love embraces it,  
as it embraces all the rest; and that bound  
is known only to Him who girds it round.

Its motion is not measured by another's,  
but all the others take their measure from it,  
as ten is measured by its half and fifth.

And how time has its roots inside this vessel  
and its leaves in all the rest — that, too,  
can now be made plain to you.

O greed, that drags all mortals down so deep  
beneath you that not one of them can lift  
his eyes back up above your waves!

The will to good blossoms fairly in men;  
but the unending rain turns the true plums  
into sour and stunted things.

Faith and innocence are only found  
in little children; both of them take flight  
before the cheeks have even grown their down.

One who still lisps will keep the holy fasts,  
then, once his tongue is loosed, will gorge himself  
on any food at all, under any moon.

Another, while he lisps, will love and heed  
his mother; then, once his speech is clear,  
he longs to see her buried in the ground.

Just so the skin turns dark that once was white  
in its first dawn — the fair daughter of him  
who brings the morning and leaves us the night.

And you, so this won't seem so strange to you,  
remember: down on earth no one rules,  
and so the human family goes astray.

But before January has slipped clear of winter —  
thanks to that hundredth part men leave neglected —  
these upper circles are going to roar so loud

that the storm so long awaited will come, and swing  
the sterns around to where the prows now point,  
so that the fleet runs true upon its course,

and the real fruit at last will follow the flower."
