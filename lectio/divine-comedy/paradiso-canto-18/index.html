The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto XVIII

Paradiso: Canto XVIII85 of 100

Now that blessed mirror of a soul
was taking joy in its own words alone,
and I was tasting mine — the bitter laced with sweet —

when the Lady who was leading me toward God
said, "Turn your thoughts. Remember that I stand
near the One who lifts the weight from every wrong."

I turned toward the loving sound of my comfort,
and what love I saw then in those holy eyes
I give up trying to say —

not only because I don't trust my own words,
but because the mind cannot climb back
that far above itself unless another leads it.

This much, and only this, can I report:
that looking at her again, my whole heart
was set free of every other longing,

while the eternal joy, shining straight down
on Beatrice, contented me from her bright face
with its reflected light.

Winning me over with the radiance of her smile,
she said, "Turn around now and listen.
Paradise is not only in my eyes."

Just as here on earth you sometimes read
a feeling in someone's face, when it runs so deep
that all the soul is swept up in it,

so in the blaze of the holy brightness
I had turned to, I made out a wish:
that it wanted to tell me something more.

It began: "On this fifth landing-place,
on the tree that lives down from its summit,
bears fruit forever and never sheds a leaf,

are blessed spirits who, before they came to Heaven,
were of such great renown below
that any Muse would grow rich telling of them.

So look now at the arms of the Cross.
The one I name will flash across it
the way swift fire leaps inside a cloud."

I saw a splendor streak along the Cross
the instant he said Joshua — and the word
and the deed were one; I caught no gap between.

And at the name of the great Maccabee
I saw another light go wheeling, spinning,
joy the whip that drove that spinning top.

So too for Charlemagne and for Roland —
my eager eyes tracked two of them
the way the eye tracks a falcon in flight.

Then William and Renouard drew my sight
along that Cross, and Duke Godfrey too,
and Robert Guiscard after them.

Then, stirred and mingling back among the other lights,
the soul that had spoken to me showed
what an artist he was among Heaven's singers.

I turned to my right side to look
for what Beatrice would tell me to do —
by word, or by some sign —

and I saw her eyes so clear, so bright,
so full of joy, that her face went past
every look she had worn before, even the last.

And the way a man, feeling delight grow deeper
as he does good from one day to the next,
becomes aware his strength is climbing,

so I became aware that my turning
with the heavens had widened its arc —
that miracle even lovelier now to see.

And the way a pale woman's face will change
in one short moment, once the weight
of shame is lifted off it,

so it changed before my eyes when I turned back,
because of the whiteness of the temperate star,
the sixth, which had drawn me up into itself.

Inside that torch of Jove I saw
the sparkling of the love that filled it
spell out our language before my eyes.

And the way birds, risen from the shore,
as if in thanks for the food they've found,
bunch into flocks — now a ring, now a long line —

so inside those lights the holy creatures
sang as they flew this way and that,
shaping themselves into a D, an I, an L.

First they moved singing to their own music;
then, as each one became one of these letters,
they paused a little while and fell silent.

O divine Muse, you who make genius
glorious and long-lived, and through it
make the cities and the kingdoms last,

light me with yourself, so I can bring out
their shapes exactly as I held them in my mind.
Let your power show in these few lines.

So they came forward in five times seven
vowels and consonants, and I took in
each part as it seemed to speak to me.

DILIGITE IUSTITIAM
were the first verb and noun of the whole inscription;
QUI IUDICATIS TERRAM were the last.

Then in the M of the fifth word they held
their order, so that Jupiter looked like silver
inlaid, just there, with gold.

And I saw more lights come down and settle
on the crown of that M, and pause there singing
the Good, I think, that draws them to itself.

Then, the way countless sparks fly up
when you strike a burning log —
the sparks fools love to read the future in —

more than a thousand lights seemed to rise from there
and climb, some higher and some less,
as the Sun that kindles them had measured out;

and once each one had gone still in its place,
I saw the head and the neck of an eagle
drawn out in that inlaid fire.

The One who paints there has no one to guide Him;
He is His own guide — and from Him comes
the shaping force that is form to the nest.

The rest of that blessedness, which at first
seemed content to flower as a lily on the M,
with a slight motion completed the design.

O gentle star, what jewels, and how many,
proved to me that the justice we have
is the work of the heaven you set with gems!

And so I pray the Mind in which your motion
and your power begin, to look at where
the smoke comes from that fouls your light —

to be angry a second time, and now,
at the buying and the selling in the temple
whose walls were built of miracles and martyrdoms.

O army of heaven I am gazing at,
pray for those still on the earth
who have all gone astray after the bad example.

Once the custom was to wage war with swords;
now it's waged by taking, here and there,
the bread the merciful Father shuts from no one.

But you, who write only so you can cancel —
remember that Peter and Paul, who died
for this vineyard you are ruining, are still alive.

Oh, you can say: "My whole desire is fixed
on the one who chose to live alone,
and was dragged to martyrdom for a girl's dance,

so I have no use for the Fisherman or for Paul."
