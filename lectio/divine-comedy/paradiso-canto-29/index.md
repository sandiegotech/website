# The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto XXIX

At the moment when the two children of Latona,  
one crowned by the Ram and one by the Scales,  
lay together as a belt across the horizon —

for just as long as the zenith holds them level,  
balanced, until each slips from that same belt  
and, changing hemispheres, tips the scale again —

for just that long, her face lit with a smile,  
Beatrice was silent, gazing steadily  
into the Point that had overpowered me.

Then she began: "I tell you — and I won't ask  
what you would hear, for I have already seen it  
there where every Where and every When converge.

Not to gain some good for himself — that is  
impossible — but so that his own splendor,  
shining, might be able to say, 'I am,'

in his eternity, outside of time,  
outside all other bound, just as it pleased him,  
the Eternal Love opened into new loves.

Nor did he lie idle, as if dull, before;  
for neither after nor before was there  
any going-forth of God upon these waters.

Form and matter, both apart and joined,  
came into being flawless, with no defect —  
like three arrows from a bow of three strings.

And as a sunbeam strikes through glass, or amber,  
or crystal, so that from its arrival  
to its full presence there is no interval,

so the threefold work rayed out at once  
from its Maker into being, all together,  
with no distinction of a first or after.

Order was created together with them,  
built into the substances; and at the summit  
of the world stood those of pure act alone.

Pure potential held the lowest place;  
and in the middle, potential and act were bound  
by a knot that never will be loosed again.

Jerome wrote to you that the angels were made  
a long stretch of centuries before  
the rest of the world was ever brought to be;

but this truth is set down in many places  
by the writers of the Holy Spirit, and you'll  
see it for yourself, if you look closely.

And even reason sees it, in a way —  
for it would not allow that the movers could  
be so long without the work that makes them whole.

Now you know where these loves were made, and when,  
and how; and so already three desires  
are quenched, three fires in you put out.

You could not count to twenty fast enough  
to match the speed with which a part of these,  
the angels, disturbed the ground of your elements.

The rest remained, and took up the art you see —  
this circling — with such joy in it that never  
do they break off, or cease to wheel for love.

The cause of the fall was the accursed pride  
of that one — the one you saw down there,  
crushed under the whole dead weight of the world.

But these you see here were humble enough  
to know themselves as gifts of that same Goodness  
which made them fit for so much understanding;

and so their sight was lifted higher still  
by grace that gives light and by their own merit,  
so that their will is steadfast now, and whole.

I would not have you doubt, but be quite sure:  
there is merit in receiving such a grace,  
in the measure that the love lays itself open.

Now, all around you in this gathered host,  
you may take in much more, if you have gathered  
these words of mine and need no further help.

But since on earth, throughout your schools, they teach  
that the angelic nature is the kind  
that understands, remembers, and wills,

I'll say a little more, so you can see  
the naked truth confused down there below,  
garbled in lectures of exactly that sort.

These beings, since they took such joy in the face  
of God, have never turned their gaze away  
from him, from whom not anything is hidden;

and so their sight is never cut across  
by some new thing, and so they have no need  
to call a memory back through broken thought.

So down below men dream while wide awake,  
believing they speak truth, or not believing —  
and the second carries the deeper guilt and shame.

You don't keep to one road, down there, while you  
philosophize — so much does the love of show,  
and the thought it breeds, carry you off course.

And even this is borne with up here  
with less disdain than when the Holy Word  
is set aside, or twisted out of true.

They give no thought to what it cost in blood  
to sow that Word in the world, or how he pleases God  
who keeps close to it in humility.

Each one strains after show, spins out his own  
inventions; and these the preachers serve up whole,  
while the Gospel itself is left to hold its peace.

One says the moon turned backward in her course  
at Christ's Passion, and threw herself between,  
so that the sun's light could not reach below —

and he lies; for the light hid of its own will:  
that eclipse answered as much for Spaniards  
and for Indians as it did for the Jews.

Florence does not have so many men named  
Lapo or Bindo as it has such fables,  
bawled from the pulpits, back and forth, all year,

so that the lambs, who know no better, come  
home from the pasture fed on empty wind —  
and not seeing the harm does not excuse them.

Christ did not say to his first followers,  
'Go out and preach the world a pack of tales,'  
but gave to them instead a true foundation;

and that alone rang out so loud from their lips  
that, in the war to set the Faith ablaze,  
they made the Gospel both their shield and spear.

Now men go out to preach with jokes and clowning,  
and if the people only laugh out loud,  
the hood puffs up, and nothing more is asked.

But in that cowl there nests so foul a bird  
that, if the common people could but see it,  
they'd see what kind of pardons they trust in.

On this the folly has grown so great on earth  
that, with no proof at all, no testimony,  
they'd flock to any indulgence on offer.

By this the pig of Saint Anthony fattens,  
and many others fatter and worse than pigs,  
paying with coin that bears no honest stamp.

But since we have wandered far enough afield,  
turn back your eyes now to the straight road,  
so that the way grows shorter with the time.

This nature multiplies itself in numbers  
so vast that there was never speech, nor mind  
of any mortal, that could reach so far.

And if you mark what Daniel has revealed,  
you'll see that even in his thousands he  
keeps the determined number out of sight.

The primal Light, that pours through all of them,  
is taken in by just as many modes  
as there are splendors it is wedded to.

And since the love comes following the act  
by which they grasp him, the sweetness of that love  
burns in them, each its own — warmer, or cooler.

Behold now the height and breadth of the eternal  
power, since it has made itself so many  
mirrors, in which it splinters into light,

while staying, in itself, one as before."
