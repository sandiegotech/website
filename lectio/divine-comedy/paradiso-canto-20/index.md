# The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto XX

When the one who lights the whole world  
sinks so far below our hemisphere  
that on every side the daylight burns away,

the sky, that he alone had kindled,  
all at once shows itself again  
with many lights, and in them one shines clear.

This turn of the heavens came back to my mind  
as the standard of the world and of its lords  
fell silent in that blessed beak,

for all those living lamps,  
brighter than before, began their songs —  
songs that slip and fall from my memory.

O gentle Love, cloaked in a smile,  
how burning you appeared in those sparks  
that carried nothing but the breath of holy thought.

After the precious, clear-cut jewels  
with which I saw the sixth light set ablaze  
had let the angel-bells fall still,

I seemed to hear the murmur of a river  
falling clear from stone to stone,  
showing the fullness of its mountain spring.

And as the sound takes shape  
in the neck of a lute, or as the wind  
gathers tone at the vent of a country pipe,

just so, the moment the waiting was done,  
that murmur of the Eagle rose up  
along its neck, as if the neck were hollow.

There it became a voice, and broke from the beak  
in a form of words my heart had been waiting for —  
and it is there I wrote them down.

"The part of me that sees and bears the sun  
in earthly eagles," it began,  
"is what you must now look at steadily,

for of the fires that make my shape,  
those by which the eye sparkles in my head  
stand highest in every rank.

He who shines at the center, for the pupil,  
was once the singer of the Holy Spirit  
who bore the ark from city to city.

Now he knows the worth of his own song,  
in the measure it was his own choice,  
by the reward that matches it exactly.

Of the five who make an arc for my brow,  
the one set nearest to my beak  
is he who comforted the widow for her son.

Now he knows how dearly it costs  
not to follow Christ — he has tasted  
this sweet life and the bitter one before it.

He who comes next along the curve  
I speak of, high upon its arc,  
put death off by true repentance.

Now he knows the eternal judgment  
holds firm and does not change, though worthy prayer  
below can make today's debt fall due tomorrow.

The next, with the laws and with me,  
meaning well in a thing that bore bad fruit,  
turned Greek by yielding to the shepherd.

Now he knows the harm that came  
from his good deed does him no injury,  
though the world be wrecked because of it.

And the one you see on the descending arc  
was William, mourned by the same land  
that weeps for Charles and Frederick, still alive.

Now he knows how heaven loves  
a just king — and even in the show  
of his own brightness he keeps showing it.

Who, down in the wandering world, would believe  
that Ripheus the Trojan, here in this ring,  
could be the fifth of the holy lights?

Now he knows more than the world can see  
of grace divine, though even his own sight  
cannot reach all the way to the floor of it."

Like a lark that wheels out into the air,  
first singing, then falling silent, content  
with the last sweetness that fills her,

so seemed to me that image, the imprint  
of the eternal pleasure by whose will  
everything becomes the thing it is.

And though to my doubt I was  
as glass is to the color that fills it,  
the doubt could not bear to wait in silence,

but forced from my mouth, by its own weight,  
the words "What things are these?" —  
and at that I saw great flashing of their joy.

Then, with its eye still more on fire,  
the blessed standard answered me,  
so as not to leave me hanging in wonder:

"I see you believe these things  
because I say them, but you don't see how —  
so that, though believed, they stay hidden.

You do as one who grasps a thing by name  
well enough, but cannot see  
what it truly is until another shows him.

The Kingdom of Heaven suffers violence  
from burning love and from that living hope  
which overcomes the will of God —

not the way one man overpowers another,  
but it conquers because it wills to be conquered,  
and, conquered, conquers by its own kindness.

The first soul of the brow and the fifth  
astonish you, because you see  
the region of the angels painted with them.

They did not leave their bodies, as you think,  
as pagans, but as Christians, firm in faith  
in the feet that were to suffer, and had suffered.

For one came back from Hell — where no one  
ever returns to good will — back to his bones,  
and that was the reward of living hope:

the living hope that put its power  
into prayers made to God to raise him,  
so his will could be moved again.

The glorious soul I speak of,  
returning to the flesh, where he stayed only briefly,  
believed in the One with power to help him,

and, believing, caught such fire  
of true love that, at his second death,  
he was worthy to come into this joy.

The other, by a grace that wells  
from a spring so deep no creature's eye  
has ever reached its first wave,

set all his love, down there, on righteousness;  
and so, from grace to grace, God opened  
his eyes to our redemption still to come.

He believed in it, and from that day  
bore the stench of paganism no more,  
and rebuked the perverse people for it.

Those three Maidens you saw  
at the right-hand wheel stood as his baptism  
a thousand years and more before baptizing came.

O predestination, how far off  
your root lies from the sight of all  
who do not see the First Cause whole —

and you, mortals, hold yourselves back  
from judging; for even we who look on God  
do not yet know all the chosen,

and the lack is sweet to us,  
because our good is perfected in this good:  
whatever God wills, we will it too."

So, by that divine shape,  
to clear my dim sight,  
a pleasant medicine was given me;

and as a good lutenist follows  
a good singer with the trembling of the strings,  
and the song takes on more delight from it —

so, while it spoke, I remember  
I watched both of those blessed lights,  
like eyes that blink in time together,

moving their small flames in time to the words.
