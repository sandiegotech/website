The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto XXVIII

Paradiso: Canto XXVIII95 of 100

After she had laid bare the truth that stands
against the life of wretched mortals here —
she who imparadises all my mind —

then, as a man sees in a looking-glass
the flame of a candle lit behind his back
before the thing itself has crossed his thought,

and turns to learn whether the glass tells true,
and finds that it agrees with what is there
the way a song keeps step with its own measure,

just so, my memory recalls, I turned,
for I had been gazing into those fair eyes
where Love first wove the snare that took me captive.

And when I turned, and my own eyes were struck
by what shows in that volume of the heavens
the moment one looks hard upon its wheeling,

I saw a Point that rayed out light so keen
that any sight it kindles has to close
before a brightness sharpened to that edge.

Whatever star looks smallest from down here
would seem a moon if set beside that Point,
the way one star is set beside another.

Perhaps as near as a bright halo seems
to ring the light that paints it on the air
when the mist that holds it is the densest,

so distant from the Point a ring of fire
wheeled round it, and so fast that it outran
the swiftest motion that girdles the world.

And this first ring was circled by a second,
the second by a third, the third a fourth,
the fourth a fifth, and then the fifth a sixth.

On these came the seventh, spread by now so wide
that Juno's messenger, the whole rainbow,
would be too narrow a band to hold it round.

So too the eighth, the ninth — and each of them
turned slower than the last, the more its place
stood farther in number out from the First.

The flame burned clearest, purest, in that ring
whose stainless spark stood nearest to the Point —
because, I think, it drinks the most of truth.

My Lady, who could see me lost in doubt,
said: "On that Point the heavens depend, and all
of nature hangs upon it utterly.

Look at the ring that clings most close to it,
and know its motion runs so swift because
the burning love that spurs it gives no rest."

And I to her: "If the whole world were ranged
in the order I can see in these bright wheels,
then what you set before me would content me.

But in the world of sense it goes the other way:
the farther out a sphere stands from the center,
the more divine it is in its turning.

So if my longing is to reach its rest
here in this marvelous and angelic temple
whose only walls are made of love and light,

I still must hear why copy and its model
do not run true to one another's pattern,
since on my own I turn it round in vain."

"It is no wonder if your fingers fail
to loose a knot so tight," she said — "it has
grown stubborn for the simple want of trying."

My Lady spoke; then: "Take what I shall tell you
if you would be filled, and sharpen on it
the keen edge of your own quick understanding.

The bodily heavens run wide or narrow
according to the more or less of power
that is poured out and spread through all their parts.

Greater goodness works a greater good;
a greater good asks a greater body to hold it,
so long as every part is whole and equal.

And so this sphere, which sweeps the whole sublime
universe along with it, answers truly
to the ring that loves and knows the most of all.

From which it follows: lay your measure down
upon the power, and not upon the look
of these shapes that seem to you mere rings —

and you will see a marvelous accord,
more power to greater, less to smaller sphere,
through every heaven and its Intelligence."

As the great dome of air is left serene
and shining when the north wind blows on us
from the cheek where his breath is gentlest,

for the haze that troubled it is cleared and gone
until the sky breaks open and laughs out
with all the splendor of its pageantry —

just so was I, once my Lady had given me
her clear reply; and like a star at evening
the truth stood plain and steady in my sight.

And when her words had come to their full stop,
those circles flashed and sparked the way that iron
throws off its sparks the moment it runs molten.

Each glint took up the sparkling of the rest,
and they were so many that their number
outruns the chessboard doubled square by square.

I heard them sing hosanna, choir to choir,
up to the fixed Point that holds them in their where,
and always will, and always has, forever.

And she, who saw the doubt still turning in me,
said: "The first two circles you beheld
have shown you the Seraphim and Cherubim.

So swiftly they keep pace with their own bonds
to grow as like the Point as they are able,
and they can, as far as their sight ascends.

Those other loves that circle around the two
are called the Thrones of the divine regard,
for they bring the first triad to its close.

And you should know they all have their delight
in measure as their vision pierces deeper
into the Truth where every mind finds rest.

From this you see how blessedness is founded
on the act of seeing, not on the act of love —
for love comes after, second, in its train.

And the measure of that seeing is its merit,
which grace brings forth, and a good will brings forth;
and so from grade to grade the climb goes on.

The second triad, putting out its buds
in this eternal spring that never knows
a nighttime Ram to strip the branches bare,

sings out hosanna without end, a song
woven of three melodies that sound
through the three orders of joy it is braided in.

Three holy ranks make up this hierarchy:
first the Dominions, and the Virtues next,
and then the third order, which is the Powers.

Then in the two dances next to the last,
the Principalities and Archangels wheel;
the final round is wholly the Angels' play.

All of these orders turn their gaze on high,
and reach so far below that toward God
they all are drawn, and all of them draw upward.

And Dionysius set himself to gaze
on these Orders with such longing that he named them
and ranked them, dividing them just as I do.

Gregory, later, broke from him on this;
but the moment he opened his eyes in this heaven
he smiled at his own error, at himself.

And if a mortal voiced so deep a secret
down there on earth, do not let it amaze you;
the one who saw it here revealed it to him,

with much more truth besides about these circles."
