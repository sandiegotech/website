The Divine Comedy, Inferno: Canto XIX

Inferno: Canto XIX19 of 100

O Simon Magus, O you wretched followers
who take the things of God, which ought to be
the brides of holiness, and like thieves

whore them out for silver and for gold —
now the trumpet has to sound for you,
since this third ditch is where you belong.

We had already climbed, above the next ditch,
out to the part of the ridge that hangs
straight down over the middle of the pit.

O highest Wisdom, how great is the art
you show in heaven, on earth, in the evil world,
and how justly your power deals out its shares!

I saw, along the sides and on the floor,
the livid stone pierced full of holes,
all of one size, and every one of them round.

They looked to me no wider and no narrower
than the ones in my own beautiful Saint John,
the fonts they made for the baptizers to stand in —

one of which, not many years ago,
I broke to save someone who was drowning in it.
Let this be my seal to set the record straight.

Out of the mouth of every hole there stuck
the feet of a sinner, and the legs
up to the calf; the rest stayed in below.

The soles of all of them were set on fire,
and the joints kicked and jerked so hard
they would have snapped any rope or strap.

The way a flame on something oily runs
only across the outer surface,
so the fire ran here from heel to toe.

"Master, who is that one writhing harder
than all his fellows, twisting more," I asked,
"the one a redder flame is feeding on?"

And he to me: "If you want me to carry you
down along the bank that runs the lowest,
you'll learn from him who he is and what he did."

And I: "Whatever pleases you pleases me.
You are my lord; you know I never stray
from what you wish, and you know what I leave unsaid."

So we came out onto the fourth embankment,
turned, and climbed down to the left,
to the bottom of that pierced and narrow floor.

And the good master did not set me down
from off his hip until he'd brought me
to the hole of the one who grieved so with his legs.

"Whoever you are, stuck upside down,
planted like a stake, you sorrowful soul,"
I began to say, "speak, if you can."

I stood there like the friar who hears the confession
of a hired killer already fixed head-first,
who calls him back to put the death off longer.

And he cried out: "Are you standing there already,
are you standing there already, Boniface?
The record lied to me by several years.

Have you had your fill of the wealth so soon,
the wealth that made you unafraid to seize
the beautiful Lady by fraud, and then defile her?"

I became like someone who stands there baffled,
not taking in the answer he's been given,
as if he'd been mocked, with nothing to say back.

Then Virgil said: "Tell him this, and quickly:
'I am not the one, I am not who you think.'"
And I answered him the way I'd been told to.

At that the spirit wrenched with both his feet,
then, sighing, in a voice full of grief,
said to me: "Then what do you want of me?

If you care so much to know just who I am
that you came down the bank for it,
know that I wore the great mantle once,

and I was truly a son of the She-bear,
so hungry to advance the cubs that up there
I pocketed wealth, and down here, myself.

Beneath my head the others are dragged down,
all the ones who went before me in simony,
flattened away along the crack in the rock.

Down there I'll drop in my turn, the moment
that one arrives I took you for
when I shot my sudden question at you.

But I've already toasted my feet up here,
stood like this upside down, far longer
than he will stand with his feet turned red;

for after him will come, from toward the west,
a lawless Shepherd, fouler in his deeds,
just the man to cover him and me.

He'll be a new Jason, like the one we read of
in Maccabees — and as that king was pliant
to Jason, so the king of France to him."

I don't know if I was too bold here,
but this is the only measure I answered him in:
"Tell me — how much treasure did Our Lord

demand of Saint Peter, before he handed
the keys into his keeping? Surely
he asked nothing but 'Follow me.'

And Peter and the rest took no silver,
no gold from Matthias when the lot fell
and chose him for the place the lost soul forfeited.

So stay right where you are — you're justly punished —
and keep good guard over the ill-got cash
that made you so bold against Charles.

And were it not that even here I'm held
by reverence for those highest keys
you carried in the cheerful upper life,

I would use words that cut still harder,
because your greed lays the whole world low,
trampling the good and raising up the vile.

It was you Shepherds the Evangelist saw
when he beheld her who sits upon the waters
committing fornication with the kings —

the one born with the seven heads, who drew
her power and strength from the ten horns,
as long as virtue still pleased her husband.

You have made yourselves a god of gold and silver.
How are you any different from the idol-worshipper,
except he prays to one, and you to a hundred?

Ah, Constantine — what a mother of evil,
not your conversion, but that dowry
the first rich Father took from your hands!"

And while I sang him notes like these,
whether it was anger or his conscience biting,
he thrashed there hard with both his feet.

I do believe it pleased my guide,
so satisfied was the look on his lips
the whole time he listened to the sound of those true words.

So he took me up in both his arms,
and once he had me gathered to his chest,
climbed back up the way he'd come down.

He didn't tire of holding me clasped to him,
but carried me up to the crest of the arch
that crosses from the fourth bank to the fifth.

There he set his burden down with care,
with care, on the crag so steep and rough
it would have been hard going even for goats.

And from there another valley opened to me.
