# The Divine Comedy, Paradiso: Canto IX

Lovely Clemence, once your Charles had made  
the truth plain to me, he told me of the wrongs  
his line would suffer in the years ahead;

but he said, "Keep silent — let the years turn."  
So all I can tell you is this: a grief  
that is just and earned will follow on your loss.

And by now the life within that holy light  
had turned back to the Sun that fills it full,  
as to the good that is enough for all.

Ah, souls deceived, and creatures with no faith,  
who twist your hearts away from such a good  
and fix your eyes on nothing — vanity!

And now, look — another of those splendors  
drew toward me, and it showed it wished to please me  
by brightening on the surface where I watched.

The eyes of Beatrice, still resting on me  
just as before, gave me the sweet assurance  
that she consented to what I desired.

"Be quick, then, blessed spirit," I said, "and pay  
what my wish asks — give me the proof that I  
can throw back to you the thought I think in you."

At this the light, still new and strange to me,  
out of the depths where it had sung before,  
like one who delights in doing good, went on:

"In that stretch of corrupted Italian land  
that lies between Rialto and the springs  
where the Brenta and the Piave have their source,

a hill rises, not climbing very high,  
from which there once came down a burning brand  
that fell on that whole region like a war.

From one same root he and I both were born;  
Cunizza I was called, and here I shine  
because the light of this star mastered me.

But I forgive myself, and gladly, the cause  
that fixed my lot, and it gives me no grief —  
though that might seem too strange to your crowd below.

Of this bright, precious jewel of our heaven,  
the one set nearest me, great fame remained,  
and before that fame dies out,

this hundredth year will be made five times over.  
See, then, whether a man should make himself  
excellent, so a second life outlasts the first.

The mob shut in between the Adige  
and the Tagliamento doesn't think this way,  
and even scourged, it still will not repent.

But soon it will come: Padua at the marsh  
will change the water that bathes Vicenza,  
because those people set themselves against their duty.

And where the Sile and the Cagnano meet,  
one lords it, marching with his head held high —  
the net is being woven now to catch him.

Feltre too will weep her godless shepherd's crime,  
so monstrous a thing that for the like of it  
no man was ever locked away in Malta.

Wide indeed would have to be the vat  
to hold the blood of all the Ferrarese,  
and weary the man who weighed it ounce by ounce —

the blood this courteous priest will offer up  
to prove himself a loyal party man;  
and such gifts suit the way they live down there.

Above us there are mirrors — you call them Thrones —  
from which God shines on us as He who judges,  
so that these words of mine seem good to say."

Here it fell silent, and it had the look  
of one turned elsewhere now, taking again  
the wheeling round it held to before.

The other joy, the one already known to me,  
became a thing that blazed before my eyes  
like a fine ruby struck full by the sun.

Up here, gladness is gained as brightness,  
as down there it shows as a smile; but below,  
the shade grows dark outside as the mind grows sad.

"God sees all things, and your sight, blessed spirit,  
is in Him," I said, "so that no wish of His  
can ever be a thing hidden from you.

Your voice, then — the voice that forever gladdens  
the heavens with the singing of those holy fires  
that make themselves a cowl out of six wings —

why does it not yet answer what I long for?  
I would not wait for you to ask me first,  
if I were in you as you are in me."

"The largest of the valleys where the waters  
spread out," its words began at once, "not counting  
that sea which garlands the whole earth around,

runs so far east between its warring shores,  
against the sun, that the line which once was its  
horizon becomes, far off, its noon.

I lived on the shore of that valley, in the land  
between the Ebro and the Magra, whose short course  
parts the Tuscan from the Genoese.

Nearly the same sunset, the same sunrise,  
fall on Bougie and on the town I came from —  
the town that once made its own harbor hot with blood.

Folco the people called me, those who knew  
my name; and now this heaven takes my mark  
the way that I once took the mark of it.

For the daughter of Belus burned no hotter than I,  
wronging both Sychaeus and Creusa,  
as long as it still became my youthful hair —

nor that girl of Rhodope, deceived  
by Demophoon, nor Hercules himself  
when he had shut up Iole in his heart.

Yet here we do not repent — we smile,  
not at the sin, which never comes back to mind,  
but at the Power that ordered and foresaw.

Here we look into the art that makes so fair  
an effect, and we make out the good  
by which the world above turns the world below.

But so that you may carry from this sphere  
every wish that was born in you while here,  
wholly satisfied, I must go further still.

You'd like to know who is within this light  
that sparkles here beside me, glittering  
like a ray of sun in clear, untroubled water.

Then know that resting in there, at peace,  
is Rahab — joined now to our order, and stamped  
into its very highest rank, its crown.

Into this heaven, where the cone of shadow  
cast by your world comes to its point, she was raised  
first of all the souls of Christ's triumph.

It was only fitting to leave her in some heaven  
as a palm of that high victory He won  
with the one palm and the other on the Cross,

because she favored Joshua's first proud deed,  
his glory there in the Holy Land —  
a land that barely stirs the Pope's memory now.

Your city — which is an offshoot of the one  
who was the first to turn his back on his Maker,  
the one whose grasping envy is so wept —

your city mints and scatters the cursed flower  
that has driven sheep and lambs alike astray,  
since it has turned the shepherd to a wolf.

For this the Gospel and the mighty Doctors  
are left to rot, and only the Decretals  
get studied — you can see it in their worn-out margins.

On this the Pope and Cardinals are bent;  
their thoughts never travel as far as Nazareth,  
there where Gabriel once spread wide his wings.

But the Vatican, and those other holy parts  
of Rome that have been the burying ground  
for the soldiery that followed after Peter,

will soon be free of this adultery."
