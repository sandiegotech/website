# Genesis, Chapter 8

The Waters Go Down

And God remembered Noah, and all the wild animals and all the livestock with him in the ark. And God made a wind pass over the earth, and the waters went down. The springs of the deep and the windows of the sky were closed, and the rain from the sky was held back, and the waters drained off the earth steadily. At the end of a hundred and fifty days the waters had gone down, and in the seventh month, on the seventeenth day of the month, the ark came to rest on the mountains of Ararat. The waters kept going down until the tenth month; in the tenth month, on the first day, the tops of the mountains appeared.

At the end of forty days Noah opened the window of the ark he had made, and he sent out a raven. It went back and forth until the waters had dried up from the earth. Then he sent out a dove, to see whether the waters had gone down from the face of the ground. But the dove found no place to set the sole of her foot, and she came back to him in the ark, because there was water over the face of the whole earth. He put out his hand and took her and brought her back inside with him. He waited another seven days and sent the dove out of the ark again. And the dove came back to him in the evening — and there in her beak was a freshly picked olive leaf. So Noah knew that the waters had gone down from the earth. He waited another seven days and sent out the dove, and she did not come back to him again.

In the six hundred and first year, in the first month, on the first day of the month, the waters had dried up from the earth. And Noah pulled back the covering of the ark and looked, and there it was: the face of the ground was dry. And in the second month, on the twenty-seventh day of the month, the earth was dry.

And God said to Noah, “Go out of the ark, you and your wife and your sons and your sons' wives with you. Bring out with you every living thing that is with you, from all flesh — birds and animals and every crawling thing that crawls on the earth — so they can swarm over the earth and be fruitful and multiply on it.” So Noah went out, and his sons and his wife and his sons' wives with him. Every wild animal, every crawling thing, every bird, everything that moves on the earth — they went out of the ark by families.

And Noah built an altar to the LORD, and he took some of every clean animal and every clean bird and offered burnt offerings on the altar. And the LORD smelled the soothing smell, and the LORD said in his heart, “I will never again curse the ground because of human beings, even though the shape of the human heart is evil from youth. And I will never again strike down every living thing, as I have done. As long as the earth lasts — seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night will never stop.”
