Genesis, Chapter 30

The Sisters, and the Flocks

Chapter 3030 of 50

And when Rachel saw that she was bearing Jacob no children, Rachel envied her sister. And she said to Jacob, “Give me children, or I am a dead woman.” And Jacob's anger flared against Rachel, and he said, “Am I in God's place? He is the one who has held back the fruit of your womb.” And she said, “Here is my slave Bilhah. Go in to her, and let her give birth on my knees, so that I too may be built up through her.” So she gave him Bilhah her slave as a wife, and Jacob went in to her. And Bilhah conceived and bore Jacob a son. And Rachel said, “God has judged my case, and he has also heard my voice and given me a son.” That is why she named him Dan. And Bilhah, Rachel's slave, conceived again and bore Jacob a second son. And Rachel said, “With mighty wrestlings I have wrestled with my sister, and I have won.” And she named him Naphtali.

When Leah saw that she had stopped bearing, she took Zilpah her slave and gave her to Jacob as a wife. And Zilpah, Leah's slave, bore Jacob a son. And Leah said, “What luck!” And she named him Gad. And Zilpah, Leah's slave, bore Jacob a second son. And Leah said, “How happy I am — women will call me happy.” And she named him Asher.

And in the days of the wheat harvest Reuben went out and found mandrakes in the field, and he brought them to Leah his mother. And Rachel said to Leah, “Please give me some of your son's mandrakes.” And she said to her, “Is it a small thing that you took my husband? Now you want to take my son's mandrakes too?” And Rachel said, “All right — he can lie with you tonight, in exchange for your son's mandrakes.” And Jacob came in from the field in the evening, and Leah went out to meet him and said, “You are coming in to me. I have hired you, fair and square, with my son's mandrakes.” And he lay with her that night. And God listened to Leah, and she conceived and bore Jacob a fifth son. And Leah said, “God has given me my wages, because I gave my slave to my husband.” And she named him Issachar. And Leah conceived again and bore Jacob a sixth son. And Leah said, “God has given me a good gift. This time my husband will honor me, because I have borne him six sons.” And she named him Zebulun. And afterward she bore a daughter and named her Dinah.

And God remembered Rachel, and God listened to her and opened her womb. And she conceived and bore a son, and she said, “God has taken away my disgrace.” And she named him Joseph, saying, “May the LORD add me another son.”

And when Rachel had borne Joseph, Jacob said to Laban, “Send me off, so I can go to my own place, to my own land. Give me my wives and my children, the ones I worked for, and let me go. You know the work I have done for you.” And Laban said to him, “If I have found favor in your eyes — I have learned by divination that the LORD has blessed me because of you.” And he said, “Name your wages, and I will pay them.” And he said to him, “You know how I have worked for you, and what has become of your livestock with me. What you had before I came was little, and it has burst out into a multitude, and the LORD has blessed you wherever I turned. And now, when do I get to provide for my own household?”

And he said, “What should I give you?” And Jacob said, “Don't give me anything. If you will do this one thing for me, I will go back to pasturing your flock and keeping it. Let me go through your whole flock today and take out of it every speckled and spotted animal, and every dark-colored one among the lambs, and the spotted and speckled among the goats. Those will be my wages. And my honesty will testify for me later, when you come to look over my wages: anything with me that is not speckled or spotted among the goats, or dark among the lambs, will count as stolen.” And Laban said, “Good — let it be as you say.” And that same day he took out the male goats that were streaked and spotted, and all the female goats that were speckled and spotted, every one with white on it, and all the dark ones among the lambs, and he put them in the hands of his sons. And he set three days' journey between himself and Jacob, while Jacob pastured the rest of Laban's flock.

And Jacob took fresh rods of poplar and almond and plane tree, and peeled white streaks in them, laying bare the white on the rods. And he set the rods he had peeled in front of the flocks, in the troughs, in the watering channels where the flocks came to drink — and they went into heat when they came to drink. And the flocks bred in front of the rods, and they bore young that were streaked, speckled, and spotted. And Jacob separated the lambs, and he turned the faces of the flock toward the streaked and all the dark ones in Laban's flock. And he put his own droves by themselves and did not put them with Laban's flock. And whenever the sturdy animals were breeding, Jacob would set the rods in the troughs in front of their eyes, so they would breed among the rods. But when the animals were weak he did not set them there — so the weak ones went to Laban and the sturdy ones to Jacob. And the man burst out and out: he had large flocks, and female and male slaves, and camels and donkeys.
