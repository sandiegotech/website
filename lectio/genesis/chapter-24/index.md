# Genesis, Chapter 24

Rebekah at the Well

And Abraham was old, well along in years, and the LORD had blessed Abraham in everything. And Abraham said to the senior servant of his household, the one in charge of everything he had, “Put your hand under my thigh. I want you to swear by the LORD, God of heaven and God of the earth, that you will not take a wife for my son from the daughters of the Canaanites I live among. You will go to my own country, to my own kindred, and take a wife for my son Isaac.” And the servant said to him, “Suppose the woman is not willing to follow me to this land. Should I take your son back to the land you came from?” And Abraham said to him, “Be careful not to take my son back there. The LORD, God of heaven, who took me from my father's house and from the land of my birth, who spoke to me and swore to me, ‘To your offspring I will give this land’ — he will send his messenger ahead of you, and you will take a wife for my son from there. And if the woman is not willing to follow you, you are released from this oath of mine. Only do not take my son back there.” And the servant put his hand under the thigh of Abraham his master and swore to him about this matter.

And the servant took ten of his master's camels and set out, with all kinds of good things from his master in his hand. And he got up and went to Aram-naharaim, to the city of Nahor. And he made the camels kneel outside the city by the well of water, toward evening, the time when the women come out to draw water. And he said, “LORD, God of my master Abraham, let it turn out well for me today, and show kindness to my master Abraham. Look — I am standing here by the spring of water, and the daughters of the men of the city are coming out to draw water. Let it be that the young woman I say to, ‘Please tip your jar so I can drink,’ and she says, ‘Drink — and I will water your camels too,’ let her be the one you have appointed for your servant Isaac. And by that I will know that you have shown kindness to my master.”

And before he had finished speaking — there was Rebekah coming out, who was born to Bethuel son of Milcah, the wife of Nahor, Abraham's brother, with her jar on her shoulder. And the young woman was very beautiful, a virgin; no man had known her. And she went down to the spring and filled her jar and came up. And the servant ran to meet her and said, “Please give me a little water from your jar.” And she said, “Drink, my lord.” And she hurried and lowered her jar onto her hand and gave him a drink. And when she had finished giving him a drink, she said, “I will draw for your camels too, until they have finished drinking.” And she hurried and emptied her jar into the trough and ran back to the well to draw, and she drew for all his camels. And the man was watching her in silence, waiting to know whether the LORD had made his journey succeed or not.

And when the camels had finished drinking, the man took a gold nose ring weighing half a shekel and two bracelets for her wrists weighing ten shekels of gold, and said, “Whose daughter are you? Tell me — is there room in your father's house for us to spend the night?” And she said to him, “I am the daughter of Bethuel, the son Milcah bore to Nahor.” And she said to him, “We have plenty of straw and feed, and room to spend the night.” And the man bowed his head and bowed down to the LORD, and said, “Blessed be the LORD, God of my master Abraham, who has not abandoned his kindness and his faithfulness toward my master. As for me, the LORD has led me on the road to the house of my master's kin.”

And the young woman ran and told her mother's household about these things. Now Rebekah had a brother named Laban, and Laban ran out to the man, to the spring. When he saw the nose ring, and the bracelets on his sister's wrists, and when he heard the words of Rebekah his sister — “This is what the man said to me” — he came out to the man, and there he was, standing with the camels at the spring. And he said, “Come in, blessed of the LORD. Why are you standing outside? I have cleared the house, and made room for the camels.” And the man came into the house, and Laban unloaded the camels and gave straw and feed to the camels and water to wash his feet and the feet of the men with him. And food was set in front of him, but he said, “I will not eat until I have said what I came to say.” And he said, “Speak.”

And he said, “I am Abraham's servant. And the LORD has blessed my master greatly, and he has become great. He has given him flocks and herds, silver and gold, male and female slaves, camels and donkeys. And Sarah, my master's wife, bore a son to my master in her old age, and he has given him everything he has. And my master made me swear: ‘You will not take a wife for my son from the daughters of the Canaanites in whose land I live. Instead you will go to my father's house, to my clan, and take a wife for my son.’ And I said to my master, ‘Suppose the woman will not follow me.’ And he said to me, ‘The LORD, in front of whom I have walked, will send his messenger with you and make your journey succeed, and you will take a wife for my son from my clan, from my father's house. Then you will be released from my oath — when you come to my clan. And if they will not give her to you, you are released from my oath.’

“And I came to the spring today, and I said, ‘LORD, God of my master Abraham, if you are going to make the journey I am on succeed — look, I am standing by the spring of water. Let it be that the young woman who comes out to draw, and I say to her, Please give me a little water from your jar to drink, and she says to me, You drink, and I will draw for your camels too — let her be the woman the LORD has appointed for my master's son.’ And before I had finished speaking in my heart — there was Rebekah coming out with her jar on her shoulder. And she went down to the spring and drew, and I said to her, ‘Please give me a drink.’ And she hurried and lowered her jar from her shoulder and said, ‘Drink — and I will water your camels too.’ So I drank, and she watered the camels too. And I asked her, ‘Whose daughter are you?’ And she said, ‘The daughter of Bethuel, Nahor's son, whom Milcah bore to him.’ And I put the ring on her nose and the bracelets on her wrists. And I bowed my head and bowed down to the LORD, and blessed the LORD, God of my master Abraham, who led me on the true road to take the daughter of my master's brother for his son. And now, if you are going to deal kindly and faithfully with my master, tell me. And if not, tell me, so I can turn to the right or to the left.”

And Laban and Bethuel answered, “This has come from the LORD. We cannot say anything to you, bad or good. Rebekah is in front of you. Take her and go, and let her be the wife of your master's son, as the LORD has spoken.” And when Abraham's servant heard their words, he bowed to the ground before the LORD. And the servant brought out objects of silver and objects of gold and clothing, and gave them to Rebekah, and he gave costly gifts to her brother and her mother. And he ate and drank, he and the men with him, and they spent the night.

And in the morning they got up, and he said, “Send me off to my master.” And her brother and her mother said, “Let the young woman stay with us a while, ten days or so. After that she can go.” And he said to them, “Don't hold me back, now that the LORD has made my journey succeed. Send me off, so I can go to my master.” And they said, “We will call the young woman and ask her.” And they called Rebekah and said to her, “Will you go with this man?” And she said, “I will go.” So they sent off Rebekah their sister, and her nurse, and Abraham's servant and his men. And they blessed Rebekah and said to her, “Our sister, may you become thousands upon thousands, and may your offspring take possession of the gate of those who hate them.” And Rebekah got up, with her young women, and they mounted the camels and followed the man. And the servant took Rebekah and went.

Now Isaac had come from the direction of Beer-lahai-roi; he was living in the Negev. And Isaac went out to walk in the field toward evening, and he looked up and saw — there were camels coming. And Rebekah looked up and saw Isaac, and she got down from the camel. And she said to the servant, “Who is that man walking in the field to meet us?” And the servant said, “That is my master.” So she took her veil and covered herself. And the servant told Isaac everything he had done. And Isaac brought her into the tent of Sarah his mother, and he took Rebekah, and she became his wife, and he loved her. And Isaac was comforted after his mother's death.
