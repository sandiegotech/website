# Genesis, Chapter 25

Two Nations

And Abraham took another wife, whose name was Keturah. And she bore him Zimran, Jokshan, Medan, Midian, Ishbak, and Shuah. And Jokshan fathered Sheba and Dedan, and the sons of Dedan were the Asshurim, the Letushim, and the Leummim. And the sons of Midian: Ephah, Epher, Hanoch, Abida, and Eldaah. All these were the descendants of Keturah. And Abraham gave everything he had to Isaac. But to the sons of his concubines Abraham gave gifts, and while he was still alive he sent them away from Isaac his son, eastward, to the land of the east.

And these are the days of the years of Abraham's life: a hundred and seventy-five years. And Abraham breathed his last and died at a good old age, an old man, full of years, and he was gathered to his people. And Isaac and Ishmael his sons buried him in the cave of Machpelah, in the field of Ephron son of Zohar the Hittite, facing Mamre — the field Abraham bought from the Hittites. There Abraham was buried, and Sarah his wife. And after Abraham's death, God blessed Isaac his son. And Isaac settled near Beer-lahai-roi.

And these are the generations of Ishmael, Abraham's son, whom Hagar the Egyptian, Sarah's slave, bore to Abraham. These are the names of Ishmael's sons, by their names, in the order of their birth: Nebaioth the firstborn of Ishmael, then Kedar, Adbeel, Mibsam, Mishma, Dumah, Massa, Hadad, Tema, Jetur, Naphish, and Kedemah. These are Ishmael's sons, and these are their names, by their villages and their camps — twelve chieftains of their tribes. And these are the years of Ishmael's life: a hundred and thirty-seven years. And he breathed his last and died, and was gathered to his people. And they lived from Havilah to Shur, which faces Egypt, in the direction of Assyria. He settled at odds with all his kin.

And these are the generations of Isaac, Abraham's son: Abraham fathered Isaac. And Isaac was forty years old when he took Rebekah as his wife, the daughter of Bethuel the Aramean of Paddan-aram, the sister of Laban the Aramean. And Isaac pleaded with the LORD for his wife, because she was barren. And the LORD granted his plea, and Rebekah his wife conceived. And the children shoved each other inside her, and she said, “If it is like this, why am I?” And she went to ask the LORD. And the LORD said to her: “Two nations are in your womb, and two peoples will part from your body. One people will be stronger than the other, and the older will serve the younger.”

And when her days to give birth were completed — there were twins in her womb. And the first came out red, all of him like a hairy coat, and they named him Esau. And after that his brother came out, and his hand was gripping Esau's heel, and he was named Jacob. Isaac was sixty years old when she bore them. And the boys grew up, and Esau was a man who knew hunting, a man of the open country; and Jacob was a quiet man, staying among the tents. And Isaac loved Esau, because he had a taste for wild game; but Rebekah loved Jacob.

And Jacob was boiling a stew, and Esau came in from the open country, and he was exhausted. And Esau said to Jacob, “Let me gulp down some of that red stuff — that red stuff — because I am exhausted.” That is why he was named Edom. And Jacob said, “Sell me your birthright today.” And Esau said, “Look, I am about to die. What good is a birthright to me?” And Jacob said, “Swear to me today.” And he swore to him, and he sold his birthright to Jacob. And Jacob gave Esau bread and lentil stew, and he ate and drank and got up and went off. So Esau despised his birthright.
