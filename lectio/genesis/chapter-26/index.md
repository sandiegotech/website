# Genesis, Chapter 26

Isaac Digs the Wells Again

And there was a famine in the land, besides the first famine that happened in Abraham's time, and Isaac went to Abimelech king of the Philistines, at Gerar. And the LORD appeared to him and said, “Do not go down to Egypt. Live in the land I tell you. Stay in this land as a stranger, and I will be with you and bless you — because to you and to your offspring I will give all these lands, and I will keep the oath I swore to Abraham your father. And I will multiply your offspring like the stars of the sky, and I will give your offspring all these lands, and in your offspring all the nations of the earth will be blessed — because Abraham listened to my voice and kept my charge, my commands, my statutes, and my instructions.”

So Isaac settled in Gerar. And the men of the place asked about his wife, and he said, “She is my sister,” because he was afraid to say “my wife” — thinking, the men of the place might kill me over Rebekah, because she is beautiful. And when he had been there a long time, Abimelech king of the Philistines looked down through a window and saw — there was Isaac, playing with Rebekah his wife. And Abimelech called Isaac and said, “So she really is your wife! How could you say, ‘She is my sister’?” And Isaac said to him, “Because I thought I might die over her.” And Abimelech said, “What is this you have done to us? One of the people might easily have lain with your wife, and you would have brought guilt on us.” And Abimelech ordered all the people: “Whoever touches this man or his wife will certainly be put to death.”

And Isaac sowed in that land and reaped a hundredfold that same year, and the LORD blessed him. And the man became great, and he kept getting greater until he was very great. He had holdings of flocks and holdings of herds and a large household, and the Philistines envied him. And all the wells his father's servants had dug in the days of Abraham his father, the Philistines had stopped up and filled with dirt. And Abimelech said to Isaac, “Go away from us. You have become far too powerful for us.” So Isaac left there and camped in the Valley of Gerar and settled there.

And Isaac dug again the wells of water they had dug in the days of Abraham his father, which the Philistines had stopped up after Abraham's death, and he gave them the same names his father had given them. And Isaac's servants dug in the valley and found there a well of fresh water. And the herders of Gerar quarreled with Isaac's herders: “The water is ours.” So he named the well Esek, because they fought with him over it. And they dug another well, and they quarreled over that one too, and he named it Sitnah. And he moved on from there and dug another well, and over that one they did not quarrel, and he named it Rehoboth, and said, “Now the LORD has made room for us, and we will be fruitful in the land.”

And he went up from there to Beersheba. And the LORD appeared to him that night and said, “I am the God of Abraham your father. Do not be afraid, because I am with you. I will bless you and multiply your offspring, for the sake of Abraham my servant.” And he built an altar there and called on the name of the LORD, and pitched his tent there, and Isaac's servants dug a well there.

And Abimelech came to him from Gerar, with Ahuzzath his adviser and Phicol the commander of his army. And Isaac said to them, “Why have you come to me, when you hate me and sent me away from you?” And they said, “We have seen clearly that the LORD is with you, and we said, let there be an oath between us — between us and you — and let us cut a covenant with you: that you will do us no harm, just as we did not touch you, and did nothing but good for you, and sent you away in peace. You are now the blessed of the LORD.” And he made them a feast, and they ate and drank. And they got up early in the morning and swore to each other, and Isaac sent them off, and they left him in peace. And that same day Isaac's servants came and told him about the well they had dug, and said to him, “We have found water.” And he named it Shibah. That is why the name of the city is Beersheba to this day.

And Esau was forty years old when he took as wives Judith daughter of Beeri the Hittite and Basemath daughter of Elon the Hittite. And they were a bitterness of spirit to Isaac and Rebekah.
