# Genesis, Chapter 33

The Face of a Brother

And Jacob looked up and saw — there was Esau coming, and four hundred men with him. And he divided the children between Leah and Rachel and the two slave women. And he put the slave women and their children first, and Leah and her children after them, and Rachel and Joseph last. And he himself crossed over ahead of them, and he bowed to the ground seven times, until he came near his brother. And Esau ran to meet him and threw his arms around him and fell on his neck and kissed him. And they wept.

And he looked up and saw the women and the children, and said, “Who are these with you?” And he said, “The children God has graciously given your servant.” And the slave women came near, they and their children, and they bowed. And Leah too came near with her children, and they bowed. And afterward Joseph came near, and Rachel, and they bowed.

And he said, “What did you mean by this whole camp I met?” And he said, “To find favor in the eyes of my lord.” And Esau said, “I have plenty, my brother. Keep what is yours.” And Jacob said, “No — please, if I have found favor in your eyes, take my gift from my hand. For seeing your face is like seeing the face of God, and you have received me kindly. Please take my blessing that has been brought to you, because God has been gracious to me, and because I have everything.” And he pressed him, and he took it.

And he said, “Let's move on and go, and I will go alongside you.” And he said to him, “My lord knows that the children are frail, and the flocks and herds that are nursing are with me. If they are driven hard for a single day, all the flocks will die. Let my lord go on ahead of his servant, and I will move along slowly, at the pace of the livestock in front of me and the pace of the children, until I come to my lord at Seir.” And Esau said, “Then let me leave some of the people who are with me with you.” And he said, “Why do that? Let me find favor in the eyes of my lord.” So Esau turned back that day on his way to Seir.

And Jacob traveled to Succoth and built himself a house and made shelters for his livestock. That is why the place is named Succoth. And Jacob came safely to the city of Shechem, in the land of Canaan, when he came from Paddan-aram, and he camped in front of the city. And he bought the piece of land where he had pitched his tent from the sons of Hamor, Shechem's father, for a hundred pieces of silver. And he set up an altar there and called it El-elohe-Israel.
