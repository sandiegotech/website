# Genesis, Chapter 32

The Night at the Jabbok

And Jacob went on his way, and messengers of God met him. And when Jacob saw them he said, “This is God's camp.” And he named that place Mahanaim. And Jacob sent messengers ahead of him to Esau his brother, to the land of Seir, the country of Edom. And he ordered them: “Say this to my lord Esau — this is what your servant Jacob says: I have been living with Laban as a stranger, and I stayed until now. And I have oxen and donkeys, flocks and male and female slaves. And I have sent to tell my lord, so I may find favor in your eyes.” And the messengers came back to Jacob and said, “We went to your brother Esau, and he is coming to meet you — and four hundred men with him.”

And Jacob was very afraid, and it pressed in on him. And he split the people who were with him, and the flocks and the herds and the camels, into two camps. And he said, “If Esau comes to the one camp and strikes it, the camp that is left will get away.” And Jacob said, “God of my father Abraham, God of my father Isaac — the LORD who said to me, ‘Go back to your country and your kindred and I will do you good’ — I am too small for all the kindnesses and all the faithfulness you have shown your servant. With only my staff I crossed this Jordan, and now I have become two camps. Save me, please, from the hand of my brother, from the hand of Esau, because I am afraid of him — that he will come and strike me down, mothers with their children. And you said, ‘I will certainly do you good, and make your offspring like the sand of the sea, too many to be counted.’”

And he spent that night there, and out of what he had with him he took a gift for Esau his brother: two hundred female goats and twenty males, two hundred ewes and twenty rams, thirty nursing camels with their young, forty cows and ten bulls, twenty female donkeys and ten males. And he put them in the hands of his servants, each drove by itself, and said to his servants, “Cross over ahead of me, and leave a space between one drove and the next.” And he ordered the first one: “When Esau my brother meets you and asks you, ‘Whose man are you? Where are you going? Whose are these ahead of you?’ — then you say, ‘They belong to your servant Jacob. It is a gift, sent to my lord Esau. And look, he is behind us.’” And he ordered the second and the third and everyone following the droves the same way: “This is what you are to say to Esau when you find him. And say: ‘Look, your servant Jacob is behind us.’” Because he said to himself, “I will cover his face with the gift going ahead of me, and after that I will see his face. Maybe he will lift up my face.” So the gift crossed over ahead of him, and he spent that night in the camp.

And he got up that night and took his two wives and his two slave women and his eleven children and crossed the ford of the Jabbok. He took them and sent them across the stream, and he sent across everything he had. And Jacob was left alone. And a man wrestled with him until the breaking of day. And when he saw that he could not beat him, he struck the socket of his hip, and Jacob's hip was wrenched as he wrestled with him. And he said, “Let me go, because the day is breaking.” And he said, “I will not let you go unless you bless me.”

And he said to him, “What is your name?” And he said, “Jacob.” And he said, “Your name will no longer be Jacob but Israel — because you have wrestled with God and with men, and you have won.” And Jacob asked and said, “Tell me your name.” And he said, “Why is it you ask my name?” And he blessed him there.

And Jacob named the place Peniel — “because I have seen God face to face, and my life was spared.” And the sun rose on him as he passed Penuel, and he was limping because of his hip. That is why to this day the Israelites do not eat the thigh muscle that is on the socket of the hip: because he struck the socket of Jacob's hip at the thigh muscle.
