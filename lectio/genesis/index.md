# Genesis

Anonymous · Ancient Israel

- c. 900–500 BCE
- Western tradition
- Scripture
- Lectio Edition 2026

The first book of the Bible. Eleven chapters about everyone: creation, a garden, a murder, a flood, a tower. Then thirty-nine about one family: Abraham and Sarah, Isaac and Rebekah, Jacob, Leah and Rachel, Joseph and his brothers.

## The Hebrew is plain. Four hundred years of English have made it grand.

Genesis is spare. The Hebrew has a small vocabulary, repeats itself on purpose, and almost never explains. It says what happened and leaves the judgment to the reader.

The King James Version of 1611 is one of the great works of English, and it adds a solemnity the Hebrew does not have. It also varies words the Hebrew repeats. Abraham answers "Here I am" three times in one chapter, to God, to his son, and to the messenger who stops the knife. The King James gives it three ways.

The Lectio Edition keeps the refrains word for word, keeps more of the Hebrew's "and" than modern taste likes, and leaves a puzzle as a puzzle. The King James stays in the app beside it.

**Where it stands:** In the app, the King James is still what opens by default for Genesis. The Lectio Edition is complete, all fifty chapters. It is a beta, and it becomes the default after the readers' review and the editor's read.

## What this edition decided.

These rules come from the charter written for the biblical books and from the review.

- Where the Hebrew is unknown or can be read two ways, the edition prints the received text and leaves it open. A scholar's guess goes in a note, not in the verse. "Gopher wood" stays "gopher wood". Nobody knows what it was.
- Refrains and repeated words are the same each time.
- The divine name is "the LORD" in capitals, the convention English Bibles have used since Tyndale. It marks that a substitution is being made.
- *Shamayim* is "sky" for the physical sky and "heaven" where it is God's place or title.
- *Hesed* is "kindness".
- The poems inside Genesis are verse and should be set as lines. The build cannot do that yet. See below.

### Where it differs from the older translations

The edition records 34 divergences: places where it departs from the older English, each with its reason. Here are 7. The full record is in the app, one tap from the text.

### בְּרֵאשִׁית בָּרָא (*bereshit bara*)

1:1

**Older:** "In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth." (King James)

**Lectio:** When God began to create the heavens and the earth

The Hebrew most likely opens a clause that runs on to "God said, Let there be light". Creation starts with a scene already dark and wet. Rashi read it this way in the eleventh century.

### תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ (*tohu wabohu*)

1:2

**Older:** "without form, and void" (King James)

**Lectio:** a shapeless waste

The Hebrew is a rhyming pair. In the same verse *ruach* is breath, wind and spirit at once, and the edition has "the breath of God hovered".

### עֵזֶר כְּנֶגְדּוֹ (*ezer kenegdo*)

2:18

**Older:** "an help meet for him" (King James)

**Lectio:** a partner who matches him

*Ezer* is used most often of God and implies no lower rank. *Kenegdo* means his counterpart.

### לֶךְ־לְךָ (*lekh lekha*)

12:1

**Older:** "Get thee out of thy country" (King James)

**Lectio:** out of your country

Two blunt Hebrew words. The verse begins "Go". Everything after it follows from a man obeying an order that gives no reason.

### כָּרַת בְּרִית (*karat berit*)

15:18

**Older:** "the LORD made a covenant with Abram" (King James)

**Lectio:** the LORD cut a covenant with Abram

The Hebrew idiom is to cut a covenant, and here it is literal. Animals are cut in half and fire passes between the pieces. "Made" hides the knife.

### הִנֵּנִי (*hineni*)

22:1, 22:7, 22:11

**Older:** "Behold, here I am", "Here am I" (King James)

**Lectio:** Here I am

One word, three times. It is the spine of the chapter. Varying it breaks it.

### סֻלָּם (*sullam*)

28:12

**Older:** "a ladder" (King James)

**Lectio:** a stairway

The word occurs only here. The picture is most likely the ramp of a temple tower, a fixed way between the sky and a stone in open country.
