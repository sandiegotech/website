# Genesis, Chapter 49

Jacob Blesses His Sons

And Jacob called his sons and said, “Gather, and I will tell you what will happen to you in the days to come. Come together and listen, sons of Jacob; listen to Israel your father.

“Reuben, you are my firstborn, my strength and the first fruit of my vigor, first in rank and first in power. Unstable as water, you will not be first — for you went up to your father's bed. Then you defiled it. My couch he went up to!

“Simeon and Levi are brothers; their weapons are tools of violence. Let my soul not enter their council; let my honor not be joined to their assembly. For in their anger they killed men, and at their pleasure they hamstrung oxen. Cursed be their anger, for it is fierce, and their fury, for it is cruel. I will divide them in Jacob, and scatter them in Israel.

“Judah — you, your brothers will praise. Your hand will be on the neck of your enemies; your father's sons will bow down to you. A lion's cub is Judah; from the prey, my son, you have gone up. He crouches, he lies down like a lion, like a lioness — who will rouse him? The scepter will not depart from Judah, nor the ruler's staff from between his feet, until Shiloh comes; and the obedience of peoples is his. He ties his donkey to the vine, his donkey's colt to the choice vine. He washes his clothes in wine, his robe in the blood of grapes. His eyes are darker than wine, and his teeth whiter than milk.

“Zebulun will live by the shore of the sea. He will be a shore for ships, and his flank will be at Sidon.

“Issachar is a strong-boned donkey, lying down between the saddlebags. He saw that rest was good, and that the land was pleasant, and he bent his shoulder to the load and became a slave at forced labor.

“Dan will judge his people as one of the tribes of Israel. Let Dan be a snake on the road, a viper on the path, that bites the horse's heels so its rider falls backward. For your salvation I wait, LORD.

“Gad — raiders will raid him, but he will raid at their heels.

“From Asher, his bread is rich, and he will provide the delicacies of a king.

“Naphtali is a doe let loose, who gives lovely words.

“Joseph is a fruitful bough, a fruitful bough by a spring, whose branches climb over the wall. The archers harassed him bitterly; they shot at him and bore him a grudge. But his bow stayed firm, and the arms of his hands were quick — by the hands of the Mighty One of Jacob, from there, the Shepherd, the Stone of Israel; by the God of your father, who will help you, and Shaddai, who will bless you with blessings of the sky above, blessings of the deep that lies below, blessings of the breasts and of the womb. The blessings of your father are mightier than the blessings of my ancestors, up to the bounds of the everlasting hills. May they be on the head of Joseph, on the brow of the one set apart from his brothers.

“Benjamin is a wolf that tears. In the morning he eats the prey, and in the evening he divides the spoil.”

All these are the tribes of Israel, twelve, and this is what their father said to them. He blessed them, each with the blessing that was his. And he commanded them and said to them, “I am about to be gathered to my people. Bury me with my fathers, in the cave that is in the field of Ephron the Hittite, in the cave in the field of Machpelah, which faces Mamre, in the land of Canaan — the field Abraham bought from Ephron the Hittite as a burial holding. There they buried Abraham and Sarah his wife. There they buried Isaac and Rebekah his wife. And there I buried Leah. The field and the cave in it were bought from the Hittites.” And when Jacob finished commanding his sons, he drew his feet up into the bed, and he breathed his last and was gathered to his people.
