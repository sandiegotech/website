# Genesis, Chapter 40

The Cupbearer and the Baker

And after these things the cupbearer of the king of Egypt and the baker offended their lord, the king of Egypt. And Pharaoh was furious with his two officials, the chief cupbearer and the chief baker, and he put them under guard in the house of the captain of the guard, in the prison, the place where Joseph was confined. And the captain of the guard assigned Joseph to them, and he attended them. And they were under guard for some time.

And the two of them dreamed a dream, each his own dream, in a single night, each dream with its own meaning — the cupbearer and the baker of the king of Egypt, who were confined in the prison. And Joseph came to them in the morning and saw them — and they were troubled. And he asked Pharaoh's officials who were with him under guard in his master's house, “Why are your faces so dark today?” And they said to him, “We dreamed a dream, and there is no one to interpret it.” And Joseph said to them, “Don't interpretations belong to God? Tell me, please.”

And the chief cupbearer told his dream to Joseph. He said to him, “In my dream — there was a vine in front of me, and on the vine three branches. And as it budded, its blossoms came out, and its clusters ripened into grapes. And Pharaoh's cup was in my hand, and I took the grapes and squeezed them into Pharaoh's cup, and I put the cup in Pharaoh's palm.” And Joseph said to him, “This is its meaning. The three branches are three days. In three days Pharaoh will lift up your head and put you back in your post, and you will put Pharaoh's cup in his hand, as you used to do when you were his cupbearer. But remember me when it goes well with you, and please show me kindness: mention me to Pharaoh, and get me out of this house. For I was stolen, stolen from the land of the Hebrews, and here too I have done nothing that they should have put me in the pit.”

And the chief baker saw that he had interpreted well, and he said to Joseph, “I too, in my dream — there were three baskets of white bread on my head. And in the top basket there was every kind of food for Pharaoh, baker's work, and the birds were eating it out of the basket on my head.” And Joseph answered, “This is its meaning. The three baskets are three days. In three days Pharaoh will lift up your head — off you — and hang you on a tree, and the birds will eat your flesh off you.”

And on the third day, Pharaoh's birthday, he made a feast for all his servants, and he lifted up the head of the chief cupbearer and the head of the chief baker among his servants. And he put the chief cupbearer back in his cupbearing, and he put the cup in Pharaoh's palm. But the chief baker he hanged, as Joseph had interpreted for them. And the chief cupbearer did not remember Joseph. He forgot him.
