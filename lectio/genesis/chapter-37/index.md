# Genesis, Chapter 37

The Dreamer and the Coat

And Jacob settled in the land where his father had lived as a stranger, in the land of Canaan. These are the generations of Jacob. Joseph, seventeen years old, was pasturing the flock with his brothers — he was a boy among the sons of Bilhah and the sons of Zilpah, his father's wives — and Joseph brought a bad report of them to their father. Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his sons, because he was the son of his old age, and he made him an ornamented coat. And his brothers saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers, and they hated him, and could not speak a peaceful word to him.

And Joseph dreamed a dream and told it to his brothers, and they hated him even more. And he said to them, “Listen to this dream I dreamed. There we were, binding sheaves in the middle of the field, and my sheaf rose and stood up straight, and your sheaves gathered around and bowed down to my sheaf.” And his brothers said to him, “Are you really going to be king over us? Are you really going to rule us?” And they hated him even more, for his dreams and for his words. And he dreamed another dream and told it to his brothers. He said, “Look, I dreamed another dream — and there were the sun and the moon and eleven stars, bowing down to me.” And he told it to his father and to his brothers, and his father rebuked him and said to him, “What is this dream you dreamed? Are we really going to come, I and your mother and your brothers, and bow to the ground before you?” And his brothers were jealous of him, but his father kept the thing in mind.

And his brothers went to pasture their father's flock at Shechem. And Israel said to Joseph, “Aren't your brothers pasturing at Shechem? Come, I will send you to them.” And he said to him, “Here I am.” And he said to him, “Go and see whether all is well with your brothers and well with the flock, and bring me back word.” So he sent him from the valley of Hebron, and he came to Shechem. And a man found him — there he was, wandering in the field — and the man asked him, “What are you looking for?” And he said, “I am looking for my brothers. Tell me, please, where they are pasturing.” And the man said, “They have moved on from here. I heard them say, ‘Let's go to Dothan.’” So Joseph went after his brothers and found them at Dothan.

And they saw him from a distance, and before he came near them they plotted against him, to put him to death. And they said to one another, “Here comes that master of dreams. Come on, let's kill him and throw him into one of the pits, and we will say a vicious animal ate him. Then we will see what becomes of his dreams.” And Reuben heard, and he saved him from their hands. He said, “Let's not take his life.” And Reuben said to them, “Shed no blood. Throw him into this pit here in the wilderness, but do not lay a hand on him” — so that he could save him from their hands and bring him back to his father. And when Joseph came to his brothers, they stripped Joseph of his coat, the ornamented coat he had on. And they took him and threw him into the pit. And the pit was empty; there was no water in it.

And they sat down to eat bread. And they looked up and saw — there was a caravan of Ishmaelites coming from Gilead, their camels carrying gum and balm and resin, on their way to take it down to Egypt. And Judah said to his brothers, “What do we gain if we kill our brother and cover up his blood? Come, let's sell him to the Ishmaelites, and let our hand not be on him, because he is our brother, our own flesh.” And his brothers listened. And Midianite men, traders, passed by, and they pulled Joseph up and lifted him out of the pit, and they sold Joseph to the Ishmaelites for twenty pieces of silver. And they brought Joseph to Egypt. And Reuben came back to the pit — and Joseph was not in the pit. And he tore his clothes. And he went back to his brothers and said, “The boy is gone! And I — where am I to go?”

And they took Joseph's coat, and slaughtered a goat, and dipped the coat in the blood. And they sent the ornamented coat and had it brought to their father, and they said, “We found this. Recognize it, please: is it your son's coat or not?” And he recognized it and said, “My son's coat! A vicious animal has eaten him. Joseph is torn, torn to pieces.” And Jacob tore his clothes and put sackcloth on his waist, and he mourned for his son many days. And all his sons and all his daughters rose to comfort him, but he refused to be comforted, and said, “No — I will go down to my son in Sheol, mourning.” And his father wept for him. And the Medanites sold him into Egypt, to Potiphar, a court official of Pharaoh, the captain of the guard.
