Genesis, Chapter 27

The Stolen Blessing

Chapter 2727 of 50

When Isaac was old and his eyes had grown too dim to see, he called Esau his older son and said to him, “My son.” And he said, “Here I am.” And he said, “Look — I am old. I don't know the day of my death. So now take your gear, your quiver and your bow, and go out to the open country and hunt me some game. And make me the tasty food I love, and bring it to me so I can eat — so that I can bless you before I die.” And Rebekah was listening while Isaac spoke to Esau his son. And Esau went out to the open country to hunt game and bring it back.

And Rebekah said to Jacob her son, “Listen — I heard your father speaking to Esau your brother. He said, ‘Bring me game and make me tasty food, so I can eat and bless you in front of the LORD before my death.’ So now, my son, listen to my voice, to what I am telling you. Go to the flock and get me two good young goats from there, and I will make them into tasty food for your father, the kind he loves. And you will bring it to your father, so he can eat — so that he can bless you before his death.” And Jacob said to Rebekah his mother, “But Esau my brother is a hairy man, and I am a smooth man. Suppose my father touches me. I will look like a fraud to him, and I will bring a curse on myself and not a blessing.” And his mother said to him, “Your curse be on me, my son. Just listen to my voice. Go, get them for me.”

So he went and got them and brought them to his mother, and his mother made tasty food, the kind his father loved. And Rebekah took the best clothes of Esau her older son, which she had in the house, and put them on Jacob her younger son. And she put the skins of the young goats on his hands and on the smooth part of his neck. And she put the tasty food and the bread she had made into the hand of Jacob her son.

And he came to his father and said, “My father.” And he said, “Here I am. Who are you, my son?” And Jacob said to his father, “I am Esau, your firstborn. I have done what you told me. Get up, please, sit and eat from my game, so that you can bless me.” And Isaac said to his son, “How did you find it so quickly, my son?” And he said, “Because the LORD your God brought it to me.” And Isaac said to Jacob, “Come closer, so I can touch you, my son — whether you really are my son Esau or not.” And Jacob came close to Isaac his father, and he touched him and said, “The voice is Jacob's voice, but the hands are Esau's hands.” And he did not recognize him, because his hands were hairy like the hands of Esau his brother. So he blessed him. And he said, “Are you really my son Esau?” And he said, “I am.”

And he said, “Bring it close to me, and let me eat from my son's game, so that I can bless you.” And he brought it close to him, and he ate; and he brought him wine, and he drank. And Isaac his father said to him, “Come close and kiss me, my son.” And he came close and kissed him. And he smelled the smell of his clothes, and he blessed him and said: “See — the smell of my son is like the smell of a field the LORD has blessed. May God give you the dew of the sky, and the richness of the earth, and plenty of grain and new wine. May peoples serve you, and nations bow down to you. Be master over your brothers, and let your mother's sons bow down to you. Cursed be everyone who curses you, and blessed be everyone who blesses you.”

And it happened, as soon as Isaac had finished blessing Jacob — Jacob had barely gone out from the presence of Isaac his father — that Esau his brother came in from his hunting. And he too made tasty food and brought it to his father, and he said to his father, “Let my father get up and eat from his son's game, so that you can bless me.” And Isaac his father said to him, “Who are you?” And he said, “I am your son, your firstborn, Esau.” And Isaac shook with a very great trembling and said, “Then who was it that hunted game and brought it to me, and I ate it all before you came, and I blessed him? And blessed he will stay.”

When Esau heard his father's words, he cried out a great and very bitter cry, and said to his father, “Bless me too, my father!” And he said, “Your brother came with deceit, and he took your blessing.” And he said, “Is he not rightly named Jacob? He has cheated me these two times: he took my birthright, and look — now he has taken my blessing.” And he said, “Haven't you kept back a blessing for me?” And Isaac answered Esau, “Look — I have made him master over you, and I have given him all his brothers as servants, and I have sustained him with grain and new wine. So what can I do for you, my son?” And Esau said to his father, “Do you have only one blessing, my father? Bless me too, my father!” And Esau lifted up his voice and wept.

And Isaac his father answered him: “Look — away from the richness of the earth your home will be, and away from the dew of the sky above. And by your sword you will live, and you will serve your brother. But when you break loose, you will tear his yoke off your neck.” And Esau held a grudge against Jacob because of the blessing his father had given him, and Esau said in his heart, “The days of mourning for my father are coming close. Then I will kill Jacob my brother.”

And Rebekah was told the words of Esau her older son, and she sent and called Jacob her younger son and said to him, “Listen — Esau your brother is consoling himself about you by planning to kill you. So now, my son, listen to my voice. Get up, run to Laban my brother, in Haran, and stay with him a while, until your brother's fury dies down — until your brother's anger turns away from you and he forgets what you did to him. Then I will send and bring you back from there. Why should I lose both of you in one day?” And Rebekah said to Isaac, “I am sick of my life because of these Hittite women. If Jacob takes a wife from the Hittite women, from the women of this land, what good is my life to me?”
