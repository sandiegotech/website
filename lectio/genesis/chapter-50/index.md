# Genesis, Chapter 50

God Meant It for Good

And Joseph fell on his father's face and wept over him and kissed him. And Joseph commanded his servants the physicians to embalm his father, and the physicians embalmed Israel. Forty days were taken for him, for that is how long the embalming takes; and the Egyptians wept for him seventy days. And when the days of weeping for him were past, Joseph spoke to Pharaoh's household: “If I have found favor in your eyes, please speak in Pharaoh's ears and say, ‘My father made me swear, saying: Look, I am about to die. In my grave that I dug for myself in the land of Canaan — there you are to bury me. So now let me go up, please, and bury my father, and I will come back.’” And Pharaoh said, “Go up and bury your father, as he made you swear.”

And Joseph went up to bury his father, and with him went up all Pharaoh's servants, the elders of his house and all the elders of the land of Egypt, and all Joseph's household, and his brothers, and his father's household. Only their little ones and their flocks and their herds they left in the land of Goshen. And chariots and horsemen went up with him too, and the camp was very large. And they came to the threshing floor of Atad, which is beyond the Jordan, and there they mourned with a very great and heavy mourning, and he kept seven days of mourning for his father. And the people of the land, the Canaanites, saw the mourning at the threshing floor of Atad and said, “This is a heavy mourning for Egypt.” That is why it was named Abel-mizraim, which is beyond the Jordan. And his sons did for him just as he had commanded them. His sons carried him to the land of Canaan and buried him in the cave of the field of Machpelah, the field facing Mamre that Abraham had bought as a burial holding from Ephron the Hittite. And Joseph went back to Egypt, he and his brothers and all who had gone up with him to bury his father, after he had buried his father.

And Joseph's brothers saw that their father was dead, and they said, “What if Joseph bears a grudge against us, and pays us back in full for all the evil we did him?” And they sent word to Joseph: “Your father gave this command before he died: ‘Say this to Joseph: Please, forgive the offense of your brothers and their sin, for they did you evil.’ So now, please forgive the offense of the servants of the God of your father.” And Joseph wept when they spoke to him. And his brothers also came and fell down before him and said, “Here we are, your slaves.” And Joseph said to them, “Do not be afraid. Am I in God's place? You planned evil against me; God planned it for good, to do what is being done today — to keep many people alive. So now, do not be afraid. I will provide for you and your little ones.” And he comforted them and spoke to their hearts.

And Joseph lived in Egypt, he and his father's household, and Joseph lived a hundred and ten years. And Joseph saw the third generation of Ephraim's children; the children of Machir son of Manasseh were also born on Joseph's knees. And Joseph said to his brothers, “I am about to die. But God will surely come to you and bring you up from this land to the land he swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob.” And Joseph made the sons of Israel swear: “God will surely come to you, and you are to carry my bones up from here.” And Joseph died, a hundred and ten years old. And they embalmed him, and he was laid in a coffin in Egypt.
