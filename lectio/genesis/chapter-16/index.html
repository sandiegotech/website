Genesis, Chapter 16

Hagar

Chapter 1616 of 50

Now Sarai, Abram's wife, had borne him no children. She had an Egyptian slave named Hagar. And Sarai said to Abram, “Look — the LORD has kept me from bearing children. Go in to my slave. Maybe I will be built up through her.” And Abram listened to Sarai's voice. So Sarai, Abram's wife, took Hagar her Egyptian slave — after Abram had been living ten years in the land of Canaan — and gave her to her husband Abram as a wife. And he went in to Hagar, and she conceived. And when she saw that she had conceived, her mistress became small in her eyes.

And Sarai said to Abram, “The wrong done to me is on you. I put my slave into your arms, and when she saw that she had conceived, I became small in her eyes. Let the LORD judge between me and you.” And Abram said to Sarai, “Look — your slave is in your hands. Do to her whatever seems right to you.” And Sarai treated her harshly, and she ran away from her.

And the messenger of the LORD found her by a spring of water in the wilderness, the spring on the road to Shur. And he said, “Hagar, slave of Sarai — where have you come from, and where are you going?” And she said, “I am running away from Sarai, my mistress.” And the messenger of the LORD said to her, “Go back to your mistress, and submit to her.”

And the messenger of the LORD said to her, “I will greatly multiply your offspring — too many to be counted.” And the messenger of the LORD said to her, “Look — you are pregnant, and you will give birth to a son. You will name him Ishmael, because the LORD has heard your suffering. He will be a wild donkey of a man: his hand against everyone, and everyone's hand against him, and he will live at odds with all his brothers.”

And she called the name of the LORD who had spoken to her: “You are El-roi — the God who sees me” — because she said, “Have I really seen here the one who sees me?” That is why the well was called Beer-lahai-roi; it is between Kadesh and Bered. And Hagar bore Abram a son, and Abram named his son, whom Hagar bore, Ishmael. And Abram was eighty-six years old when Hagar bore Ishmael to him.
