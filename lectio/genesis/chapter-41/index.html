Genesis, Chapter 41

Pharaoh's Dreams

Chapter 4141 of 50

And at the end of two full years Pharaoh dreamed — and there he was, standing by the Nile. And out of the Nile came up seven cows, fine-looking and fat, and they grazed among the reeds. And seven other cows came up after them out of the Nile, ugly and gaunt, and they stood beside the cows on the bank of the Nile. And the ugly, gaunt cows ate the seven fine-looking, fat cows. And Pharaoh woke up. And he slept and dreamed a second time — and there were seven ears of grain coming up on a single stalk, fat and good. And seven ears, thin and scorched by the east wind, sprouted after them. And the thin ears swallowed the seven fat, full ears. And Pharaoh woke up — and it was a dream. And in the morning his spirit was troubled, and he sent and called all the magicians of Egypt and all its wise men. And Pharaoh told them his dream, and there was no one who could interpret them for Pharaoh.

And the chief cupbearer spoke to Pharaoh: “Today I bring up my offenses. Pharaoh was furious with his servants, and he put me under guard in the house of the captain of the guard, me and the chief baker. And we dreamed a dream in a single night, he and I, each of us a dream with its own meaning. And there with us was a Hebrew boy, a slave of the captain of the guard. We told him, and he interpreted our dreams for us; he interpreted for each of us according to his dream. And just as he interpreted for us, so it was: me he put back in my post, and him he hanged.”

And Pharaoh sent and called Joseph, and they rushed him out of the pit. And he shaved and changed his clothes and came to Pharaoh. And Pharaoh said to Joseph, “I dreamed a dream, and there is no one to interpret it. And I have heard it said of you that you hear a dream and can interpret it.” And Joseph answered Pharaoh, “Not I. God will give an answer for Pharaoh's well-being.” And Pharaoh said to Joseph, “In my dream — there I was, standing on the bank of the Nile. And out of the Nile came up seven cows, fat and fine-looking, and they grazed among the reeds. And seven other cows came up after them, scrawny and very ugly and lean — I have never seen any as ugly in all the land of Egypt. And the lean, ugly cows ate the first seven cows, the fat ones. And they went into their bellies, and you could not tell that they had gone into their bellies: they looked as ugly as before. And I woke up. And I saw in my dream — there were seven ears of grain coming up on a single stalk, full and good. And seven ears, withered, thin, scorched by the east wind, sprouted after them. And the thin ears swallowed the seven good ears. And I told the magicians, and no one could explain it to me.”

And Joseph said to Pharaoh, “Pharaoh's dream is one. What God is about to do, he has told Pharaoh. The seven good cows are seven years, and the seven good ears are seven years. It is one dream. And the seven lean, ugly cows that came up after them are seven years, and so are the seven empty ears scorched by the east wind: they will be seven years of famine. It is as I said to Pharaoh: what God is about to do, he has shown Pharaoh. Look — seven years are coming of great plenty in all the land of Egypt. And after them seven years of famine will arise, and all the plenty in the land of Egypt will be forgotten, and the famine will consume the land. And the plenty will not be known in the land because of the famine that follows, for it will be very heavy. And as for the dream coming to Pharaoh twice — the thing is fixed by God, and God is hurrying to do it. So now let Pharaoh look for a man who is discerning and wise, and set him over the land of Egypt. Let Pharaoh act and appoint overseers over the land, and take a fifth from the land of Egypt in the seven years of plenty. Let them gather all the food of these good years that are coming, and pile up grain under Pharaoh's hand as food in the cities, and guard it. And the food will be a reserve for the land against the seven years of famine that will come on the land of Egypt, so the land is not cut off by the famine.”

And the thing was good in the eyes of Pharaoh and in the eyes of all his servants. And Pharaoh said to his servants, “Can we find a man like this, one in whom is the spirit of God?” And Pharaoh said to Joseph, “Since God has made all this known to you, there is no one as discerning and wise as you. You will be over my house, and by your word all my people will be led. Only by the throne will I be greater than you.” And Pharaoh said to Joseph, “See, I have set you over all the land of Egypt.” And Pharaoh took his signet ring off his hand and put it on Joseph's hand, and he dressed him in garments of fine linen and put the gold chain on his neck. And he had him ride in the chariot of his second-in-command, and they called out before him, “Abrek!” So he set him over all the land of Egypt. And Pharaoh said to Joseph, “I am Pharaoh; and without you no man will lift his hand or his foot in all the land of Egypt.” And Pharaoh named Joseph Zaphenath-paneah, and he gave him Asenath, daughter of Potiphera, priest of On, as a wife. And Joseph went out over the land of Egypt.

Joseph was thirty years old when he stood before Pharaoh king of Egypt. And Joseph went out from Pharaoh's presence and passed through all the land of Egypt. And in the seven years of plenty the land produced by handfuls. And he gathered all the food of the seven years that were in the land of Egypt, and he put food in the cities: the food of the fields around each city he put inside it. And Joseph piled up grain like the sand of the sea, a great deal of it, until he stopped counting, because it could not be counted. And before the year of famine came, two sons were born to Joseph, whom Asenath, daughter of Potiphera, priest of On, bore to him. And Joseph named the firstborn Manasseh — “because God has made me forget all my hardship, and all my father's house.” And the second he named Ephraim — “because God has made me fruitful in the land of my misery.”

And the seven years of plenty that were in the land of Egypt came to an end, and the seven years of famine began to come, as Joseph had said. And there was famine in all the lands, but in all the land of Egypt there was bread. And all the land of Egypt went hungry, and the people cried out to Pharaoh for bread. And Pharaoh said to all Egypt, “Go to Joseph. Whatever he tells you, do.” And the famine was over the whole face of the earth. And Joseph opened all that was in them and sold grain to Egypt, and the famine grew severe in the land of Egypt. And all the earth came to Egypt to buy grain, to Joseph, because the famine was severe over all the earth.
