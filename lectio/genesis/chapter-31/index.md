# Genesis, Chapter 31

The Flight, and the Heap of Witness

And he heard the words of Laban's sons: “Jacob has taken everything that was our father's, and out of what belonged to our father he has built all this wealth.” And Jacob looked at Laban's face — and it was not toward him as it had been before. And the LORD said to Jacob, “Go back to the land of your fathers, to your kindred, and I will be with you.” And Jacob sent and called Rachel and Leah out to the field, to his flock, and said to them, “I see your father's face, that it is not toward me as it was before. But the God of my father has been with me. And you know that I have worked for your father with all my strength, and your father has cheated me and changed my wages ten times over — but God did not let him do me harm. If he said, ‘The speckled will be your wages,’ then the whole flock bore speckled; and if he said, ‘The streaked will be your wages,’ then the whole flock bore streaked. So God has taken away your father's livestock and given it to me.

“And at the time the flock was breeding, I looked up and saw in a dream — and there were the males mounting the flock, streaked and speckled and mottled. And the messenger of God said to me in the dream, ‘Jacob.’ And I said, ‘Here I am.’ And he said, ‘Look up and see: all the males mounting the flock are streaked and speckled and mottled, because I have seen everything Laban is doing to you. I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed a standing stone, where you made a vow to me. Now get up, go out of this land, and go back to the land of your birth.’” And Rachel and Leah answered him, “Is there any share or inheritance left for us in our father's house? Doesn't he treat us as foreigners? He sold us, and he has been eating up the money paid for us. All the wealth God has taken from our father belongs to us and to our children. So now — whatever God has told you, do it.”

Then Jacob got up and put his children and his wives on the camels. And he drove off all his livestock and all the property he had gathered, the livestock he had come to own in Paddan-aram, to go to Isaac his father in the land of Canaan. Now Laban had gone to shear his sheep, and Rachel stole the household gods that belonged to her father. And Jacob stole the heart of Laban the Aramean by not telling him he was running. So he ran, he and everything he had. He got up and crossed the river and set his face toward the hill country of Gilead.

And on the third day Laban was told that Jacob had run. And he took his kinsmen with him and chased after him seven days' journey, and caught up with him in the hill country of Gilead. And God came to Laban the Aramean in a dream at night and said to him, “Be careful you say nothing to Jacob, good or bad.” And Laban caught up with Jacob. Now Jacob had pitched his tent in the hill country, and Laban pitched with his kinsmen in the hill country of Gilead. And Laban said to Jacob, “What have you done, stealing my heart and driving my daughters off like prisoners of war? Why did you run in secret and steal away from me, and not tell me? I would have sent you off with joy and songs, with tambourine and lyre. And you didn't even let me kiss my grandchildren and my daughters goodbye. You have done a stupid thing. I have it in my hand to do you harm — but the God of your father spoke to me last night and said, ‘Be careful you say nothing to Jacob, good or bad.’ And now you have gone off because you were homesick for your father's house — but why did you steal my gods?”

And Jacob answered Laban, “Because I was afraid. I thought you would tear your daughters away from me. Whoever you find your gods with will not live. In front of our kinsmen, identify what is yours among my things and take it.” Now Jacob did not know that Rachel had stolen them. And Laban went into Jacob's tent, and into Leah's tent, and into the tents of the two slave women, and he did not find them. And he came out of Leah's tent and went into Rachel's tent. Now Rachel had taken the household gods and put them in the camel's saddle cushion and was sitting on them. And Laban felt all around the tent and did not find them. And she said to her father, “Don't let my lord be angry that I cannot get up in front of you — the way of women is on me.” So he searched and did not find the household gods.

And Jacob was furious, and he argued it out with Laban. Jacob answered Laban, “What is my offense? What is my sin, that you have come after me in hot pursuit? You have felt through all my things — what have you found of all your household goods? Set it here, in front of my kinsmen and yours, and let them decide between the two of us. Twenty years I have been with you. Your ewes and your she-goats never miscarried, and I never ate the rams of your flock. What was torn by animals I never brought to you — I bore the loss myself. You demanded it from my hand, whether it was stolen by day or stolen by night. This is how I was: by day the heat ate me, and the cold by night, and sleep fled from my eyes. Twenty years I have been in your house. I worked for you fourteen years for your two daughters, and six years for your flock, and you changed my wages ten times over. If the God of my father, the God of Abraham and the Terror of Isaac, had not been with me, you would have sent me away empty-handed now. God has seen my misery and the labor of my hands, and last night he handed down his verdict.”

And Laban answered Jacob, “The daughters are my daughters, the children are my children, the flocks are my flocks — everything you see is mine. And what can I do today about these daughters of mine, or about the children they have borne? So now — come, let us cut a covenant, you and I, and let it be a witness between me and you.” And Jacob took a stone and set it up as a standing stone. And Jacob said to his kinsmen, “Gather stones.” And they took stones and made a heap, and they ate there by the heap. And Laban called it Jegar-sahadutha, and Jacob called it Galeed. And Laban said, “This heap is a witness between me and you today.” That is why it was named Galeed — and Mizpah, because he said, “May the LORD keep watch between me and you when we are out of each other's sight.

“If you mistreat my daughters, or if you take other wives besides my daughters — no one is with us, but see: God is witness between me and you.” And Laban said to Jacob, “Here is this heap, and here is the standing stone I have set between me and you. This heap is a witness and the standing stone is a witness: I will not go past this heap to you, and you will not go past this heap and this standing stone to me, to do harm. May the God of Abraham and the God of Nahor — the God of their father — judge between us.” And Jacob swore by the Terror of his father Isaac. And Jacob offered a sacrifice in the hill country and called his kinsmen to eat bread, and they ate bread and spent the night in the hill country. And Laban got up early in the morning and kissed his grandchildren and his daughters and blessed them, and Laban went off and returned to his place.
