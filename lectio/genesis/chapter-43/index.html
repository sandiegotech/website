Genesis, Chapter 43

Benjamin

Chapter 4343 of 50

And the famine was heavy in the land. And when they had finished eating the grain they had brought from Egypt, their father said to them, “Go back, buy us a little food.” And Judah said to him, “The man warned us solemnly: ‘You will not see my face unless your brother is with you.’ If you will send our brother with us, we will go down and buy food for you. But if you will not send him, we will not go down, because the man said to us, ‘You will not see my face unless your brother is with you.’” And Israel said, “Why did you do me this harm, telling the man you had another brother?” And they said, “The man kept asking about us and about our family: ‘Is your father still alive? Do you have a brother?’ And we told him what he asked. How could we know he would say, ‘Bring your brother down’?” And Judah said to Israel his father, “Send the boy with me, and let us get up and go, so that we live and do not die — we, and you, and our little ones. I myself will be his pledge. From my hand you may demand him. If I do not bring him back to you and set him in front of you, I will bear the blame before you all my days. If we had not lingered, we could have been there and back twice by now.”

And Israel their father said to them, “If it must be so, then do this: take some of the best the land yields in your bags, and carry a gift down to the man — a little balm and a little honey, gum and resin, pistachios and almonds. And take double the silver in your hand, and the silver that was put back in the mouths of your packs, take back in your hand. Maybe it was a mistake. And take your brother. Get up, go back to the man. And may El Shaddai give you compassion before the man, so that he sends back your other brother, and Benjamin. As for me — if I am bereaved, I am bereaved.”

And the men took this gift, and they took double the silver in their hand, and Benjamin, and they got up and went down to Egypt and stood before Joseph. And Joseph saw Benjamin with them, and he said to the one in charge of his house, “Bring the men into the house, and slaughter an animal and prepare it, because the men will eat with me at noon.” And the man did as Joseph said, and the man brought the men into Joseph's house. And the men were afraid at being brought into Joseph's house, and they said, “It is because of the silver that came back in our packs the first time that we are being brought in — to fall on us and overpower us and take us as slaves, with our donkeys.” And they came up to the man in charge of Joseph's house and spoke to him at the entrance of the house. They said, “Please, my lord — we came down the first time to buy food. And when we came to the lodging place and opened our packs, there was each man's silver in the mouth of his pack, our silver in full weight. We have brought it back in our hand. And we have brought down other silver in our hand to buy food. We do not know who put our silver in our packs.” And he said, “Peace to you. Do not be afraid. Your God, the God of your father, gave you treasure in your packs. Your silver came to me.” And he brought Simeon out to them.

And the man brought the men into Joseph's house and gave them water, and they washed their feet, and he gave fodder to their donkeys. And they laid out the gift for Joseph's coming at noon, because they had heard they were to eat bread there. And Joseph came home, and they brought him the gift that was in their hand, into the house, and they bowed to the ground before him. And he asked how they were, and said, “Is your father well, the old man you spoke of? Is he still alive?” And they said, “Your servant our father is well. He is still alive.” And they bowed their heads and bowed down. And he looked up and saw Benjamin his brother, his mother's son, and said, “Is this your youngest brother, the one you spoke of to me?” And he said, “God be gracious to you, my son.” And Joseph hurried out, because his compassion for his brother burned in him and he wanted to weep, and he went into an inner room and wept there. Then he washed his face and came out, and he held himself in and said, “Serve the bread.”

And they served him by himself, and them by themselves, and the Egyptians who ate with him by themselves — because Egyptians cannot eat bread with Hebrews; that is an abomination to Egyptians. And they were seated in front of him, the firstborn according to his birthright and the youngest according to his youth, and the men looked at one another in amazement. And portions were carried to them from in front of him, and Benjamin's portion was five times larger than any of theirs. And they drank, and drank deep with him.
